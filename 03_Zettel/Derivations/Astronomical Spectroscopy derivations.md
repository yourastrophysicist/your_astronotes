## 1. The Radiative Transfer Equation and Formal Integration

### 1.1 Microscopic Interactions and Differential Equation
Consider a pencil of radiation of monochromatic specific intensity $I_\nu(\vec{r}, \hat{n}, t)$ propagating through an astrophysical gas along path element $ds$.
Two fundamental processes alter the beam intensity:
1. **Absorption & Scattering Out of the Beam**:
   Characterized by the volume absorption coefficient $\alpha_\nu$ (units: $\text{cm}^{-1}$) or mass absorption coefficient $\kappa_\nu = \alpha_\nu / \rho$ ($\text{cm}^2/\text{g}$):
   $$dI_{\nu, abs} = -\alpha_\nu I_\nu ds = -\kappa_\nu \rho I_\nu ds$$
2. **Emission & Scattering Into the Beam**:
   Characterized by the monochromatic volume emissivity $j_\nu$ (units: $\text{erg s}^{-1} \text{cm}^{-3} \text{Hz}^{-1} \text{sr}^{-1}$):
   $$dI_{\nu, em} = j_\nu ds$$

Combining both processes yields the **Equation of Radiative Transfer**:
$$\frac{dI_\nu}{ds} = -\alpha_\nu I_\nu + j_\nu$$

### 1.2 Dimensionless Formulation, Optical Depth and Source Function
Define the differential optical depth along the propagation direction:
$$d\tau_\nu \equiv \alpha_\nu ds = \kappa_\nu \rho ds$$
$\tau_\nu$ represents the number of photon mean free paths across distance $s$.

Define the **Source Function** $S_\nu$:
$$S_\nu \equiv \frac{j_\nu}{\alpha_\nu} = \frac{j_\nu}{\kappa_\nu \rho}$$
Divide the transfer equation by $\alpha_\nu$:
$$\boxed{\frac{dI_\nu}{d\tau_\nu} = -I_\nu + S_\nu}$$

### 1.3 Exact Formal Solution via Integrating Factor
Multiply both sides by the integrating factor $e^{\tau_\nu}$:
$$e^{\tau_\nu} \frac{dI_\nu}{d\tau_\nu} + I_\nu e^{\tau_\nu} = S_\nu e^{\tau_\nu} \implies \frac{d}{d\tau_\nu}\left(I_\nu e^{\tau_\nu}\right) = S_\nu e^{\tau_\nu}$$

Integrate from the back of the medium ($\tau_\nu = 0$, where intensity is $I_\nu(0)$) to the front observer surface at total optical depth $\tau_\nu$:
$$\int_0^{\tau_\nu} \frac{d}{dt}\left(I_\nu(t) e^t\right) dt = \int_0^{\tau_\nu} S_\nu(t) e^t dt$$
$$I_\nu(\tau_\nu) e^{\tau_\nu} - I_\nu(0) = \int_0^{\tau_\nu} S_\nu(t) e^t dt$$

Multiply through by $e^{-\tau_\nu}$:
$$\boxed{I_\nu(\tau_\nu) = I_\nu(0) e^{-\tau_\nu} + \int_0^{\tau_\nu} S_\nu(t) e^{-(\tau_\nu - t)} dt}$$

*Physical interpretation*:
- $I_\nu(0) e^{-\tau_\nu}$: The background radiation incident at the rear, exponentially attenuated by the optical depth $\tau_\nu$.
- $\int_0^{\tau_\nu} S_\nu(t) e^{-(\tau_\nu - t)} dt$: The cumulative thermal/scattered emission generated by every layer $t$ inside the medium, attenuated only by the intervening material $(\tau_\nu - t)$ between layer $t$ and the observer.

### 1.4 Special Astrophysical Solutions
- **Isothermal Medium in Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)**:
  In LTE, Kirchhoff's law holds: $S_\nu = B_\nu(T)$, where $B_\nu(T)$ is the Planck function. If the temperature is uniform ($B_\nu(T) = \text{const}$):
  $$I_\nu(\tau_\nu) = I_\nu(0) e^{-\tau_\nu} + B_\nu(T) \int_0^{\tau_\nu} e^{-(\tau_\nu - t)} dt = I_\nu(0) e^{-\tau_\nu} + B_\nu(T) \left[ e^{-(\tau_\nu - t)} \right]_0^{\tau_\nu}$$
  $$\boxed{I_\nu(\tau_\nu) = I_\nu(0) e^{-\tau_\nu} + B_\nu(T) \left(1 - e^{-\tau_\nu}\right)}$$

- **Kirchhoff's laws of spectroscopy, the proof**:
  1. *Optically Thick Limit ($\tau_\nu \gg 1$)*:
     $$e^{-\tau_\nu} \to 0 \implies I_\nu \to B_\nu(T)$$
     An optically thick gas emits a continuous blackbody spectrum, completely erasing background radiation.
  2. *Optically Thin Gas with No Background ($I_\nu(0) = 0, \tau_\nu \ll 1$)*:
     $$1 - e^{-\tau_\nu} \approx \tau_\nu \implies I_\nu \approx B_\nu(T) \tau_\nu = j_\nu s$$
     The gas produces pure **emission lines** at wavelengths where opacity $\alpha_\nu$ is high!
  3. *Optically Thin Cloud in front of a Hot Background Source ($I_\nu(0) \gg B_\nu(T), \tau_\nu \ll 1$)*:
     $$I_\nu \approx I_\nu(0)(1 - \tau_\nu) + B_\nu(T) \tau_\nu = I_\nu(0) - [I_\nu(0) - B_\nu(T)] \tau_\nu$$
     If $I_\nu(0) > B_\nu(T)$ (cooler intervening cloud), flux drops at line center: **absorption lines**!

## 2. Line Broadening Physics and Profile Functions

The atomic absorption cross-section per atom is:
$$\sigma_\nu = \frac{\pi e^2}{m_e c} f_{lu} \phi(\nu)$$
where $f_{lu}$ is the dimensionless absorption oscillator strength, and $\phi(\nu)$ is the normalized line profile function ($\int_{-\infty}^\infty \phi(\nu) d\nu = 1$).

### 2.1 Natural (Radiation) Broadening, Lorentzian
By the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, an excited atomic state has finite radiative lifetime $\tau_{rad} = 1/\sum_k A_{uk}$.
The transition probability leads to a damped harmonic oscillator with damping constant $\gamma_{nat} = \sum_k A_{uk}$.
Fourier transforming the exponentially decaying wave amplitude $e^{-\gamma t/2} e^{i\omega_0 t}$ yields the **Lorentzian profile**:
$$\boxed{\phi_L(\nu) = \frac{\gamma / 4\pi^2}{(\nu - \nu_0)^2 + (\gamma / 4\pi)^2}}$$
- Full Width at Half Maximum: $\text{FWHM}_L = \frac{\gamma}{2\pi}$.
- Wing behavior: For $|\nu - \nu_0| \gg \gamma$, $\phi_L(\nu) \propto \frac{1}{(\nu - \nu_0)^2}$ (slow power-law drop-off).

### 2.2 Thermal Doppler Broadening, Gaussian
Atoms in a gas at kinetic temperature $T$ move with a 1D Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution along the line of sight ($v_z$):
$$P(v_z) dv_z = \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi k_B T}} \exp\left(-\frac{m v_z^2}{2 k_B T}\right) dv_z$$
Each moving atom sees the radiation frequency Doppler-shifted:
$$\nu = \nu_0 \left(1 + \frac{v_z}{c}\right) \implies v_z = c \frac{\nu - \nu_0}{\nu_0}$$
Substituting $v_z$ into $P(v_z)$:
$$\phi_D(\nu) d\nu = P(v_z) \frac{dv_z}{d\nu} d\nu = \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi k_B T}} \frac{c}{\nu_0} \exp\left[ -\frac{m c^2 (\nu - \nu_0)^2}{2 k_B T \nu_0^2} \right] d\nu$$

Define the **Doppler width** $\Delta\nu_D$ (including microturbulent velocity $\xi_{turb}$):
$$\boxed{\Delta\nu_D \equiv \frac{\nu_0}{c} \sqrt{\frac{2 k_B T}{m} + \xi_{turb}^2}}$$
The thermal Doppler profile is a **pure Gaussian**:
$$\boxed{\phi_D(\nu) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi} \Delta\nu_D} \exp\left[ -\left(\frac{\nu - \nu_0}{\Delta\nu_D}\right)^2 \right]}$$
- Peak amplitude: $\phi_D(\nu_0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\Delta\nu_D}$.
- Full Width at Half Maximum: $\text{FWHM}_D = 2\sqrt{\ln 2} \Delta\nu_D \approx 1.665 \Delta\nu_D$.
- Wing behavior: For $|\nu - \nu_0| \gg \Delta\nu_D$, $\phi_D(\nu) \propto e^{-u^2}$, decaying exponentially fast.

### 2.3 The Voigt Profile (Convolution)
In a real stellar atmosphere, both thermal Doppler motion and radiative/collisional damping operate simultaneously.
The resulting profile is the convolution of Gaussian Doppler broadening with the Lorentzian damping distribution:
$$\phi_V(\nu) = (\phi_D * \phi_L)(\nu) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \phi_D(\nu') \phi_L(\nu - \nu') d\nu'$$

In terms of dimensionless variables:
$$u \equiv \frac{\nu - \nu_0}{\Delta\nu_D}, \quad a \equiv \frac{\gamma}{4\pi \Delta\nu_D}$$
The profile is expressed via the **Hjerting function** $H(a, u)$:
$$\boxed{\phi_V(\nu) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi} \Delta\nu_D} H(a, u), \quad H(a, u) \equiv \frac{a}{\pi} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{e^{-y^2}}{(u - y)^2 + a^2} dy}$$

*Interpretation of the Voigt profile*:
- In typical stellar atmospheres, $a \approx 10^{-3} - 10^{-1} \ll 1$.
- **Line Core ($|u| \lesssim 3$)**: Gaussian dominates. Thermal motion determines the core width ($\sim \Delta\nu_D$).
- **Line Wings ($|u| \gtrsim 3-5$)**: The Gaussian $e^{-u^2}$ drops to zero, while the Lorentzian wings $\frac{a}{\pi u^2}$ decay slowly as $1/u^2$. Therefore, the damping wings of strong absorption lines are completely governed by the Lorentzian component!

## 3. Equivalent Width and the Curve of Growth Derivation

### 3.1 Mathematical Definition of Equivalent Width
The **Equivalent Width** $W_\lambda$ of an absorption line is the width of a completely black rectangular box (zero transmission) that removes the same total flux as the actual line:
$$\boxed{W_\lambda \equiv \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left(1 - \frac{I_\lambda}{I_c}\right) d\lambda = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left(1 - e^{-\tau_\lambda}\right) d\lambda \quad [\text{Å or cm}]}$$
Similarly, in frequency units:
$$W_\nu \equiv \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left(1 - e^{-\tau_\nu}\right) d\nu = \frac{c}{\lambda_0^2} W_\lambda$$

The monochromatic optical depth for column density $N_l$ (absorbing atoms in lower level per $\text{cm}^2$) is:
$$\tau_\nu = N_l \sigma_\nu = N_l \frac{\pi e^2}{m_e c} f_{lu} \phi(\nu) \equiv \tau_0 \frac{\phi(\nu)}{\phi(\nu_0)}$$
where the optical depth at line center is:
$$\tau_0 = N_l \frac{\pi e^2}{m_e c} f_{lu} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\Delta\nu_D} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi} e^2}{m_e c} \frac{N_l f_{lu}}{\Delta\nu_D}$$

### 3.2 The Three Regimes of the Curve of Growth
The **Curve of Growth** plots $\log_{10}(W_\lambda / \lambda_0)$ versus $\log_{10}(N_l f_{lu} \lambda_0)$.

#### Regime 1, The Linear Regime (Optically Thin, $\tau_0 \ll 1$)
When the line is optically thin, the optical depth is small everywhere: $\tau_\nu \ll 1$.
Taylor expanding $1 - e^{-\tau_\nu} \approx \tau_\nu$:
$$W_\nu = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left(1 - e^{-\tau_\nu}\right) d\nu \approx \int_{-\infty}^\infty \tau_\nu d\nu = \int_{-\infty}^\infty N_l \frac{\pi e^2}{m_e c} f_{lu} \phi(\nu) d\nu$$
Since $\phi(\nu)$ is normalized ($\int \phi d\nu = 1$):
$$W_\nu = \frac{\pi e^2}{m_e c} N_l f_{lu}$$
Converting to wavelength units ($W_\lambda = \frac{\lambda_0^2}{c} W_\nu$):
$$\boxed{W_\lambda = \frac{\pi e^2}{m_e c^2} \lambda_0^2 N_l f_{lu} \propto N_l}$$
- **Log-Log Slope**: $\frac{d\log W_\lambda}{d\log N_l} = 1.0$.
- **Astrophysical Power**: In this regime, the equivalent width is strictly proportional to column density $N_l$ and completely independent of temperature $T$ or velocity dispersion $\Delta\nu_D$. This is the ideal regime for measuring chemical abundances.

#### Regime 2, The Saturated (Flat) Regime (Intermediate, $1 \lesssim \tau_0 \lesssim 10^3$)
As column density increases, the line center becomes optically thick ($\tau_0 > 1$). At the core, $1 - e^{-\tau_\nu} \to 1$ (the line is completely black at center, "saturated").
However, the optical depth in the wings is still $< 1$.
The line profile is dominated by the Gaussian Doppler core: $\tau(u) = \tau_0 e^{-u^2}$.
Setting $\tau(u_c) = 1$ defines the saturation half-width $u_c$:
$$\tau_0 e^{-u_c^2} = 1 \implies u_c = \sqrt{\ln \tau_0}$$
Inside $|u| < u_c$, $1 - e^{-\tau(u)} \approx 1$. Outside, it drops rapidly to zero.
Approximating the integral:
$$W_\nu = \int_{-\infty}^\infty (1 - e^{-\tau_0 e^{-u^2}}) \Delta\nu_D du \approx 2 \Delta\nu_D u_c = 2 \Delta\nu_D \sqrt{\ln \tau_0}$$
Converting to wavelength:
$$\boxed{W_\lambda \approx \frac{2 \lambda_0}{c} \Delta\nu_D \sqrt{\ln \tau_0} = 2 \Delta\lambda_D \sqrt{\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi} e^2}{m_e c} \frac{N_l f \lambda_0}{\Delta v}\right)} \propto \Delta v \sqrt{\ln N_l}}$$
- **Log-Log Slope**: Almost zero ($\frac{d\log W}{d\log N} \sim \frac{1}{2\ln\tau_0} \to 0$).
- **Astrophysical Power**: Increasing column density $N$ by a factor of 100 barely increases $W_\lambda$! However, $W_\lambda$ is directly proportional to $\Delta\lambda_D = \frac{\lambda_0}{c} \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m} + \xi^2}$. Thus, the flat plateau is used to determine **microturbulent velocity** $\xi_{turb}$ and gas temperature.

#### Regime 3, The Damping (Square-Root) Regime (Strong, $\tau_0 \gg 10^4$)
At extreme column densities, the Gaussian core is completely overwhelmed and the Lorentzian damping wings become optically thick ($\tau_\nu > 1$).
In the wings ($|u| \gg 1$), the Hjerting function is $H(a, u) \approx \frac{a}{\pi u^2}$.
The optical depth is:
$$\tau(u) = \tau_0 \frac{a}{\pi u^2}$$
The integral for equivalent width:
$$W_\nu = \Delta\nu_D \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left[ 1 - \exp\left(-\frac{\tau_0 a}{\pi u^2}\right) \right] du$$
Let $x^2 \equiv \frac{\tau_0 a}{\pi u^2} \implies u = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_0 a}{\pi}} \frac{1}{x}, \quad du = -\sqrt{\frac{\tau_0 a}{\pi}} \frac{dx}{x^2}$.
$$W_\nu = 2 \Delta\nu_D \sqrt{\frac{\tau_0 a}{\pi}} \int_0^\infty \frac{1 - e^{-x^2}}{x^2} dx$$
Using the standard definite integral $\int_0^\infty \frac{1 - e^{-x^2}}{x^2} dx = \sqrt{\pi}$:
$$W_\nu = 2 \Delta\nu_D \sqrt{\frac{\tau_0 a}{\pi}} \sqrt{\pi} = 2 \Delta\nu_D \sqrt{\tau_0 a}$$

Substitute $\tau_0 = \frac{\sqrt{\pi} e^2}{m_e c} \frac{N_l f}{\Delta\nu_D}$ and $a = \frac{\gamma}{4\pi \Delta\nu_D}$:
$$W_\nu = 2 \Delta\nu_D \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi} e^2}{m_e c} \frac{N_l f}{\Delta\nu_D}\right) \left(\frac{\gamma}{4\pi \Delta\nu_D}\right)} = \sqrt{\frac{e^2}{m_e c} \gamma N_l f}$$
Converting to wavelength units:
$$\boxed{W_\lambda = \frac{\lambda_0^2}{c} \sqrt{\frac{e^2}{m_e c} \gamma N_l f} \propto \sqrt{N_l}}$$
- **Log-Log Slope**: $\frac{d\log W_\lambda}{d\log N_l} = 0.5$ (Square-root law).
- **Astrophysical Applications**:
  - Damped Lyman-$\alpha$ systems (DLAs) in quasar absorption spectra ($N_{HI} > 2 \times 10^{20}\,\text{cm}^{-2}$).
  - Solar Fraunhofer lines (e.g. Ca II H & K, Na I D) where Collisional / Van der Waals damping $\gamma \propto P_{gas}$ dominates, allowing astronomers to measure stellar surface gravity $\log g$!
