{% raw %}
the **Kormendy relation** (1977) is a 2D scaling relation among elliptical galaxies: surface brightness $\langle\mu\rangle$ correlates with effective radius $R_e$.

## the relation

$$\boxed{\, \langle\mu\rangle_e = a\log_{10}(R_e) + b \,}$$

with $\langle\mu\rangle_e$ = mean surface brightness within $R_e$ (in mag/arcsec$^2$). slope $a \approx 3$ (in the B-band), constant $b$ depends on band.

equivalently:
$$L \propto R_e^{0.6}$$

so brighter ellipticals are larger, but **less concentrated** (lower mean surface brightness).

## the physical interpretation

surface brightness is **proportional to mass density times $M/L$**. so a steep Kormendy relation means:
- larger ellipticals have **lower density** (extended haloes).
- ellipticals can't all be self-similar; structural relations exist.

## the 3D extension: Fundamental Plane

Kormendy is one **edge-on projection** of the **fundamental plane**:
$$R_e \propto \sigma^{1.4}\,\langle I\rangle^{-0.9}$$

projecting onto $\langle I\rangle - R_e$ gives Kormendy. Fundamental Plane has tighter scatter because it includes velocity dispersion as the third axis.

see [Fundamental plane of ellipticals](./Fundamental%20plane%20of%20ellipticals.html).

## the use

Kormendy relation:
- distinguishes **classical bulges** (follow Kormendy) from **pseudo-bulges** (don't, because they're disk-formed).
- separates **cluster ellipticals** from **field ellipticals** (slight differences).
- traces ellipticals back to their progenitors (high-$z$ red ellipticals follow shifted Kormendy).

## modern values

Kormendy slope in V-band is approximately:
$$\langle\mu\rangle_V = 3.0 \log R_e + 18\,\text{mag/arcsec}^2$$

(approximate, varies with sample selection). low surface brightness ellipticals + dwarfs sometimes break this relation, suggesting different formation mechanisms.
