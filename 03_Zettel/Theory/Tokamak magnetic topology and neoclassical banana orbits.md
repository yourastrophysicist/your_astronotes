in a tokamak, magnetic confinement is achieved by combining a strong toroidal field $B_\phi$ with a poloidal field $B_\theta$ generated by a toroidal plasma current, forming nested helical magnetic flux surfaces.

## magnetic topology and field variation

due to the toroidal geometry, the magnetic field strength varies inversely with major radius $R = R_0 + r \cos\theta$:

$$B(r, \theta) \approx B_0 \left( 1 - \epsilon \cos\theta \right)$$

where $\epsilon = r / R_0 \ll 1$ is the inverse aspect ratio, and $\theta$ is the poloidal angle ($\theta = 0$ at the outboard low-field side, $\theta = \pi$ at the inboard high-field side).

## trapped versus passing particles

because the magnetic field is stronger on the inboard side, the tokamak functions as a toroidal magnetic mirror:
1. **passing particles**: particles with large parallel velocity $\lvert v_\parallel\rvert / v > \sqrt{2\epsilon}$ circulate continuously around the torus.
2. **trapped particles**: particles with $\lvert v_\parallel\rvert / v \le \sqrt{2\epsilon}$ are mirrored before reaching the inboard side, bouncing back and forth on the outboard side.
   the trapped fraction of particles is:
   $$f_{\text{tr}} \approx \sqrt{2\epsilon} = \sqrt{\frac{2r}{R_0}}$$

## neoclassical banana orbits

the combination of vertical magnetic drift $\mathbf{v}_D$ and parallel bounce motion causes the projected trajectory of a trapped particle in the poloidal plane to trace a closed banana-shaped orbit.

integrating drift displacements yields the **banana width**:

$$\Delta r_b \approx \frac{q_s}{\sqrt{\epsilon}} \rho_L \gg \rho_L$$

where $q_s = \frac{r B_\phi}{R_0 B_\theta}$ is the safety factor.
because trapped particles step by the large banana width $\Delta r_b$ during collisions rather than the gyroradius $\rho_L$, cross-field neoclassical transport exceeds classical transport by factor $q_s^2 / \epsilon^{3/2}$.
friction between passing and trapped particles drives the **bootstrap current** ($j_{\text{boot}} \propto -\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{B_\theta} \frac{dp}{dr}$), enabling steady-state tokamak operation.
