in 1926 and 1936, **Edwin Hubble** devised the first systematic morphological classification scheme for external galaxies. commonly known as the **Hubble Tuning Fork Diagram** (il diagramma a diapason di Hubble), it remains the standard morphological taxonomy of extragalactic astronomy.

![galaxies-12.png](../../assets/images/galaxies-12.png)

---

## structure of the Hubble Tuning Fork

the classification arranges galaxies along a bifurcated sequence:

![galaxies-13.png](../../assets/images/galaxies-13.png)

### 1. the Elliptical Sequence (E0 to E7) — the "Handle":
elliptical galaxies are classified from **E0** (circular in projection) to **E7** (most elongated/flattened), defined by the apparent axis ratio of major axis $a$ and minor axis $b$:

$$\boxed{\, n = 10 \left(1 - \frac{b}{a}\right) \,}$$

- **E0**: $b/a = 1.0 \implies n = 0$ (spherical appearance).
- **E4**: $b/a = 0.6 \implies n = 4$.
- **E7**: $b/a = 0.3 \implies n = 7$ (maximum observed flattening; systems flatter than E7 are classified as disk systems).

*(crucial observational caveat: this is an **apparent** projected ellipticity on the plane of the sky. an intrinsically oblate or prolate E7 galaxy viewed face-on along its symmetry axis appears as an E0 galaxy!).*

---

### 2. the Bifurcated Spiral Branches — the "Prongs":
at the junction sits the **S0 (lenticular)** class. the sequence then splits into two parallel branches of spiral galaxies:
1. **Normal Spirals (S)**: Sa $\to$ Sb $\to$ Sc.
2. **Barred Spirals (SB)**: SBa $\to$ SBb $\to$ SBc.

![galaxies-14.png](../../assets/images/galaxies-14.png)

the progression from **a** to **c** along both branches is defined by three coordinated physical trends:
1. **Bulge-to-disk luminosity ratio ($B/T$)**:
   - Sa/SBa: dominant, luminous central bulge ($B/T \sim 0.3 - 0.5$).
   - Sc/SBc: tiny, inconspicuous bulge ($B/T < 0.1$).
2. **Tightness of spiral arms**:
   - Sa/SBa: spiral arms are smooth, continuous, and tightly wound around the nucleus.
   - Sc/SBc: spiral arms are open, loosely wound, and broadly splayed.
3. **Clumpiness / resolution of arms**:
   - Sa/SBa: arms are smooth, containing few discrete HII regions or bright stellar associations.
   - Sc/SBc: arms are highly structured, knotty, and resolved into brilliant chains of blue O/B star clusters and glowing HII emission nebulae.

*(later extended by Gerard de Vaucouleurs to include Sd and Sm intermediate spiral types).*

![galaxies-15.png](../../assets/images/galaxies-15.png)

---

## 3. Lenticulars (S0 / SB0) and Irregulars (Irr)

- **S0 / SB0 (Lenticular galaxies)**: sit at the critical pivot point where the handle meets the prongs. they possess a disk and bulge like spirals, but show no trace of spiral arms or ongoing star formation.
- **Irr (Irregular galaxies)**: placed on the far right of the diagram beyond Sc/SBc; completely lack rotational or reflectional symmetry (divided into Irr I with active starbursts and Irr II with disturbed tidal morphology).

![galaxies-16.png](../../assets/images/galaxies-16.png)

---

## historical vs modern terminology: "Early-Type" vs "Late-Type"

Hubble originally hypothesized that galaxies evolved along the sequence from left to right: starting as smooth ellipticals ("early-type") and developing into intricate spirals ("late-type"). 

**we now know this evolutionary direction is physically incorrect**:
- galaxies do not evolve from E to S!
- in hierarchical $\Lambda$CDM structure formation, small disk galaxies form first; major galaxy mergers between spirals destroy disks and violently randomize stellar orbits, forming massive **elliptical galaxies**!

nevertheless, astronomers universally retain Hubble's terminology as convenient shorthand:
- **Early-Type Galaxies (ETGs)**: Ellipticals and S0 galaxies.
- **Late-Type Galaxies (LTGs)**: Spirals (Sa-Sc, SBa-SBc) and Irregulars.
