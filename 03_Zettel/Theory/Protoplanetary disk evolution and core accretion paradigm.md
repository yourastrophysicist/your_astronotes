planets form within circumstellar protoplanetary disks of gas and dust orbiting young pre-main-sequence stars (T Tauri and Herbig Ae/Be stars) on lifetimes of $\tau_{\text{disk}} \sim 1 - 10\text{ Myr}$.

## dust dynamics and the meter-size drift barrier

sub-micron dust grains collide and coagulate into millimeter-sized pebbles. however, gas in the disk is supported by a radial pressure gradient ($\partial p / \partial r < 0$), causing it to orbit at slightly sub-Keplerian speed:

$$v_{\phi, \text{gas}} = v_{\text{K}} (1 - \eta_{\text{drag}}), \quad \eta_{\text{drag}} = -\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{c_s}{v_{\text{K}}}\right)^2 \frac{d\ln p}{d\ln r} \sim 10^{-3}$$

solid pebbles orbit at full Keplerian velocity $v_{\text{K}}$, experiencing a headwind from the slower gas.
the drag force removes angular momentum, causing particles with Stokes number $\text{St} = \Omega_{\text{K}} t_{\text{stop}} \sim 1$ (meter-sized boulders at $1\text{ AU}$, millimeter-sized pebbles in outer disk) to spiral inward onto the central star on catastrophic timescales of only $\sim 100\text{ years}$.

## overcoming the barrier: the streaming instability

Youdin and Goodman (2005) discovered the **streaming instability**: as dust clumps migrate inward, their drag exerts an equal and opposite back-reaction on the gas, slowing the local headwind and concentrating pebbles into dense filaments.
when local dust density exceeds Roche density ($\rho_d \gtrsim \rho_R \sim M_\star / r^3$), clumps undergo spontaneous gravitational collapse, directly forming $10 - 100\text{ km}$ **planetesimals** without encountering the drift barrier.

## the core accretion three-phase paradigm

Perri & Cameron (1974), Mizuno (1980), Pollack et al. (1996):
1. **Phase I: core assembly**: planetesimals collide and merge runaway/oligarchically until reaching isolation mass ($M_{\text{core}} \sim 1 - 10 M_\oplus$).
2. **Phase II: quasi-static envelope buildup**: a thin gas envelope forms around the core in hydrostatic equilibrium. accretion is limited by radiative cooling ($L_{\text{KH}} \sim G M_{\text{core}} \dot{M} / R$).
3. **Phase III: runaway gas accretion**: once envelope mass equals core mass ($M_{\text{env}} \sim M_{\text{core}} \approx 10 M_\oplus$, the **critical core mass**), hydrostatic equilibrium breaks down. the core undergoes rapid dynamical gas collapse, accreting gas at the disk supply limit until opening a gap: forming a gas giant.
