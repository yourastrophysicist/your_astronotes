title: "UFDG star formation histories"
---
ultra-faint dwarf galaxies are **fossils of the early universe**. their colour-magnitude diagrams show that the bulk of their stars formed in a single ancient episode and that star formation **shut down well before $z \sim 1$**, in many cases before $z \sim 6$, i.e. before the universe was a billion years old.

**the canonical SFH**:

- a single short burst (or rapid sequence of bursts) lasting $\lesssim 1$ Gyr, completed by $z \gtrsim 6$.
- truncation by **cosmic reionisation**: the UV background heating around $z \sim 6$ photoevaporated gas from the shallow potential wells of UFDG-mass halos, choking off subsequent star formation.
- no measurable younger population in the cleanest UFDGs (e.g. bootes i, ursa major i, hercules, coma ber). their main-sequence turnoffs are all consistent with $\gtrsim 12$-$13$ Gyr.

**how this is measured**: deep HST imaging reaches the main-sequence turnoff in nearby UFDGs ($D \lesssim 100$ kpc). isochrone fitting gives ages with $\sim 1$ Gyr precision. the absence of a younger turnoff or a blue main sequence sets a hard upper limit on any recent star formation. for the closest UFDGs, brown et al. (2012, 2014) showed using HST/ACS that bootes i, hercules, leo iv, ursa major i, coma ber, and CVn ii have **CMDs indistinguishable from each other and from M92**, an old metal-poor globular cluster, and that all of them must have completed star formation before $z \approx 6$.

**reionisation as the regulator**:

- below a halo mass of $\sim 10^9 M_\odot$, the UV photoionising background can heat baryons above the virial temperature and prevent further accretion of cold gas. this is the **filtering mass** picture (gnedin 2000, bullock et al. 2000).
- UFDG halo masses are around $10^8$-$10^9 M_\odot$ (extrapolated from current $M(<r_{1/2})$ to virial mass), straddling this threshold. so reionisation cleanly shuts them off.
- the few UFDGs that show extended SF (eridanus ii, leo t at the boundary) sit in slightly more massive halos that retained some gas.

**chemical signatures of a quick burst**:

- low mean $[\text{Fe/H}]$ ($\sim -2.5$) because few generations of supernovae enriched the gas before it was lost.
- broad $[\text{Fe/H}]$ spread despite low mean (often $\sim 0.5$-$1$ dex) because each individual SN dominated local enrichment in such small systems (stochastic nucleosynthesis).
- presence of **CEMP-no stars** (carbon-enhanced metal-poor without s-process or r-process enrichment), which are inherited from pop iii progenitors (see [Pop III remnants in UFDGs](Pop%20III%20remnants%20in%20UFDGs.html)).
- enhanced $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$, characteristic of pure core-collapse SN enrichment with no time for type ia.
- in some cases (reticulum ii) a strong r-process enhancement attributed to a single early neutron-star merger.

**comparison with classical dSphs and LMC/SMC**:

- classical dwarf spheroidals (draco, ursa minor, sculptor, fornax, leo i/ii) have more massive halos and show extended star formation lasting up to $\sim 5$-$10$ Gyr. fornax in particular has stars as young as $\sim 1$ Gyr.
- LMC and SMC are still forming stars today.
- UFDGs are at the fossil extreme: **single old burst, no young population**, except in the borderline objects.

**why this matters**:

- UFDGs are direct probes of **reionisation feedback**. their old-only population is direct evidence that reionisation can quench dwarfs.
- their stellar populations are the closest local analogues of the high-redshift dwarfs that JWST is now beginning to find.
- they are the building blocks that contributed to the very oldest, most metal-poor part of the milky way halo (see [Halo accretion from dwarf galaxies](Halo%20accretion%20from%20dwarf%20galaxies.html)).
