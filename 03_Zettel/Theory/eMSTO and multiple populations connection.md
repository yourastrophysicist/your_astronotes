{% raw %}
one of the deepest open questions of the course: are **young eMSTO clusters** ($\sim 1$-$2$ Gyr LMC + SMC clusters with broadened MS turn-offs from stellar rotation) and **old GCs with multiple populations** ($> 10$ Gyr clusters with Na-O anti-correlations + He spreads + chromosome maps) the SAME phenomenon, observed at different stages of cluster evolution? or are they fundamentally different?

this question matters because the answer determines whether [MPs in old GCs](./Multiple%20populations%20in%20GCs%20discovery.html) are a primordial phenomenon set at cluster formation, or whether they emerge from continuous secular evolution of the stellar population.

## the parallel observation

| feature | eMSTO cluster ($\sim 1$ Gyr) | old GC ($> 10$ Gyr) |
|---|---|---|
| broad MS turn-off | yes | no |
| split upper MS | yes (e.g., NGC 1755) | no |
| chemical anomalies | undetected so far | strong (Na-O, CN, helium spread) |
| chromosome map | not applicable | well-resolved (Milone+ 2017) |
| stellar mass at TO | $\sim 1.5$-$2.5\,M_\odot$ | $\sim 0.85\,M_\odot$ |

the differences in observable signatures might reflect just the **stellar mass at the TO** at different ages, not different physics.

## the hypothesis: rotation $\to$ He $\to$ chromosome map

D'Antona et al. 2015 sketched the unified scenario:

1. at cluster age $0$: a population of stars forms with a broad rotation distribution ($v/v_{\rm crit} = 0$ to $0.9$).
2. at $\sim 100$ Myr to $1$ Gyr: rotational mixing transports H + He from the envelope to the core; surface He abundance rises in fast rotators by $\Delta Y \sim 0.02$-$0.05$.
3. at $\sim 1$ Gyr: the upper MS bifurcates into slow + fast rotator branches; eMSTO is observed.
4. at $\sim 2$-$5$ Gyr: stars at the MS turn-off are $M \sim 1.4\,M_\odot$ with thin convective envelopes; magnetic braking begins to slow them.
5. at $> 5$ Gyr: rotation has been braked to $v < 50$ km/s; eMSTO disappears; but the **He surface enrichment** persists.
6. at $\sim 12$ Gyr (old GC): MS turn-off is at $M \sim 0.85\,M_\odot$; stars are slow + magnetically braked; the He variations imprinted earlier are now visible as the chromosome map He spread (Milone et al. 2017).

if this picture is correct, eMSTO + chromosome maps are **the same star + same physics** observed at different stages.

## evidence consistent with this picture

- both eMSTO + chromosome maps require differential evolution within an SSP (rotation distribution / He spread);
- Milone's chromosome map separates 1G + 2G stars partly via He;
- young clusters show no spectroscopic chemical anomalies yet, consistent with rotation effects being NEEDED before He enrichment becomes detectable;
- the cluster mass dependence is similar: more massive clusters show stronger eMSTOs + stronger MP signatures.

## evidence against (or complicating)

- **light element variations** (Na-O, CN, MgAl) are NOT explained by simple rotation. these require external pollution from AGB or fast rotating massive stars (FRMS).
- **iron variations** in Type II GCs (NGC 1851, M22, $\omega$ Cen) require core-collapse SNe pollution, which rotation cannot deliver.
- some old GCs ($\sim 5$ Gyr) show MPs but no eMSTO signatures even at intermediate ages.

so rotation alone cannot explain the FULL chemistry of old GC MPs. the polluter scenarios (AGB, FRMS, supermassive stars, interacting binaries) must operate alongside rotation.

## the most plausible synthesis

**rotation + pollution operate together at cluster formation**:

- a 1st generation forms with a broad rotation distribution;
- some 1G stars (esp. fast rotating massive stars + AGBs) eject He-rich + Na-rich + O-poor material;
- this material pollutes a 2nd generation forming from 1G ejecta + retained primordial gas;
- at $\sim 1$ Gyr, the residual rotation differences in the SSP are visible as eMSTO + split MS;
- at $\sim 12$ Gyr, the rotation is braked but the chemical signatures (He, Na, O) persist as the chromosome map.

this hybrid picture is the current consensus, though many details remain open.
