since its 2022 first light, the **James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)** has opened the high-redshift frontier for [[Population III stars|Pop III star]] research. for the first time, the era when the very first stars formed ($z \sim 10$-$20$, $\sim 200$-$500$ Myr after the Big Bang) is directly observationally accessible.

## what JWST adds

the key JWST capabilities for Pop III science:

- **NIRSpec** (0.6-5.3 $\mu$m): IR spectroscopy of $z > 10$ galaxies, redshift confirmation, line diagnostics.
- **NIRCam** (0.6-5.0 $\mu$m): ultra-deep imaging at IR wavelengths where Lyman-$\alpha$, He II 1640, [O III] 5007 + Balmer break shift to.
- **MIRI** (5-28 $\mu$m): rest-frame UV at $z > 10$ shifts here.
- **lensing magnification**: gravitational lensing by foreground clusters magnifies $z > 10$ galaxies by factors of $\sim 10$-$100$, putting them within reach.

key surveys: JADES, CEERS, COSMOS-Web, GLASS-JWST, UNCOVER, ENIS, NIRSpec/NIRCam GTO programs.

## Pop III spectroscopic signatures at high z

predictions for Pop III spectra (Schaerer 2002; Inoue 2011):

1. **He II 1640 emission**: requires $T_{\rm eff} > 10^5$ K, only achievable in extremely massive low-metallicity stars; rest-frame 1640 Å redshifts to NIRCam coverage at $z = 7$-$15$.
2. **strong UV continuum**, specifically blue UV slope $\beta \sim -3$ (essentially metal-free).
3. **Lyman continuum escape**: Pop III galaxies should be efficient ionising-photon producers.
4. **absence of metal lines**: no [O III] 5007, no C III] 1909 if truly Pop III.

high-equivalent-width He II 1640 with weak/absent metal lines is the cleanest Pop III spectroscopic signature.

## JWST candidates so far

several intriguing candidates have been reported but not yet definitively confirmed:

- **GLASS-z12 / GN-z11**: $z = 10.6$, JADES NIRSpec confirmation, low-metallicity but not Pop III pure.
- **LAP1-B** (Vanzella et al. 2023): lensed object at $z = 6.6$ with $M_* < 10^4\,M_\odot$, low metallicity, possible early-galaxy candidate.
- **CR7** (Sobral et al. 2015): pre-JWST candidate at $z = 6.6$ with strong He II + weak metal lines; later observations weakened the Pop III claim.
- **JADES-GS-z14-0**: $z = 14$, the highest confirmed redshift galaxy as of 2024-25.

so far, no unambiguous **pure Pop III** detection has been made. all systems show some metal enrichment, suggesting that even the earliest detected galaxies have already undergone Pop III $\to$ Pop II transition.

## the protocluster + first-cluster connection

JWST has also enabled:

- **Sparkler galaxy** (Mowla et al. 2022; Adamo + Padova group ongoing) — a lensed $z \sim 1.4$ galaxy in the SMACS 0723 field with star-cluster-like sources around it; possibly first-detected protoglobular clusters formed at $z \sim 9$.
- **first galaxies = first proto-GC sites**: present-day GCs may form in just-starting galaxies at $z \sim 8$-$15$, providing the link between high-z observations + local GC science.

these observations are the **direct interface** between [[Multiple populations in GCs discovery|multiple-population]] science (Milone's specialty) and high-redshift galaxy formation: present-day GCs may be the fossils of the first JWST-observable starbursts.

## remaining challenges

- contrast: Pop III SF regions are intrinsically faint; even with JWST sensitivity, only the most magnified targets are reachable.
- contamination: dust + metal-line emission from neighbouring Pop II material can contaminate Pop III He II detections.
- spectroscopy depth: $R \sim 1000$ NIRSpec is sufficient for redshift determination but may miss subtle line ratio differences.

upcoming surveys (JWST cycle 4-6 + ELT first light) should resolve these issues.

## why this matters

JWST is closing a $50$-year gap between theoretical Pop III predictions and direct observation. for the Stellar Astrophysics course, this represents:

- the convergence of [[Population III stars|Pop III theory]] + [[Pop III nucleosynthesis signatures|stellar archaeology in UFDGs]] + JWST direct detection;
- the link between resolved-stellar-population science (the bulk of the course) + high-redshift galaxy formation;
- the realisation that present-day GCs (Milone's research) may be the surviving fossils of the first galaxies JWST is now imaging.
