title: "X-ray sources in globular clusters"
---
X-ray observations of [[Globular Clusters]] reveal a zoo of compact-object binaries that is otherwise invisible. the high spatial resolution of Chandra ($\sim 0.5''$) is essential because GC cores are crowded; pre-Chandra data could only see the brightest few sources per cluster. Chandra surveys since 2000 (heinke et al., grindlay et al., pooley et al.) have catalogued thousands of X-ray sources across the galactic GC system.

**bright X-ray sources: LMXBs**

the brightest X-ray sources are accreting neutron stars in LMXBs with $L_X \sim 10^{36}\text{--}10^{38}\,\text{erg/s}$. the donor is typically an evolved star (often a [[Red giant branch RGB|RGB]] subgiant) overflowing its Roche lobe. only $\sim 15$ persistent and transient bright LMXBs are known in galactic GCs, but per unit stellar mass this is $\sim 100\times$ the rate in the field disc. the boost is the same dynamical processing argument: NS-MS exchanges in dense cores produce LMXBs.

these bright LMXBs are the progenitors of [[Millisecond pulsars in GCs|MSPs]]: when the donor exhausts its envelope, the NS is left as a recycled MSP.

**quiescent LMXBs (qLMXBs)**

once accretion drops, the NS LMXB enters a low state: $L_X \sim 10^{32}\text{--}10^{33}\,\text{erg/s}$, with a soft thermal spectrum from the NS surface plus a faint power-law tail. these qLMXBs are extremely useful for measuring NS radii via fits to the H-atmosphere model, since the GC distance is well-known. clusters like NGC 6397, M13, and 47 Tuc each host a handful of qLMXBs.

**cataclysmic variables**

[[Cataclysmic variables in clusters|CVs]] (WD accreting from a low-mass companion) populate the soft X-ray sources at $L_X \sim 10^{30}\text{--}10^{33}\,\text{erg/s}$. the boundary layer between the disc and the WD radiates in soft X-rays, so CVs appear as relatively soft point sources.

**active binaries (ABs)**

at the faint end ($L_X \lesssim 10^{30}\,\text{erg/s}$), ABs (RS CVn-like systems and BY Dra binaries: tidally locked, rapidly rotating, magnetically active main-sequence or subgiant binaries) dominate. their X-rays come from coronal emission analogous to a hyperactive sun. ABs are the most numerous X-ray-emitting class in any GC.

**the X-ray luminosity function**

a typical X-ray luminosity function in a massive GC has:
- a few bright sources ($> 10^{35}$ erg/s) (LMXBs),
- $\sim 10\text{--}100$ intermediate sources (qLMXBs, CVs),
- hundreds of faint sources (ABs).

**dynamical processing**

the total number of X-ray sources per unit cluster mass correlates with the encounter rate parameter $\Gamma \propto \rho^2 / \sigma$ better than with cluster mass alone, confirming that dynamical interactions in dense cores form most exotica.

**identifications and follow-up**

X-ray positions need optical/UV/H$\alpha$ counterparts to classify each source. HST photometry, MUSE IFU spectroscopy, and Gaia astrometry have enabled detailed classification across galactic GCs. some X-ray sources turn out to be [[Hunting BHs via radial velocities|stellar-mass black hole]] candidates if their companion is detected via radial velocities.
