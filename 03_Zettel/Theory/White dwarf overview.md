# white dwarf overview

white dwarfs (WDs) are the evolutionary end stage of more than 95% of all stars: every star with a zero-age main-sequence mass smaller than roughly $8\text{-}10\,M_\odot$ ends as a white dwarf, after the AGB envelope is shed in the Planetary nebula phase (or simply expelled by mass loss). this includes the sun. only the more massive minority go on to form neutron stars or black holes through core collapse.

a WD is essentially the exposed stellar core. its support against gravity does not come from thermal gas pressure but from the **degeneracy pressure of electrons**, a quantum effect: the Pauli exclusion principle forces electrons into ever-higher momentum states as the gas is compressed, producing a pressure that depends on density but not on temperature. so a white dwarf can keep itself up indefinitely without burning any fuel. it has no nuclear energy source and simply cools.

the typical numbers are striking. masses cluster around $0.6\,M_\odot$ with a tail up to $\sim 1.4\,M_\odot$, while radii are comparable to that of the Earth, $R \sim 0.01\,R_\odot \approx 7000$ km. the resulting mean density is of order $10^6$ g cm$^{-3}$. surface gravities are huge, $\log g \sim 8$, much higher than for normal main-sequence stars, and this leaves clear fingerprints on their spectra (very broad pressure-broadened lines, see [Spectroscopic determination of log g](./Spectroscopic%20determination%20of%20log%20g.html)).

in the [HR diagram](./HR%20diagram.html) white dwarfs occupy a sequence well below the main sequence, displaced toward higher temperatures at any given luminosity (the small radius means a small surface area, so for a given luminosity they must be hot). in cluster CMDs they appear as the **white dwarf cooling sequence (WDCS)**, a faint, narrow stripe to the lower left. see [Color-magnitude diagrams of clusters](./Color-magnitude%20diagrams%20of%20clusters.html) and the canonical sketch ![L06_p11_white_dwarf_HRD-11.png](../../assets/images/L06_p11_white_dwarf_HRD-11.png).

key qualitative properties:

- they are stellar core remnants, mostly carbon-oxygen (see [White dwarf types He CO ONeMg](./White%20dwarf%20types%20He%20CO%20ONeMg.html)).
- support is by electron degeneracy pressure, not thermal pressure.
- there is a maximum mass (the [Chandrasekhar mass limit](./Chandrasekhar%20mass%20limit.html), $\sim 1.44\,M_\odot$).
- they exhibit an inverse [White dwarf mass-radius relation](./White%20dwarf%20mass-radius%20relation.html): more massive WDs are smaller.
- they evolve almost only by cooling (see [White dwarf cooling theory](./White%20dwarf%20cooling%20theory.html)), so position in the CMD encodes age.
- at the faint end the cooling sequence turns blue, see [WDCS turn to blue and CIA](./WDCS%20turn%20to%20blue%20and%20CIA.html).

their atmospheres are usually pure H (DA type) or pure He (DB), since gravitational settling separates elements very efficiently at $\log g \sim 8$. heavier metals sink out of sight on short timescales unless replenished from outside (this is where polluted WDs come from).
