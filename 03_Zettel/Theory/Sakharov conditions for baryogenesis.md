# Sakharov conditions for baryogenesis

> *the three mandatory physical requirements for dynamically generating the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe.*

---

## core physical intuition

The observed universe contains matter (protons, neutrons, electrons) but virtually no primordial antimatter. In 1967, Andrei Sakharov proved that no matter-antimatter asymmetry can be dynamically generated from an initially symmetric early universe unless three fundamental conditions are simultaneously satisfied:
1. Baryon number violation
2. C and CP symmetry violation
3. Departure from thermal equilibrium

---

## key derivation & equations

The observed baryon-to-photon ratio is:
$$\eta_B \equiv \frac{n_b - n_{\bar{b}}}{n_\gamma} = (6.12 \pm 0.04) \times 10^{-10}$$

### The three Sakharov conditions:

1. **Baryon number ($B$) violation**:
   $$\Delta B \neq 0$$
   If $B$ is strictly conserved, an initial state with $\langle B \rangle = 0$ will remain zero for all time.
2. **$C$ and $CP$ violation**:
   * If Charge conjugation ($C$) is conserved: $\Gamma(X \to q q) = \Gamma(\bar{X} \to \bar{q}\bar{q})$, producing identical rates for particles and antiparticles.
   * If Parity-Charge conjugation ($CP$) is conserved: left-handed and right-handed decay asymmetries cancel out.
3. **Departure from thermal equilibrium**:
   In thermal equilibrium, the CPT theorem ensures that particles and antiparticles have identical masses ($m_X = m_{\bar{X}}$). The equilibrium phase-space distributions depend only on mass and temperature:
   $$n_X^{\rm eq} = n_{\bar{X}}^{\rm eq}$$
   Furthermore, any reaction producing a baryon asymmetry is exactly balanced by the inverse reaction destroying it (detailed balance). An asymmetry can only survive if reactions freeze out out of equilibrium.

---

## astrophysical context

* Explains the survival of ordinary baryonic matter against total annihilation in the early universe.
* Standard Model electroweak baryogenesis fails because the CP violation in the CKM matrix is too small ($10^{-20}$) and the electroweak phase transition is a smooth crossover, necessitating new physics.
