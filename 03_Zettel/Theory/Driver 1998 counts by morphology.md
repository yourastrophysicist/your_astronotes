# driver 1998 counts by morphology

up: [Pablo_02_Statistical_properties_of_galaxies](../../02_Literature/Lectures/Observational_Cosmology/Pablo_02_Statistical_properties_of_galaxies.html) · [Galaxy number counts N(m)](./Galaxy%20number%20counts%20N%28m%29.html)

## the experiment

Driver et al. 1998 took the Hubble Deep Field (HDF), classified visually each galaxy into **E/S0 (ellipticals + lenticulars)**, **Sabc (early spirals)**, **Sd/Irr (late spirals + irregulars)**, and counted them as a function of $I_{814}$ magnitude.

the HDF was deep enough ($I \lesssim 28$) to resolve morphologies for thousands of galaxies, which earlier ground-based work could not do.

## the result

three different counts, three different slopes:

- **E/S0**: the count rises but flattens by $I \sim 22$, roughly tracking the no-evolution prediction. this is consistent with ellipticals being old, passively evolving systems whose number density does not change much since $z \sim 1$.
- **Sabc**: similar to E/S0 but slightly steeper, with mild excess at faint magnitudes. consistent with mild luminosity evolution.
- **Sd/Irr**: counts *much* steeper than the no-evolution prediction. by $I \sim 25$ they outnumber both other classes combined. this is the famous *faint blue galaxy excess*.

## why this matters cosmologically

the faint blue galaxy excess is the signature of a population of *small, blue, irregular* galaxies that were much more abundant at $z \sim 0.3$–$1$ than today. there are two ways to produce it:

1. they merged into present-day larger galaxies (hierarchical buildup),
2. they faded into present-day low-surface-brightness dwarfs (luminosity evolution).

probably both. either way, the morphological split tells you that the *total* count slope at faint magnitudes is dominated by a population that does not look like local galaxies.

## what driver 1998 used the counts for

he overplotted three cosmological models, $\Omega_M = 1, 0.3, 0.05$, on the morphology-segregated counts. the E/S0 counts (least evolving) preferred $\Omega_M = 0.3$, providing pre-supernova evidence for a low-density universe. supernova cosmology two years later confirmed it more cleanly with $\Omega_\Lambda$.
