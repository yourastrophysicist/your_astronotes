title: "Primordial helium abundance from HII regions"

low-metallicity extragalactic H II regions (such as blue compact dwarf galaxies I Zw 18 and SBS 0335-052) provide the cleanest observational measurement of the primordial helium mass fraction $Y_p$ produced during Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN).

## physical method

in H II regions with ionizing stars hotter than $\sim 35000\text{ K}$, helium is singly ionized. optical recombination lines:
- He I $\lambda 4471$ ($4^3D \rightarrow 2^3P$)
- He I $\lambda 5876$ ($3^3D \rightarrow 2^3P$)
- He I $\lambda 6678$ ($3^1D \rightarrow 2^1P$)

the ionic ratio is determined relative to hydrogen recombination:

$$\frac{n(He^+)}{n(H^+)} = \frac{I(\text{He I } \lambda 5876)}{I(\text{H}\beta)} \frac{\alpha_{\text{eff}}(\text{H}\beta)}{\alpha_{\text{eff}}(\lambda 5876)}$$

## cosmological linear regression

stars produce additional helium alongside oxygen and nitrogen through stellar nucleosynthesis. to isolate primordial helium from stellar processing:

$$Y = Y_p + \left(\frac{dY}{dZ}\right) Z \approx Y_p + \left(\frac{dY}{d(O/H)}\right) (O/H)$$

extrapolating to zero metallicity ($O/H \rightarrow 0$):

$$Y_p \approx 0.245 \pm 0.003$$

this empirical value is in complete concordance with standard Big Bang Nucleosynthesis ($Y_p = 0.247$) derived from the baryon-to-photon ratio $\eta$ measured by the Planck CMB power spectrum.
