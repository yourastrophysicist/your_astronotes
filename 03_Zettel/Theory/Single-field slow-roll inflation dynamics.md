# Single-field slow-roll inflation dynamics

> *the classical mechanics of a scalar field rolling down a sufficiently flat potential to drive accelerated expansion.*

---

## core physical intuition

A homogeneous scalar field $\phi(t)$ minimally coupled to gravity has an energy density $\rho_\phi = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2 + V(\phi)$ and pressure $p_\phi = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2 - V(\phi)$. When the potential energy dominates over the kinetic energy ($\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2 \ll V(\phi)$), the pressure becomes negative ($p_\phi \approx -\rho_\phi$), mimicking a cosmological constant and driving quasi-exponential expansion ($a(t) \propto e^{Ht}$).

The expansion of space generates a Hubble friction term $3H\dot{\phi}$ in the Klein-Gordon equation. If the potential is sufficiently flat, this friction balances the gravitational slope, causing the field to roll slowly at terminal velocity.

---

## key derivation & equations

The action for a real scalar field minimally coupled to Einstein gravity is:
$$S = \int d^4 x \sqrt{-g} \left[ \frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2} R + \frac{1}{2} g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi - V(\phi) \right]$$

Varying with respect to $\phi$ gives the Klein-Gordon equation in flat FLRW spacetime:
$$\ddot{\phi} + 3H\dot{\phi} + V'(\phi) = 0$$

The Friedmann equation is:
$$H^2 = \frac{1}{3 M_{\rm Pl}^2}\left[ \frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2 + V(\phi) \right]$$

The exact relation for the time derivative of $H$ is:
$$\dot{H} = -\frac{\dot{\phi}^2}{2 M_{\rm Pl}^2}$$

### The slow-roll conditions
1. Kinetic energy much smaller than potential energy:
   $$\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2 \ll V(\phi) \implies H^2 \approx \frac{V(\phi)}{3 M_{\rm Pl}^2}$$
2. Acceleration much smaller than friction:
   $$|\ddot{\phi}| \ll |3H\dot{\phi}| \implies 3H\dot{\phi} \approx -V'(\phi)$$

Under these conditions, the field rolls with steady velocity:
$$\dot{\phi} \approx -\frac{V'(\phi)}{3H} \approx -\frac{M_{\rm Pl} V'}{\sqrt{3V}}$$
