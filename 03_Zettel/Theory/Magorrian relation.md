# magorrian relation

up: [Pablo_02_Statistical_properties_of_galaxies](../../02_Literature/Lectures/Observational_Cosmology/Pablo_02_Statistical_properties_of_galaxies.html)

## the relation

every massive galaxy hosts a central supermassive black hole (SMBH), and its mass correlates with the host's bulge stellar mass:

$$M_{BH} \approx 10^{-3}\, M_{\text{bulge}}$$

with $\sim 0.3$ dex scatter. equivalently, the BH carries $\sim 0.1\%$ of the bulge's stellar mass, across $\sim 6$ orders of magnitude in $M_{BH}$.

## who measured it

- **Magorrian et al. 1998**, AJ 115, 2285: fit dynamical $M_{BH}$ from HST stellar kinematics for 36 nearby galaxies. found the original ratio.
- **Ferrarese & Merritt 2000, Gebhardt et al. 2000**: $M_{BH}$-$\sigma$ relation, $M_{BH} \propto \sigma^4$, even tighter than Magorrian. velocity dispersion is a better predictor than bulge mass.
- **Kormendy & Ho 2013** ARAA: comprehensive review, $M_{BH}/M_{\text{bulge}} \approx 0.5\%$ for classical bulges and ellipticals, but pseudo-bulges and disks fall *off* the relation.

## why it matters

it is the empirical evidence for **co-evolution** of black holes and their host galaxies. the BH cannot directly see most of the galaxy (its sphere of influence is $\sim 10$ pc; the galaxy is $\sim 10$ kpc), so the correlation has to be mediated by something. the dominant explanation: **AGN feedback** in the past quasar phase regulated both the BH growth and the host's gas supply, locking them into a self-similar ratio.

this is the high-mass cutoff in the [Stellar-to-halo mass ratio](./Stellar-to-halo%20mass%20ratio.html) showing up in another guise.

## what falls off

- pseudo-bulges (built by secular disk processes) have systematically smaller $M_{BH}$ at given bulge mass
- pure-disk galaxies are below the relation
- intermediate-mass BHs (IMBHs) in dwarfs (e.g. NGC 4395) are sometimes detected but the relation breaks down

## connections

- AGN side of feedback: [Halo gravity suppression of galaxy formation](./Halo%20gravity%20suppression%20of%20galaxy%20formation.html)
