title: "Polarization E and B modes"
---
the CMB is polarised at the $\sim 10\%$ level of temperature anisotropies. polarisation decomposes into **E-modes** (curl-free) + **B-modes** (divergence-free). each carries different physics.

## the source: Thomson scattering

at recombination, photons scatter off free electrons via Thomson scattering. the scattered photon's polarisation depends on the geometry of the local **temperature quadrupole**.

specifically: an electron sees a quadrupole pattern in the surrounding photon field. Thomson scattering converts this into **linear polarisation** of the outgoing photon. so CMB polarisation reflects the **quadrupole** at the surface of last scattering.

## the E/B decomposition

CMB polarisation forms a vector field on the sky (well, a tensor field, but visualisable as line segments showing direction + amplitude). like any vector field on a 2-sphere, it splits into:
- **E-mode** ("electric"): curl-free part, like an electric field. radial / tangential patterns around peaks.
- **B-mode** ("magnetic"): divergence-free part, like a magnetic field. spiral / vortical patterns.

## E-modes

generated by:
- **scalar (density) perturbations** at recombination (the same source as temperature anisotropies).
- **reionisation** at $z \sim 7$: photons re-scatter off freed electrons, generating new polarisation at large angular scales.

E-mode power spectrum has acoustic peaks similar to temperature, but **out of phase** by $\pi/2$. correlated with temperature: TE cross-spectrum, also measured.

E-modes detected by **WMAP, Planck, ACTPol, BICEP, etc.** at $\sim 10\%$ precision.

## B-modes

two sources:
1. **primordial gravitational waves** from inflation: generate **primordial B-modes**. amplitude $\propto r$, the tensor-to-scalar ratio.
2. **gravitational lensing of E-modes** by intervening LSS: distorts E-modes into B-modes at small angular scales. **secondary B-modes**.

scalar perturbations alone produce **no** B-modes (E/B decomposition is exact for linear scalars). so detecting B-modes is **direct evidence** of either tensor perturbations or non-trivial physics.

## the inflation prize

primordial B-modes from inflation have amplitude $\propto r = A_t/A_s$ (tensor-to-scalar ratio). measuring $r$ would:
- confirm inflation (inflation predicts primordial GW; alternatives don't).
- determine the energy scale of inflation: $E_{\rm inf}^4 \propto r$.
- distinguish slow-roll inflation models.

current upper bound (BICEP/Keck + Planck): $r < 0.06$. corresponds to $E_{\rm inf} < 1.6 \times 10^{16}$ GeV.

several future experiments (CMB-S4, SO, LiteBIRD, AliCPT) target $r \sim 10^{-3}$ to $10^{-2}$, covering most well-motivated inflation models.

## the lensing B-modes

discovered by SPTpol 2015 + others. signal at $\ell \gtrsim 100$, expected from CMB lensing by intervening LSS. matches $\Lambda$CDM prediction well. used to constrain $\sum m_\nu$ + late-time growth.

## B-mode foregrounds

main contaminants:
- **Galactic dust polarisation**: dust grains aligned by Galactic magnetic field emit polarised. dominant on large angular scales.
- **synchrotron emission**: from cosmic-ray electrons in Galactic field. dominant at low frequencies.

current B-mode searches (BICEP/Keck) use multi-frequency observations to subtract these. Galactic foregrounds are the **biggest barrier** to detecting primordial B-modes.
