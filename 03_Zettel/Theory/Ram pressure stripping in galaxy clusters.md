galaxies moving through the hot ($T \sim 10^7 - 10^8\text{ K}$), diffuse Intra-Cluster Medium (ICM) experience hydrodynamic drag.

## the gunn-gott criterion (1972)

hydrodynamic ram pressure exerted on a galaxy moving at velocity $v_{\text{gal}}$:

$$P_{\text{ram}} = \rho_{\text{ICM}} v_{\text{gal}}^2$$

the gravitational restoring force per unit area anchoring interstellar gas to the stellar disk:

$$F_{\text{grav}} = 2\pi G \Sigma_* \Sigma_{\text{gas}}$$

where $\Sigma_*$ and $\Sigma_{\text{gas}}$ are the stellar and gas surface mass densities.

stripping occurs when:

$$\boxed{P_{\text{ram}} > 2\pi G \Sigma_* \Sigma_{\text{gas}}}$$

## consequences

- cold H I and molecular gas are stripped into the cluster environment, leaving gas-deficient spirals.
- star formation is rapidly quenched, transforming star-forming spirals into passive lenticular (S0) galaxies.
- stripped gas forms trailing tails with active star formation, producing **"Jellyfish Galaxies"** (e.g. ESO 137-001).
