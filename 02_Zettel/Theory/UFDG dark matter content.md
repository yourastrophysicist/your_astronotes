name: UFDG dark matter content
description: high mass-to-light ratios in ultra-faint dwarfs as evidence for dark matter dominance and constraints on warm DM models
---

ultra-faint dwarf galaxies are the **most dark-matter-dominated objects known**. their mass-to-light ratios within the half-light radius reach $M/L \sim 100$-$1000$ in solar units, far exceeding what the stellar population can produce ($M/L \sim 1$-$5$ for an old stellar population). the inferred mass is overwhelmingly non-baryonic.

**how the mass is measured**: UFDGs are **pressure-supported** spheroids without measurable rotation. for a system in dynamical equilibrium, the jeans equation relates the line-of-sight velocity dispersion to the enclosed mass. a useful estimator is the wolf et al. 2010 mass within the half-light radius:

$$M(<r_{1/2}) \approx \frac{3 \langle \sigma_{\text{los}}^2 \rangle r_{1/2}}{G}$$

so once you measure $\sigma_v \sim 3$-$8$ km/s spectroscopically and $r_{1/2} \sim 30$-$300$ pc photometrically, you have a robust mass within the half-light radius without strong assumptions about the velocity anisotropy. dividing by the stellar luminosity gives $M/L$.

**typical numbers**:

- segue 1: $\sigma_v \approx 3.7$ km/s, $r_{1/2} \approx 30$ pc, $M/L \sim 3000$.
- coma berenices: $\sigma_v \approx 4.6$ km/s, $M/L \sim 700$.
- reticulum ii: $\sigma_v \approx 3.2$ km/s, $M/L \sim 500$.
- across the UFDG population: $M/L$ spans roughly $100$ to a few thousand, with stellar mass $\sim 10^3$-$10^5 M_\odot$ and dynamical mass $\sim 10^6$-$10^7 M_\odot$ within $r_{1/2}$.

**no rotation**: long-slit and multi-fibre spectroscopy of UFDG members detects no significant rotation curve. they are kinematically hot and structurally round. this rules out a rotationally-supported origin (no disk, no tidal stream of disk debris) and confirms they are **dark-matter halo dwellers**.

**implications for dark matter physics**:

- UFDGs are the **smallest galaxies that exist**. their existence at all requires a dark-matter power spectrum that allows structure on $\sim 10^7$-$10^8 M_\odot$ subhalo scales.
- **warm dark matter** (sterile neutrinos, $\sim$ keV particles) suppresses small-scale structure via free streaming. the abundance and inner profiles of UFDGs constrain WDM particle masses to $m_{\text{WDM}} \gtrsim 3$ keV (thermal relic equivalent).
- **fuzzy / ultralight DM** ($m_a \sim 10^{-22}$ eV) predicts a soliton core and a quantum-pressure cutoff. UFDG kinematics constrain $m_a \gtrsim 10^{-21}$ eV.
- **self-interacting DM** would produce cored profiles. some UFDG kinematic data are consistent with cores rather than NFW cusps (the "core-cusp problem"), but the data quality is not yet decisive.

**indirect detection**: high $M/L$ plus low astrophysical foregrounds make UFDGs prime targets for **dark matter annihilation searches** with fermi-LAT and atmospheric cherenkov telescopes (HESS, MAGIC, VERITAS, CTA in the future). reticulum ii drew attention in 2015 with a marginal gamma-ray excess that has not been confirmed. stacked analyses of UFDGs give some of the tightest constraints on WIMP cross-sections in the $\sim 10$-$100$ GeV mass range.

**caveats**: very small velocity dispersions are vulnerable to systematics from **binary star contamination** (which inflates $\sigma_v$) and **tidal disturbance** (which can also inflate $\sigma_v$ if the system is being disrupted). multi-epoch radial velocities and gaia proper motions help control these. some UFDG candidates with very small samples have $\sigma_v$ uncertain at the factor-of-two level.

UFDG dark matter content is, after galactic rotation curves and CMB peaks, one of the cleanest pieces of evidence that dark matter exists, behaves cold-ish, and assembles into bound halos down to very small masses.
