title: "Calcium and CaII H+K"
---

**calcium** ($Z = 20$, $[Ar]\, 4s^2$) has a strong neutral and singly ionised spectrum. the Ca II H + K resonance lines at $\sim 3970, 3934$ Å are among the **strongest features in late-type stellar and integrated galaxy spectra**.

## the H + K lines

Ca II ($4s^1$ ground, hydrogenic on Ca$^+$ core) has its first excited state at $4p$, split by spin-orbit:
- **Ca II K**: $4\,^2P_{3/2}^o \to 4\,^2S_{1/2}^e$, $\lambda = 3933.66$ Å.
- **Ca II H**: $4\,^2P_{1/2}^o \to 4\,^2S_{1/2}^e$, $\lambda = 3968.47$ Å.

separation $\sim 35$ Å. K is stronger than H by a factor of 2 (statistical-weight ratio $g_{3/2}/g_{1/2} = 4/2$).

## why so prominent

three reasons combine:
1. **resonance lines**: from the ground state, so accessible to any photon at that wavelength.
2. **abundance**: Ca is reasonably abundant (logarithmic abundance $\sim 6.3$, comparable to Si).
3. **ionisation**: at $T \sim 4000$ to $7000$ K, most Ca is already singly ionised (Saha equation), so the Ca II ground state is well-populated.

so in solar-type and cooler stars, Ca II H + K are extraordinarily strong, often saturated absorbers with damping wings extending tens of angstroms.

## the Ca II IR triplet

Ca II also has a strong **infrared triplet** at $8498, 8542, 8662$ Å from $3\,^2D \to 4\,^2P^o$:
- $\lambda 8498$: $3\,^2D_{3/2} \to 4\,^2P_{1/2}^o$.
- $\lambda 8542$: $3\,^2D_{5/2} \to 4\,^2P_{3/2}^o$ (strongest).
- $\lambda 8662$: $3\,^2D_{3/2} \to 4\,^2P_{3/2}^o$.

these are **near-IR** equivalents of the H + K lines but coming from the metastable $^2D$ levels above the ground. they are seen in cool-star spectra and as **velocity dispersion** indicators for old galaxies (because they avoid the contaminating Balmer lines and are in a clean IR continuum window).

## the $4000$ Å break

just blueward of Ca II H + K lies a **smooth blanket of Fe and CN absorption lines** that depresses the continuum. combined with the H + K saturation, this produces a sharp **discontinuity** in galaxy spectra at $\sim 4000$ Å rest-frame:
$$D_n4000 = \frac{\int_{4000-4100\,\text{\AA}} F_\lambda d\lambda}{\int_{3850-3950\,\text{\AA}} F_\lambda d\lambda}$$

this is an **age-sensitive index** for stellar populations:
- young SF galaxies: $D_n4000 \approx 1.0$ (no break, blue continuum).
- intermediate age: $1.3$ to $1.6$.
- old quenched galaxies: $1.7$ to $2.2$ (deep break).

so Ca II H + K + the $4000$ Å break are **the** primary age diagnostic for unresolved galaxy spectra.

## variability and chromospheric activity

in **active stars** (rapid rotators, young Sun-like stars), the cores of Ca II H + K show **emission peaks** from chromospheric heating. the **Mt. Wilson S-index** quantifies this and tracks stellar magnetic-cycle activity over decades. used in exoplanet-hunting surveys to monitor host-star activity (which contaminates radial-velocity searches).

## as cosmological tracer

at moderate redshift, Ca II H + K shifts to redder optical wavelengths ($\sim 5800$ Å at $z = 0.5$). they remain the brightest absorption features and are routinely used to measure spectroscopic redshifts of galaxies in $z = 0$ to $1$ surveys (SDSS, BOSS).
