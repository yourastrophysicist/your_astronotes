---

the OBAFGKM scheme didn't appear from nothing. it evolved over $\sim 80$ years of refinement, anchored in Padre Secchi's 1860s photographic plates and culminating in the MK system of 1953. a brief history.

## Padre Angelo Secchi (1818-1878)

the Jesuit priest at the Roman Observatory was the first to systematically classify stars by their spectra, in the 1860s. analysed $\sim 4000$ spectra and proposed five classes:

| Secchi class | description |
|---|---|
| I | white and blue stars with strong hydrogen lines |
| II | yellow stars, hydrogen weaker, evident metallic lines |
| III | orange to red with complex band spectra (TiO) |
| IV | red with carbon bands (now C-stars / N-stars) |
| V | emission-line stars (now Be, P Cygni, novae) |

he noticed the temperature trend across his classes, though formal $T$ assignments came later.

## Henry Draper (1837-1882) and the Harvard plates

in the late 1880s, Edward Pickering at Harvard began an enormous photographic-plate spectroscopy programme. funded in part by a memorial to Henry Draper, the project produced the **Henry Draper Catalogue (HD)** with $\sim 10\,000$ stellar spectra by 1890.

Williamina Fleming led the early classification, using letters A-Q based on the relative strength of hydrogen Balmer lines. her scheme was refined by Annie Jump Cannon.

## Annie Jump Cannon (1863-1941)

Cannon classified $\sim 350\,000$ spectra by eye, completing the **HD Extension** in the 1920s. she reordered the letter classes by temperature: O B A F G K M (the famous mnemonic "Oh Be A Fine Girl Kiss Me"). the key ordering choice: noticing that the hydrogen-strength ordering of the Drapers couldn't be a true temperature sequence because hydrogen line strength was non-monotonic.

her classification became the **Harvard system** and is the basis of all modern stellar classification.

## Cecilia Payne-Gaposchkin (1900-1979)

Payne's 1925 PhD thesis at Harvard was the breakthrough that **interpreted** the Harvard sequence physically. applying Boltzmann + Saha (just then formulated by Saha 1920) to stellar spectra, she derived:

- stars are mostly hydrogen + helium.
- the OBAFGKM sequence is a **temperature sequence**, with line-strength patterns naturally produced by Saha + Boltzmann at the temperature of each class.

her conclusion was so radical that her advisor Henry Norris Russell asked her to qualify it. she did, and the conclusion was confirmed within 5 years. it remains one of the most cited and important PhD theses in astrophysics.

## William Morgan, Philip Keenan, Edith Kellman (1943, 1953)

at Yerkes Observatory in 1943, the **MKK system** (Morgan-Keenan-Kellman) added **luminosity classes** to the Harvard temperature sequence. five luminosity classes (I to V) based on line-width and line-shape diagnostics that depend on stellar surface gravity.

the 1953 revision (MK system) further refined this with:
- decimal subdivisions of each letter (e.g. G2 V for the Sun).
- detailed atlas of standard stars at each MK class.
- clear definitions of luminosity classes.

the **MK system** is the modern stellar classification used today, with extensions for unusual classes (Wolf-Rayet, peculiar A stars, lithium-rich stars, etc.).

## the modern context

since the MK system, additions include:
- **L, T, Y** classes for brown dwarfs and very cool dwarfs (added in the 1990s and 2000s).
- **WC, WN** subclasses for Wolf-Rayet stars.
- **C, S** classes for carbon stars and zirconium-oxide-band stars.
- **DA, DB, DZ, DQ** for white dwarfs of different compositions.

stellar atmosphere codes now produce synthetic spectra so detailed that they can recover MK class from a single high-S/N spectrum. but the MK system remains the **lingua franca** of stellar astronomy, used for everything from variable-star catalogs to stellar populations.

## the broader lesson

Cecilia Payne's insight: **stars are essentially the same composition; the diversity of spectra is just a temperature ordering**. this is the spectroscopic version of the cosmological principle. it makes possible all of stellar astrophysics: a single grid of stellar atmosphere models in $(T_{\rm eff}, \log g, [Fe/H])$ predicts every star's spectrum.
