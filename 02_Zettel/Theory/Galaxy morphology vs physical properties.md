title: "Galaxy morphology vs physical properties"
---

galaxy morphology (the visual classification along the Hubble fork) correlates strongly with **physical properties**: color, mass, gas content, stellar populations, environment. these correlations are the empirical foundation for understanding galaxy evolution.

---

## the central correlations

| morphology | typical color | gas content | dominant stars | typical environment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E** | red | very low | old (Pop II) | dense, clusters |
| **S0** | red | low | old | clusters, dense field |
| **Sa-Sb** | intermediate | moderate | mix | mixed |
| **Sc-Sd** | blue | rich | young + old | field |
| **Irr** | blue | very rich | young | field |

so as you move along the Hubble sequence from **E to Irr**:
- color shifts from red to blue
- gas content increases from $\sim 0$ to $\sim 30\%$ of total mass
- stellar age decreases
- specific star formation rate (sSFR = SFR/$M_*$) increases
- environment changes from cluster cores to the field

this pattern is fundamental.

---

## the morphology-density relation

Dressler (1980) showed that galaxy morphology depends strongly on the **local environment**:
- in cluster cores: ~80% of galaxies are E or S0
- in the field: ~70% are S or Irr

i.e. dense environments are correlated with passive, red, gas-poor galaxies.

physical interpretation: **environmental quenching**. processes that strip or heat gas in dense environments include:
- **ram-pressure stripping**: as a galaxy moves through the hot intracluster medium, the gas is pushed out by ram pressure
- **harassment**: gravitational interactions with nearby galaxies disturb the disk
- **strangulation/starvation**: cold gas inflows are cut off, preventing further star formation
- **major mergers**: produce compact ellipticals

so the morphology-density relation reflects the environmental history of galaxies.

---

## the color bimodality

galaxies fall into two clear families in $u-r$ vs $M_r$ space (see [Color bimodality of galaxies](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Color bimodality of galaxies.md) in the Observational Cosmology MOC):
- **blue cloud**: mostly Sc-Sd-Irr, blue colors, active star formation
- **red sequence**: mostly E-S0-Sa, red colors, passive

with a sparsely-populated **green valley** between them. this strongly suggests galaxies undergo a **rapid transition** from blue to red, rather than slowly drifting.

quenching mechanisms that could drive this transition:
- AGN feedback
- ram-pressure stripping
- virial shock heating in massive halos
- mergers and morphological transformation

---

## the bulge fraction connection

if you classify galaxies by **bulge-to-disk ratio (B/D)** rather than by Hubble type:
- Sa: B/D $\sim 1$
- Sb: B/D $\sim 0.5$
- Sc: B/D $\sim 0.2$
- Sd: B/D $\sim 0.05$

so the Hubble sequence is essentially a **bulge-to-disk continuum**. the bulge is old and dispersion-dominated; the disk is young and rotation-dominated. Hubble type is a measure of which one dominates.

modern galaxies often have **multiple bulge types**:
- **classical bulges**: like mini-ellipticals, formed by merging (E-like)
- **pseudobulges**: formed by secular evolution of the disk (disk-like, with rotation)

---

## mass and morphology

mass also correlates with morphology:
- the most massive galaxies ($M_* > 10^{11}\, M_\odot$) are mostly E and S0
- intermediate galaxies ($10^{10}-10^{11}\, M_\odot$) are mostly spirals
- low-mass galaxies are mostly irregulars or dwarf ellipticals

reasons:
- mergers that produce ellipticals are more common at high mass
- AGN feedback is more efficient in massive halos, suppressing star formation there
- gas accretion is shut off in halos above $\sim 10^{12}\, M_\odot$ (virial shock)

---

## the bigger picture: galaxy evolution

galaxies start as **gas-rich, low-mass, irregular** systems at high z. they grow by:
- **gas accretion** from cosmic filaments (cold flows at $z > 2$)
- **mergers** with other galaxies
- **star formation** within their disks

as they grow, they may:
- become spirals if the gas accretes smoothly
- become ellipticals if a major merger destroys the disk
- be quenched by AGN feedback or environmental processes
- end up on the red sequence as passive E or S0

so the present-day Hubble sequence is the **end-state** of multiple evolutionary paths. each galaxy's path depends on its mass, environment, and merger history.
