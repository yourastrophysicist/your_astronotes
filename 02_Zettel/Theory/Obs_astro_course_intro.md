the first-semester Observational Astrophysics course at the University of Padova, AY 2025/2026. unlike [Fundamentals_course_intro](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Fundamentals_course_intro.md) which sweeps through cosmology in one trunk, this course is the **observational pipeline**: how a real photon, on a real night, gets turned into a number with error bars.

## the conceptual chain

every lecture is one stage in this chain:
$$\text{coordinates} \to \text{atmosphere} \to \text{photons in a filter} \to \text{counts on a CCD} \to \text{magnitude} \to \text{distance} \to \text{intrinsic property of stars or populations}$$

so the syllabus is not "topics about astronomy" but a sequence of *steps* a photon takes from emission to scientific result.

## the seven lectures

1. **Spherical astronomy** (parts 1 and 2): how to point at the sky and describe coordinates.
2. **Earth's atmosphere**: what the photons go through last, why ground observation is hard.
3. **Radiative mechanisms and photometric concepts**: blackbody, magnitudes, filters, distance modulus.
4. **CCD and SNR**: detector physics, noise sources, the master SNR equation.
5. **Distance ladder**: AU, parallax, standard candles, Hubble flow.
6. **Stars and stellar populations**: HR diagram, classes, populations I/II/III, IMF.
7. **Population synthesis**: integrated light at high redshift, SED fitting.

## why this course matters

every later course (Stellar Astrophysics, Spectroscopy, Interferometry, Galaxies, Lab High Energy, Observational Cosmology) refines or specialises one piece of this chain. so this course is the **methodology layer**, the foundation of every observational claim I will ever make.

the exam tests fluency in the chain: I should be able to take any astrophysical question (e.g. "how do you measure the distance to galaxy X?", "how do you estimate the mass of a stellar population?") and walk it through coordinates → atmosphere → detector → calibration → physics, with the right formula at each step.
