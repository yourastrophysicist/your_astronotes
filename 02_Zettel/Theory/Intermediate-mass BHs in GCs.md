**intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs)** are hypothetical black holes with masses in the range $\sim 100$-$10^4\,M_\odot$, intermediate between stellar-mass BHs ($\sim 5$-$50\,M_\odot$) and supermassive BHs ($\geq 10^5\,M_\odot$ in galactic nuclei). globular clusters have long been suggested as the most natural environment to host them. their existence in GCs remains **controversial** despite decades of searches.

## why IMBHs in GCs are predicted

theoretical arguments:

1. **runaway stellar mergers**: in dense GC cores, the central few thousand massive stars can collide repeatedly within 1 Myr, building up to a single $\sim 10^3\,M_\odot$ object before SF ends (Portegies Zwart et al. 2002).
2. **scaling relations**: SMBH masses scale with bulge $\sigma$ (M-$\sigma$). extrapolating to GCs ($\sigma \sim 10$-$30$ km/s) predicts central BHs of $\sim 10^3$-$10^4\,M_\odot$.
3. **seed BHs for SMBH**: early universe IMBHs may grow into SMBHs via mergers; finding them in GC fossils tests this scenario.

## observational signatures

several methods, all challenging at $\sim 10^3\,M_\odot$ in dense crowded fields:

### 1. central stellar kinematics

a central IMBH should produce a **central velocity dispersion cusp**. detected by:
- HST + VLT-MUSE integrated-field spectroscopy of the central few arcsec;
- proper motions of individual cluster stars from multi-epoch HST.

predicted signature: $\sigma_v(r)$ rises sharply within the BH influence radius $r_h \sim G M_{\rm BH}/\sigma_*^2$.

problem: GCs are crowded, IMBH influence radii are small ($< 1$" for $10^3\,M_\odot$ at $\sim 10$ kpc), and the signal is degenerate with mass-segregated stellar dark remnants.

### 2. millisecond pulsar accelerations

[MSPs](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Millisecond pulsars in GCs.html) in GC cores act as precision clocks. acceleration of an MSP toward the cluster centre due to a central IMBH would produce a measurable orbital period derivative. successfully applied in Terzan 5, NGC 6440, M28 — none has yet given an unambiguous IMBH detection but constrains $M_{\rm BH} < 10^3$-$10^4\,M_\odot$.

### 3. radio + X-ray accretion signatures

an IMBH accreting cluster gas (sparse but non-zero) should produce weak radio + X-ray emission. detected in some GCs at flux levels consistent with $10^3\,M_\odot$ but ambiguous (LMXBs + accreting NSs can produce similar signatures).

example: **HLX-1 in ESO 243-49** is an off-nucleus point source with $L_X \sim 10^{42}$ erg/s, plausibly an IMBH of $\sim 10^4\,M_\odot$ in a stripped dwarf galaxy nucleus. but not a GC.

## the case of $\omega$ Centauri

$\omega$ Cen is the most massive MW GC + a likely accreted dwarf nucleus + has been claimed to host an IMBH of $\sim 4 \times 10^4\,M_\odot$ from central stellar dynamics (Noyola et al. 2008, ApJ 676, 1008). subsequent reanalyses have varied wildly:

- Anderson & van der Marel 2010: lower IMBH mass, $\sim 1 \times 10^4\,M_\odot$ or no IMBH at all.
- van der Marel & Anderson 2010: revised proper motion analysis suggests upper limit $M_{\rm BH} < 1.2 \times 10^4\,M_\odot$ at 99% confidence.
- Häberle et al. 2024 (Nature 631, 285): JWST + HST PM data revive the IMBH claim with $M_{\rm BH} \sim 8000\,M_\odot$ at 7$\sigma$ confidence based on fast-moving central stars.

so the $\omega$ Cen IMBH is currently a **strong candidate but not definitively confirmed**. similar disputes exist for M15, NGC 6388, NGC 1904, and NGC 5286.

## the case of 47 Tucanae + others

47 Tuc has yielded an upper limit $M_{\rm BH} \lesssim 1500\,M_\odot$ from MSP accelerations + central kinematics. similar limits exist for many MW GCs.

a key result: even if many GCs have IMBHs, they likely sit at $M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^2$-$10^3\,M_\odot$, well below the simple bulge $M$-$\sigma$ extrapolation. either GCs are intrinsically different from galactic nuclei, or IMBHs were ejected dynamically over their long lives.

## connection to gravitational waves

LIGO-Virgo BH-BH mergers (e.g., GW190521, $M = 85 + 66 \to 142\,M_\odot$) have produced **direct evidence** for IMBHs from a single merger event. the mass of GW190521's remnant ($\sim 142\,M_\odot$) sits in the lower IMBH range. some authors argue GCs are the natural production sites of such heavy BH-BH binaries (see [BHs from gravitational waves](../../02_Zettel/Theory/BHs from gravitational waves.html)).

## why this matters

IMBHs in GCs would be:
- a missing piece in the BH mass spectrum bridging stellar to supermassive;
- direct seeds of high-z SMBHs;
- contributors to GC dynamical evolution (single dominant BH dominates relaxation in the core);
- LIGO-Virgo merger source candidates.

confirming or denying their existence is one of the open frontiers of stellar dynamics + multi-messenger astronomy.
