# Dust shells and Wolf-Rayet stars

late-stage massive stars (Wolf-Rayet, AGB) lose mass at high rates. the lost material forms expanding shells of gas and dust around the star. interferometric imaging directly resolves these shells, measuring their geometry, velocity, and time evolution.

## the typical Wolf-Rayet star

a Wolf-Rayet (WR) star: a hot, evolved massive star ($M_{\rm initial} \gtrsim 25 M_\odot$) that has shed its hydrogen envelope. characteristics:
- $T_{\rm eff} = 30000$-$200000$ K
- $L = 10^5$-$10^6 L_\odot$
- mass-loss rate $\dot M = 10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4} M_\odot$/yr
- wind velocity ~1000-3000 km/s
- typical lifetime: $\sim 10^5$ years

the wind carries material away from the star. as it cools, dust forms — often in clumpy, asymmetric structures.

## what interferometry sees

![Hubble Space Telescope image of a Wolf-Rayet star surrounded by an expanding circumstellar gas and dust nebula.](Dust_shells_and_Wolf-Rayet_stars.jpg)


interferometric imaging of WR stars at K-band and N-band reveals:

### dust shells

extended dust emission at $r \sim 100$-1000 mas around the star. for a WR star at 1-3 kpc, this corresponds to $r = 100$-3000 AU. the dust geometry can be:
- **spherical** (uniform mass loss)
- **bipolar** (rotation-axis-driven outflow)
- **clumpy** (asymmetric mass loss, possibly from a binary)
- **spiral** (binary modulation of the wind)

### binary wind interactions

many WR stars are binaries (with O-type companions). the colliding stellar winds produce:
- shocks at the wind-collision region
- spiral patterns of heated/cooled gas
- asymmetric dust formation in the shock regions

VLTI imaging directly resolves these spirals, providing kinematic information about the binary.

## the canonical examples

### WR 104 (the "pinwheel")

an O+WR binary in Sagittarius. the colliding winds produce a striking *pinwheel* dust spiral. the spiral pitch and rotation give the orbital geometry directly.

VLTI MIDI imaging (2007): the spiral mapped at sub-mas resolution. orbital period $\sim 220$ days inferred from spiral pitch.

### gamma^2 Velorum

the closest known WR binary (340 pc). VLTI and CHARA imaging:
- WC8 + O7.5 binary
- separation 1.2 AU
- detailed wind-wind interaction at the inner orbital radius
- mass-loss rates and wind speeds for both components

### HD 192103 (WR 137)

distant WR binary with a periodic dust shell at the orbital phase of perihelion. each ~13-year orbit produces a new dust ring.

## AGB stars and Mira-type

similar physics applies to asymptotic-giant-branch stars (lower-mass evolved giants) like:
- **Mira (omicron Cet)**: pulsating AGB with a complex dust environment
- **TX Cam**: SiO-maser-emitting Mira with a polarized wind
- **CW Leo**: archetypal carbon-rich AGB, dust at <10 stellar radii

interferometric imaging shows molecular layers (water, OH, SiO masers), dust formation regions, and large-scale shell structures.

## the time evolution

for sources observed at multiple epochs (years apart), the dust shells *expand* visibly. interferometric measurements of the shell radius vs time give the *expansion velocity*:

$$v_{\rm exp} = \Delta r/\Delta t$$

typical wind velocities $\sim 10$-30 km/s for AGB, $\sim 1000$ km/s for WR. directly observed.

## the science

dust-shell observations reveal:

### 1. mass-loss rates

$\dot M$ from the dust mass and expansion velocity. compares with theoretical predictions.

### 2. wind launching mechanisms

for AGB: radiation pressure on dust grains drives the wind. interferometric measurements of dust formation radii test the theoretical models.

for WR: line-driven radiation pressure on hot ions. the wind acceleration profile, mapped via interferometry, tests these models.

### 3. binary effects

asymmetric or spiral structures indicate binarity. some WR stars previously thought single show binary signatures only via interferometry.

### 4. nucleosynthesis tracers

different dust mineralogies trace different chemical compositions. interferometric chemistry mapping (with multi-wavelength data) probes nucleosynthesis in the stellar interior.

## the chemistry connection

WR stars and AGB stars are major sources of:
- **carbon dust** (from C-rich AGB stars)
- **silicate dust** (from O-rich AGB stars)
- **oxygen-rich molecules** (water, OH)
- **carbon-bearing molecules** (HCN, CN)

interferometric imaging maps where each of these forms, providing direct constraints on stellar nucleosynthesis and the origin of cosmic dust.

## the interplay with ALMA

ALMA observes the *cooler* outer dust shells (sub-mm thermal emission). VLTI/CHARA observe the *inner* hot dust formation regions. together, they map the dust at all spatial scales.

modern WR star observations routinely combine both, telling a complete story of how a star loses mass and becomes a supernova progenitor.
