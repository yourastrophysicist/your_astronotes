the second-semester Astronomical Spectroscopy course at U Padova, AY 2025/2026, taught by Prof. Stefano Ciroi, with course notes building on lectures from Piero Rafanelli and Giovanni La Mura.

## the conceptual chain

every lecture is one stage in this chain:
$$\text{atomic physics} \to \text{level populations (Boltzmann + Saha)} \to \text{radiative transfer} \to \text{absorption / emission lines} \to \text{spectrograph} \to \text{calibrated spectrum} \to \text{plasma diagnostics}$$

it is the *complement* to [Observational_Astrophysics_MOC](../../00_Atlas/Observational_Astrophysics_MOC.html): where Obs Astro turns photons into magnitudes (one number per source), Spectroscopy resolves photons by wavelength (a vector per source) and asks what the gas was that emitted them.

## the two physical settings

every diagnostic in this course lives in one of two regimes:

1. **stellar atmospheres**: dense, near-LTE, Boltzmann + Saha at local $T$. spectrum = Planck continuum + absorption lines. line strengths from Block 3 + 4. used to measure stellar $T_{\rm eff}$, $\log g$, $[Fe/H]$, $v\sin i$, $v_r$.
2. **photoionised nebulae**: low density, far from LTE, statistical equilibrium with collisions + recombinations. spectrum = recombination + free-free continuum + emission lines + (often) forbidden lines. used to measure $T_e$, $n_e$, $A_V$, abundances.

## the two volumes of course material

- **`intro_spec.pdf`**: instrumentation focus. diffraction $\to$ gratings $\to$ spectrographs $\to$ stellar classification $\to$ object catalogue.
- **`notes_28Jul2021.pdf`**: theory focus. atomic spectroscopy $\to$ level populations $\to$ radiative transport $\to$ line physics $\to$ photoionisation equilibrium $\to$ thermal balance.

read together: the theory volume tells you *why* a line forms; the instrumentation volume tells you *how* you measure it.

## the seven examinable categories of diagnostic

oral exam questions almost always sit in one of these:
1. **spectrograph design**: derive resolution + dispersion from grating equation.
2. **atomic structure**: write down term symbols, apply Hund's rules, find ground state.
3. **selection rules**: identify whether a transition is permitted, forbidden, or semi-forbidden, and why.
4. **Saha + Boltzmann**: predict line strengths as a function of $T$, explain the OBAFGKM sequence.
5. **line broadening**: identify mechanisms, write down Lorentzian + Gaussian + Voigt, derive a curve of growth.
6. **nebular diagnostics**: $T_e$ from $[OIII]\,\lambda 4363$, $n_e$ from $[SII]\,\lambda 6716/6731$, $A_V$ from Balmer decrement.
7. **object recognition**: given a spectrum, identify the object class and explain what features point to that conclusion.
