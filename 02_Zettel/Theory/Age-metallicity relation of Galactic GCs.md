layout: default
name: Age-metallicity relation of Galactic GCs
description: Dotter et al 2011 finding of a tight inner-halo age-Fe/H track plus a younger scattered outer-halo branch as evidence for in situ versus accreted GC populations
---

dotter, sarajedini & anderson (2011, ApJ 738, 74) used HST/ACS photometry of galactic globular clusters in a uniform photometric system and fitted isochrones (dartmouth) to derive **homogeneous ages and metallicities** for $\sim 60$ clusters. when they plotted age versus $[\text{Fe/H}]$ they found that the cluster system splits naturally into two groups:

1. **inner-halo / disk GCs**: galactocentric distances $R_{GC} \lesssim 10$-$15$ kpc. these follow a **tight, narrow age-metallicity track**: roughly constant old age $\sim 12.5$-$13$ Gyr at $[\text{Fe/H}] \lesssim -1.5$, with a slight downward slope so that more metal-rich clusters are mildly younger. the small scatter ($\sigma_t \lesssim 0.5$ Gyr at fixed metallicity) suggests these clusters formed during a coherent rapid-enrichment episode in the early milky way potential, **in situ**.

2. **outer-halo GCs**: $R_{GC} \gtrsim 15$ kpc. these are systematically **younger by 1-2 Gyr at fixed $[\text{Fe/H}]$** and show **much larger scatter**, both in age and in metallicity. several of them are kinematically associated with the sagittarius stream, the gaia-enceladus debris, or other tidal substructure. the natural interpretation is that these GCs formed in **smaller progenitor galaxies (dwarfs) with their own slower, less efficient star formation histories** and were later accreted by the milky way (see [Halo accretion from dwarf galaxies](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Halo accretion from dwarf galaxies.md)).

the two-branch structure therefore maps onto the searle-zinn 1978 dichotomy and onto the modern galactic-archaeology view that the stellar halo is a hybrid of in-situ formation and accretion. it predates dotter's analysis (forbes & bridges 2010 found similar bimodality using literature ages) but dotter et al. nailed it down with homogeneous photometry, removing the systematic age offsets that plague heterogeneous compilations.

a few details that matter pedagogically:

- the **tight relation** in the inner branch is itself a clock: the slope is consistent with rapid chemical evolution where each $\sim 0.1$ Gyr of star formation increases $[\text{Fe/H}]$ by $\sim 0.1$-$0.2$ dex. this is steep, characteristic of a deep gravitational potential where supernovae cannot escape and metals are retained.
- the **outer branch's larger scatter** is not just observational error: it reflects the diversity of accreted progenitors, each with its own AMR.
- the two branches **overlap in metallicity** but separate in age, so $[\text{Fe/H}]$ alone does not classify a GC. you need both age and either kinematics or position.
- the absolute age scale depends on isochrone choice (dartmouth, victoria-regina, BaSTI all differ by a few hundred Myr) and on adopted distance scales. **relative** ages between clusters are more robust than absolute.

leaman, vandenberg & mendel (2013) extended this picture by adding spectroscopic [Fe/H] and showed that the in-situ branch is consistent with the bulge formation track, while the accreted branch resembles the AMR of the LMC and other dwarfs.

the age-metallicity relation of GCs is therefore one of the cleanest pieces of evidence for the dual origin of the galactic halo, and one of the main reasons galactic archaeology takes globular clusters as fossil tracers.
