**Hubble's law**: in the local universe, galaxies recede from us with velocity proportional to their distance. the foundational observational fact that the universe is expanding.

## the law

$$\boxed{\, \vec v = H_0\,\vec d \,}$$

with $H_0$ the Hubble constant, $\sim 67$ to $73$ km/s/Mpc.

at low $z$ the recession velocity relates to redshift by $v \approx cz$, so:
$$d \approx \frac{cz}{H_0}$$
the **Hubble distance**.

## the discovery

historical:
- **1929**: Edwin Hubble plots distances (from Cepheids) vs redshifts (from spectra) for $\sim 24$ galaxies. finds linear relation. publishes $v = H_0 d$ with $H_0 \approx 500$ km/s/Mpc (off by factor $7$ due to early calibration errors).
- **Lemaître 1927**: derives the same relation from GR + observations independently, published in French. essentially priority claim.
- **subsequent refinement**: distance ladder gradually pushes $H_0$ down to current $\sim 70$ km/s/Mpc.

## what the law means

a key insight: in an expanding universe described by FLRW, every observer sees the same Hubble flow regardless of position. the universe doesn't have a centre; everywhere expands away from everywhere else.

a useful analogy: dots painted on a balloon. as the balloon inflates, every dot sees neighbours receding, with velocity proportional to current distance. this is the **expansion of space**, not the motion of galaxies through space.

## the Hubble parameter

$H_0$ is the **current** value of the Hubble parameter:
$$H(t) \equiv \dot a(t)/a(t)$$

at other times, $H(t)$ has different values, set by the Friedmann equation:
$$H(z)^2 = H_0^2[\Omega_m(1+z)^3 + \Omega_r(1+z)^4 + \Omega_\Lambda + \Omega_k(1+z)^2]$$

so $H$ was much higher in the past (radiation- + matter-dominated eras) and approaches a constant in the future (dark-energy era).

## the Hubble time, length, volume

derived scales:
- **Hubble time** $t_H = 1/H_0 \approx 14$ Gyr. rough age of the universe.
- **Hubble length** $d_H = c/H_0 \approx 4300$ Mpc. rough size of the observable universe.
- **Hubble volume** $V_H = (4\pi/3)d_H^3 \approx 3.4 \times 10^{11}$ Mpc$^3$.

these are not exact; the universe is older than $1/H_0$ in $\Lambda$CDM (by factor $\sim 1.4$), and the particle horizon is larger than $d_H$.

## the Hubble tension

current state:
- **local ladder** (parallax → Cepheids → SN Ia): $H_0 = 73.04 \pm 1.04$ km/s/Mpc (Riess 2022).
- **CMB-anchored** (Planck $\Lambda$CDM): $H_0 = 67.4 \pm 0.5$ km/s/Mpc.

these disagree at $\sim 5\sigma$. the **Hubble tension** is one of the major open problems in cosmology. either:
- the local ladder has unidentified systematics (Cepheid metallicity, dust, blending).
- the CMB-anchored value misses new physics (early dark energy, $N_{\rm eff}$, non-standard recombination).

JWST + future GW standard sirens will help resolve.
