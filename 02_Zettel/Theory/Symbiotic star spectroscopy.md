title: "Symbiotic star spectroscopy"
---

a **symbiotic star** is a binary system containing a cool red giant + a hot accreting white dwarf. its composite spectrum mixes M-giant absorption features (TiO bands, Na I D, Ca II) with hot WD emission (He II, $[OIII]$, $[NeV]$, broad H emission). a useful natural laboratory for accretion physics.

## the system

components:
- **cool component**: M giant or early-AGB star, $T \sim 3000$ to $4000$ K, contributes the optical-NIR continuum and TiO bands.
- **hot component**: hot WD, $T \sim 10^5$ K, accretes from the giant via wind or Roche-lobe overflow. ionises a portion of the giant's wind.

orbital periods $\sim 1$ to $10$ years, separations $\sim 1$ to $10$ AU.

## the composite spectrum

at quiescence, three emission components combine:
1. **cool continuum**: M-giant photosphere, with TiO bands.
2. **hot continuum**: WD photosphere, blue-rising into UV.
3. **emission line spectrum**: from the photoionised wind nebula around the WD.

emission features:
- **Balmer lines**: very strong (broad and narrow components).
- **He II $\lambda 4686$**: signature of $> 54$ eV photons, only the hot WD provides them.
- **$[OIII]\,\lambda 4959, 5007$**: nebular, indicating low-density ionised gas.
- **$[NeIII], [NeV]$**: harder ionisation in some symbiotics.

see CI Cyg in `intro_spec.pdf`: the spectrum shows both M-giant absorption (especially in the red) and very strong nebular emission lines.

## subtypes

- **stellar (S-type)**: M-giant continuum and emission lines, no dust.
- **dust (D-type)**: heavy dust emission in IR (Mira-variable cool component, longer-period orbits).

## outbursts

some symbiotics show occasional outbursts:
- **Z And type**: optical brightening by 2-3 mag over months to years. probably accretion-rate variations.
- **R Aqr type**: jet-driven outbursts.
- **recurrent novae** (e.g. RS Oph): nuclear runaways, similar to classical novae but recurring on $\sim$ decades.

these outbursts show characteristic spectral changes, similar to nova evolution but with the cool giant continuum always present.

## as accretion-disk laboratories

symbiotics offer an unusual window into:
- **wind accretion** vs Roche-lobe overflow.
- **photoionisation** by hot accretion sources.
- **nucleosynthesis** in the burning shell on the WD.
- **type Ia SN progenitors** (single-degenerate channel).
