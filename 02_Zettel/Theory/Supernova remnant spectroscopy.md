a **supernova remnant (SNR)** is the expanding shell of supernova ejecta + swept-up ISM. spectra evolve over $\sim 10^5$ years from optically thick (early) to optically thin (late). most observed SNRs are in the **adiabatic / radiative shell phase** with rich emission-line spectra dominated by shocks.

## evolutionary phases

### free expansion (years 0 to a few hundred)
ejecta freely expand into low-density ISM. radio synchrotron from cosmic-ray accelerated electrons. optical emission faint until enough ISM is swept up.

### adiabatic / Sedov phase ($\sim 10^4$ years)
ejecta sweep up enough ISM to slow down. shocks heat the gas to $\sim 10^7$ K. emit X-ray free-free + lines. **Cas A, Tycho, Kepler**: prototypes.

### radiative shell phase ($\sim 10^5$ years)
shocks have cooled to $\sim 10^4$ to $10^6$ K. cool dense shell forms. **bright optical forbidden lines** dominate. **Crab Nebula (M1)**, **Cygnus Loop**, **Vela**: prototypes of the radiative phase.

### dispersal ($> 10^5$ years)
remnant fades into ISM. sometimes traceable as soft X-ray cavities or HI shells.

## the optical spectrum (radiative phase)

key features:
- **strong $[OIII]\,\lambda 5007$**: from $\sim 10^5$ K shocks ionising O.
- **$[SII]\,\lambda 6716, 6731$ very strong**: characteristic high $[SII]/H\alpha$ ratio distinguishes shocks from photoionisation.
- **$[OI]\,\lambda 6300$ also strong**: from the cooling postshock region.
- **$[OII]\,\lambda 3727$**, $[NII]\,\lambda 6548, 6584$**.
- **broad H$\alpha, H\beta$**: width $\sim 100$ km/s (dispersion) to $\sim 10^3$ km/s (Doppler from expansion).

example: **Crab Nebula M1** in `intro_spec.pdf`. the spectrum shows:
- prominent $[OIII]$.
- bright $[SII]$ doublet.
- moderate H$\alpha$.
- the underlying synchrotron continuum from the pulsar wind nebula.

## distinguishing SNR from HII region

a key practical question: is a given emission nebula a SNR (shock) or HII region (photoionised)?

**diagnostic**: $[SII]/H\alpha$ ratio.
- HII regions: $[SII]/H\alpha \lesssim 0.4$ (shock-poor, photoionisation-dominated).
- SNRs: $[SII]/H\alpha \gtrsim 0.4$ (shock-dominated, cooling region).

physical reason: shocks heat gas to $T \sim 10^5$ to $10^6$ K, then it cools through the $[SII]$-emitting layer at $\sim 10^4$ K. photoionised gas at $\sim 10^4$ K equilibrium has different forbidden-line emissivity ratios.

other shock signatures:
- **broad H$\alpha$** in fast SNRs.
- **$[OIII]/H\beta$** elevated in shock-heated gas.
- **X-ray emission** from $T \sim 10^7$ K post-shock.

## young SNR cases (Cas A, Tycho)

ejecta still optically dominant. spectra show **abundance signatures of nucleosynthesis**:
- O-rich knots in Cas A: $[OIII]$, [OIII]$, $[OII]$ very strong, no H or He.
- Si, S, Ar from intermediate-mass elements.
- Fe from $^{56}$Ni decay products.

modern X-ray + optical mapping reveals the spatial structure of the explosion and the progenitor type.

## the Crab Nebula, M1: the special case

a young SNR (937 yr old, observed in 1054 AD) with a **central pulsar** (PSR B0531+21). the Crab Nebula's emission has two components:
- **synchrotron**: powered by the pulsar wind, dominates from radio to gamma-ray.
- **filaments**: shocked thermal plasma at $T \sim 10^4$ to $10^5$ K. Balmer + forbidden lines.

the Crab is **the** prototype pulsar wind nebula, key calibrator for high-energy astronomy.
