layout: default
name: Halo accretion from dwarf galaxies
description: tidal streams and chemo-kinematic substructure showing that a large fraction of halo stars and outer halo GCs originate in disrupted dwarf galaxies
---

the milky way stellar halo is not a single coherent population. gaia astrometry combined with spectroscopic surveys (APOGEE, GALAH, H3) has resolved it into a patchwork of substructures, most of which are debris from disrupted dwarf galaxies. galactic archaeology now treats the halo as an archaeological site: dig in the right place and you find a different progenitor.

**the major debris**:

- **sagittarius dwarf stream**: discovered by ibata, gilmore & irwin 1994. wraps the sky in long leading and trailing arms and is being torn apart in real time. carries a population of GCs (M54, terzan 7/8, arp 2, pal 12, whiting 1, possibly NGC 2419) and a metallicity gradient indicating a once-massive progenitor, $M_\star \sim 10^9 M_\odot$.
- **gaia-enceladus / sausage**: identified by belokurov et al. 2018 and helmi et al. 2018 using gaia DR2. a major merger $\sim 8$-$10$ Gyr ago that contributed perhaps half of the local stellar halo and $\sim 20\%$ of the GC system. the kinematic signature is a population of stars with strongly radial orbits and characteristic $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$ vs $[\text{Fe/H}]$ track different from the in-situ halo.
- **sequoia**: a smaller, retrograde merger identified by myeong et al. 2019. distinct chemistry, brought in some retrograde GCs.
- **helmi streams**: helmi et al. 1999, an old kinematic substructure with characteristic angular momentum, contributes a small set of halo stars and a couple of clusters.
- **kraken / heracles** and other proposed early mergers: kruijssen et al. 2019, horta et al. 2021, harder to confirm because their debris is dispersed.

**outer-halo GCs as accreted**: many GCs at $R_{GC} \gtrsim 15$-$20$ kpc are kinematically and chemically associated with one of these progenitors. the outer-halo cluster system is therefore not a single coeval family but a mixture of accretion remnants. this is one of the key pieces of evidence for the searle-zinn 1978 picture (against the eggen-lynden-bell-sandage 1962 monolithic-collapse picture).

**field stars**: the in-situ halo (sometimes called the splash, or the heated thick disk) has higher $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$ at fixed $[\text{Fe/H}]$ than gaia-enceladus debris, because the milky way's deep potential retained metals and enriched faster. the alpha-knee position is therefore a chemical fingerprint of the progenitor's mass: massive systems have knees at high $[\text{Fe/H}]$, dwarfs have knees at low $[\text{Fe/H}]$.

**chemical clocks**: $[\text{Eu/Fe}]$, $[\text{Mg/Mn}]$, $[\text{Al/Fe}]$, and $[\text{Na/Fe}]$ are useful discriminants. accreted populations from low-mass dwarfs tend to have low $[\text{Al/Fe}]$ and low $[\text{Mg/Mn}]$ compared to in-situ halo at the same $[\text{Fe/H}]$. this is the basis of "chemical tagging" of accreted stars.

**connection to ultra-faint dwarfs**: the smallest surviving satellites (see [Ultra-faint dwarf galaxies definition](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Ultra-faint dwarf galaxies definition.md)) are likely analogues of the building blocks that formed the very early halo. they carry CEMP-no stars and r-process-rich populations that are also seen in some halo field stars, supporting the link.

the broad picture: in $\Lambda$CDM the milky way grew hierarchically, swallowing dozens of dwarfs of various masses across cosmic time. modern data finally resolve the individual contributors. about $\sim 30$-$50\%$ of the stellar halo (by mass) is accreted, with the dominant single contributor being gaia-enceladus.
