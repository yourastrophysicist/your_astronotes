name: Splitting of the upper MS in young clusters
description: bifurcation of the upper main sequence into blue and red branches in young (~100 Myr) LMC clusters NGC 1755, NGC 1850, NGC 1856, attributed to slow + fast rotators
---

in young ($\sim 50$-$300$ Myr) LMC clusters, deep HST photometry has revealed that the **upper main sequence is bifurcated** into a blue + a red branch separated by $\sim 0.05$-$0.10$ mag in colour. discovered by Milone et al. 2015, 2018 in NGC 1755, NGC 1850, NGC 1856 + others. this is one of the cleanest pieces of evidence that [stellar rotation](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Stellar rotation effects on CMD.html) dominates the [eMSTO](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Extended main sequence turn-off eMSTO.html) phenomenon.

## the observation

at $M_{\rm F814W} \sim -2$ to $+1$ (i.e., $M_* \sim 3$-$10\,M_\odot$ on the upper MS), the CMD shows two parallel sequences:

- **blue branch**: $\sim 30\%$ of upper-MS stars; colour $\sim 0.05$ mag bluer at fixed magnitude;
- **red branch**: $\sim 70\%$ of upper-MS stars; redder + slightly brighter.

below this MS region, the bifurcation merges and the MS is single-valued. the bifurcation appears specifically at the masses where rotation effects on stellar structure are strongest ($M \gtrsim 2 M_\odot$, no convective envelope to magnetically brake rotation).

## the rotation interpretation

the dominant model (Milone et al. 2018, Bastian + de Mink 2009 framework):

- **blue branch** $\equiv$ slow rotators ($v/v_{\rm crit} \lesssim 0.3$) or merger products.
- **red branch** $\equiv$ fast rotators ($v/v_{\rm crit} \sim 0.5$-$0.9$) showing gravity-darkened equators + extended atmospheric oblateness.

the colour split arises from the inclination-averaged effect of gravity darkening + rotational mixing. quantitative models (Brandt & Huang 2015) reproduce the observed split with reasonable rotation distributions.

## spectroscopic confirmation

**Marino et al. 2018, AJ 156, 116** carried out VLT-MUSE multi-object spectroscopy of NGC 1866 stars on the two MS branches. they directly measured $v\sin i$ for $\sim 50$ stars on each branch:

- blue branch: median $v\sin i \approx 50$ km/s (slow rotators);
- red branch: median $v\sin i \approx 200$ km/s (fast rotators).

this is a direct, unambiguous confirmation that the photometric split corresponds to two physically distinct rotation populations.

## why bimodal? open question

the rotation distribution in young clusters is not Gaussian, it is bimodal. why? proposed mechanisms:

1. **early disc locking**: pre-MS stars whose accretion discs lock them to the disc rotation rate end up rotating slower; without disc locking, they spin up via contraction (Sun et al. 2019).
2. **binary mass transfer + mergers**: mass-gainers in binaries can be spun up to near-critical rotation, populating the fast-rotator branch (de Mink et al. 2013).
3. **stellar collisions in dense cluster cores**: produce slow-rotating merger products (D'Antona et al. 2015).

it is plausible that NO single mechanism dominates: the bimodal distribution may emerge from the combination of disc-locking + binary evolution + dense-environment dynamics.

## why this is important

the split upper MS is THE cleanest observational signature that rotation matters for cluster CMD morphology. it:
- decisively rules out a pure age-spread interpretation of the [eMSTO](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Extended main sequence turn-off eMSTO.html);
- provides a direct empirical handle on the rotation distribution at cluster formation;
- connects to the He-variation argument for old GC chromosome maps (D'Antona et al. 2015).

## the connection to old GCs

old GCs ($> 5$ Gyr) do NOT show split upper MS: stars on the MS today are $M < 0.85\,M_\odot$ which have been magnetically braked + slow-rotating. but the He variations + chemical anomalies in old GCs may be the **fossil residue** of an earlier rotation-driven phase: when the GC was 100 Myr old, it likely had a split upper MS too; the rotation has since been braked away, but the He enrichment + chemical variations persist.

if true, eMSTO clusters today + old GC chromosome maps are the same phenomenon at different epochs. this is the essence of the [eMSTO and multiple populations connection](../../02_Zettel/Theory/eMSTO and multiple populations connection.html).
