# quenching and passive galaxies at high z

up: [Observational_Cosmology_MOC](../../00_Atlas/Observational_Cosmology_MOC.md) · [Astrophysics_of_Galaxies_MOC](../../00_Atlas/Astrophysics_of_Galaxies_MOC.md) · [Green valley and quenching tracks](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Green valley and quenching tracks.md)

## the definition of quenching

Quenching is the physical process by which a star-forming galaxy ceases star formation permanently or quasi-permanently, transitioning from the **star-forming main sequence** (SFMS, where $\text{sSFR} \equiv \text{SFR}/M_* \sim 10^{-9} - 10^{-8} \text{ yr}^{-1}$) to the **quiescent sequence** ($\text{sSFR} < 10^{-11} \text{ yr}^{-1}$).

## primary quenching mechanisms

1. **halo mass quenching (virial shock heating)**:
   - When a dark matter halo exceeds a critical mass $M_{\rm shock} \approx 10^{12} M_\odot$ (Dekel & Birnboim 2006), infalling gas is shock-heated at the virial radius to the virial temperature:
     $$T_{\rm vir} = \frac{\mu m_p G M_{\rm halo}}{2 k_B R_{\rm vir}} \sim 10^6 \text{ K}$$
   - The cooling timescale becomes longer than the gravitational dynamical timescale ($\tau_{\rm cool} > \tau_{\rm dyn}$), terminating cold gas accretion onto the central galaxy.
2. **AGN feedback**:
   - *ejective / quasar mode*: radiatively driven nuclear winds during peak accretion blow cold gas out of the galaxy potential well.
   - *maintenance / radio mode*: relativistic radio jets inject mechanical energy into the circumgalactic medium, offsetting gas cooling.
3. **morphological quenching** (Martig et al. 2009):
   - Growth of a massive central stellar spheroid stabilizes the gaseous disk against gravitational fragmentation (raising the Toomre $Q > 1$ parameter) without requiring all gas to be ejected.
4. **environmental quenching**:
   - In dense cluster environments: ram-pressure stripping, strangulation (removal of the warm gas reservoir), and tidal harassment.

## jwst discoveries of massive quiescent galaxies at z > 3-5

Recent JWST observations have discovered fully quenched, massive ($M_* \gtrsim 10^{10.5} M_\odot$) galaxies at early cosmic epochs:
- **ZF-COSMOS-20115** ($z = 3.717$) and **GS-9209** ($z = 4.658$, Carnall et al. 2023): compact, massive quiescent galaxies observed when the universe was only $\sim 1.25$ Gyr old.
- **RUBIES-EGS-QG-1** ($z = 4.9$): completely passive spectrum with prominent Balmer absorption breaks ($D_n 4000$) and negligible ongoing star formation.
- **implication**: these systems must have assembled their stellar mass in an extreme starburst (${\rm SFR} \sim 1000 M_\odot/{\rm yr}$) at $z \sim 8 - 10$ and quenched within $\lesssim 200$ Myr, placing tight constraints on the speed of early feedback mechanisms.
