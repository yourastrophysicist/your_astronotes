title: "Sectional and Gaussian curvature"

**sectional curvature** is a scalar number that gives the curvature of a 2D plane embedded in a higher-dimensional manifold. **Gaussian curvature** is the special case of sectional curvature in 2D. the geometric content of "how curved" a manifold is.

## Gaussian curvature (2D only)

for a 2D manifold with metric $g_{ij}$, the Gaussian curvature is a scalar $K$ at each point. for a sphere of radius $R$:
$$K = \frac{1}{R^2}$$

for a plane: $K = 0$. for a saddle / hyperbolic surface: $K < 0$.

related to Riemann via:
$$R_{\theta\phi\theta\phi} = K \cdot \det(g_{\rm 2D})$$

so $K$ is just the single Riemann component in 2D.

## the **Theorema Egregium** (Gauss 1828)

Gauss's "remarkable theorem": the Gaussian curvature of a 2D surface depends **only on the intrinsic metric**, not on how the surface is embedded in higher dimensions.

practical consequence: a flat sheet of paper has $K = 0$. you can roll it into a cylinder ($K = 0$ still!) without distorting distances. but you **cannot** map a sphere to a plane without distortion: any flat map of the Earth distorts something.

## sectional curvature (general dimension)

at a point $p$, choose two linearly independent tangent vectors $u, v$. the **sectional curvature** of the 2-plane $\{u, v\}$ is:
$$K(u, v) = \frac{R(u, v, v, u)}{g(u, u)g(v, v) - g(u, v)^2}$$
(numerator is Riemann with all indices lowered, evaluated on $u, v$; denominator is the area squared of the parallelogram spanned by $u, v$).

so sectional curvature is a function on the 2-Grassmannian of tangent planes at $p$. one number per plane.

equivalent: $K(u, v) = $ Gaussian curvature of the 2D **sub-manifold** generated by exponentiating the plane $\{u, v\}$ from $p$.

## examples in 4D

### Schwarzschild
sectional curvatures vary from $\sim GM/r^3$ at large $r$ to large values near $r = 2GM$. the Kretschmann scalar $R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = 48 G^2 M^2/r^6$ is a basis-independent measure that diverges at $r = 0$ (true singularity).

### de Sitter space (constant positive curvature)
all sectional curvatures equal: $K = \Lambda/3$ (positive). this is a maximally symmetric spacetime.

### Anti-de Sitter (constant negative curvature)
all sectional curvatures equal: $K = -|\Lambda|/3$ (negative). holography lives here.

## relation to Ricci and Einstein equations

if all sectional curvatures are equal at a point: $K(u, v) = K_p$ for all $u, v$. then the manifold is **locally maximally symmetric** there. the Ricci tensor is proportional to the metric: $R_{\mu\nu} = (n-1)K\,g_{\mu\nu}$ (with $n$ the dimension).

so constant sectional curvature ↔ Einstein space (Ricci proportional to metric) ↔ vacuum solutions with cosmological constant.

## why this matters for GR

the sectional / Gaussian curvature is the **most physically transparent** measure of curvature. tells you, in geometric terms, how curved 2D slices of spacetime are.

for the universe, the **spatial 3-curvature** is parameterised by $k = -1, 0, +1$, and the local sectional curvatures are $\sim K = k/a^2$. directly observable via the angular size of the CMB acoustic peaks ($\Omega_k$).
