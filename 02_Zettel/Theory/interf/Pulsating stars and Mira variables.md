# Pulsating stars and Mira variables

interferometric imaging directly resolves the *pulsating* surface of variable stars. for Mira variables (long-period red giant pulsators) and Cepheids, interferometry gives the diameter as a function of pulsation phase, testing pulsation theory and contributing to distance scales.

## Mira variables

Mira-type stars: cool, asymptotic-giant-branch (AGB) stars with periods of 100-1000 days, pulsation amplitudes of 5-9 magnitudes in V-band. dramatic surface activity:
- pulsation drives mass loss
- surrounding dust shells form and dissipate
- molecular layers (H2O, TiO) observed at IR

interferometry directly measures the *photospheric diameter* and the *surrounding dust/molecular shell* at multiple wavelengths and phases.

### example: omicron Cet (Mira itself)

VLTI/MIDI and AMBER observations:
- photosphere diameter: 30 mas (at 8 μm)
- dust shell: 50-80 mas (extending to several stellar radii)
- variability over the 332-day pulsation cycle

molecular and dust shells reveal the physics of mass loss in AGB stars.

## Cepheid variables

classical Cepheids: hot, intermediate-mass yellow supergiants with periods of 1-100 days, pulsation amplitudes of 0.5-1.5 mag in V-band. *the* standard candles for the cosmological distance ladder.

interferometric measurement of:
- mean angular diameter
- pulsation amplitude (variation with phase)
- limb darkening as a function of phase

provides:
- absolute diameter via parallax
- physical diameter from interferometric angular + parallax distance
- *surface brightness* test of standard distance methods

CHARA has measured Cepheid diameters at $\sim 1\%$ precision, enabling cross-checks of the period-luminosity relationship.

## RR Lyrae stars

shorter-period (0.2-1 day) lower-amplitude pulsators. older stellar population, used as standard candles in globular clusters.

CHARA observations: diameters and pulsation amplitudes for the brightest RR Lyrae stars. confirms theoretical predictions.

## the pulsation theory tests

interferometric data tests:

### 1. pulsation amplitude

predicted from convective-pulsation models. interferometric measurement of $\Delta R/R$ during pulsation tests these models directly.

### 2. limb darkening as function of phase

during the pulsation cycle, $T_{\rm eff}$ changes. limb darkening should change accordingly. interferometric measurements have detected these phase-dependent changes.

### 3. p-mode vs g-mode pulsations

different pulsation modes have different surface velocity patterns. interferometric Doppler imaging (combining interferometric + spectroscopic data) maps these modes.

## the science yield

what stellar pulsation interferometry has produced:
- **calibrated period-luminosity relation** for Cepheids (key to the cosmic distance ladder)
- **direct measurement** of mass loss in AGB stars
- **detection of granulation patterns** on red supergiants
- **constraints on internal structure** via pulsation-mode identification

these results feed into:
- cosmological distance scales (Cepheid → SN Ia → Hubble)
- stellar evolution models (mass loss in late stages)
- chemical evolution (where heavy elements come from)

## the practical observing strategy

observe a pulsating star at:
- multiple pulsation phases (sample 4-6 points across the cycle)
- multiple wavelengths (probe different atmospheric layers)
- multiple baselines (constrain diameter and shape)

a typical Mira observation: 4-8 phases × 2 wavelengths × 5 baselines = 40+ measurements per phase. modeling extracts the time-evolving photosphere.
