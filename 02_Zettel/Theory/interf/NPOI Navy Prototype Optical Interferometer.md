# NPOI Navy Prototype Optical Interferometer

a U.S. Navy / USNO interferometer at Anderson Mesa, Arizona. designed for **astrometry** (precise position measurement of bright stars) and **imaging**. operated since 1996 with multiple telescope upgrades.

## the architecture

- **6 + 4 telescopes** (mixed: 6 imaging dishes 0.5 m, plus astrometric stations)
- baselines: ~40 m to 79 m (current); planned upgrades to 437 m
- wavelength: visible (R, V bands)
- delay lines: ~50 m vacuum tunnels
- beam combiner: pairwise; recently upgraded to multi-beam

## the dual mission

### astrometry

NPOI's primary mission is precision astrometry: measuring the *positions* of bright stars to ~mas precision, contributing to fundamental reference frames (the catalog used to define celestial coordinates).

key contribution: **astrometric calibration** of the *Hipparcos* and *Gaia* missions, anchoring the optical reference frame to the radio (VLBI) reference frame.

### imaging

secondary mission: imaging bright stars (binaries, surfaces). less competitive with CHARA/VLTI for cutting-edge imaging, but solid for routine science.

## the science

- **astrometric measurements** of binary stars: dynamical masses for hundreds of stars
- **stellar diameters**: complementing CHARA at visible wavelengths
- **monitoring of bright stars** for variability and binary discovery
- **standards for the celestial reference frame**: maintained jointly with VLBI

## the niche

NPOI's niche is *operational consistency* — running for decades, accumulating observation logs that are useful for:
- proper motion studies
- long-baseline binary orbit characterization
- variability monitoring of bright stars

it is not the highest-resolution or highest-sensitivity instrument, but it has been operating *continuously* longer than CHARA or modern VLTI, building a long time-baseline of consistent measurements.

## the future: NPOI extensions

planned upgrades:
- baselines to 437 m (longer than CHARA)
- additional imaging telescopes
- IR capability

these would push NPOI back toward the cutting edge of imaging.
