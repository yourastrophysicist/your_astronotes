# AGN and supermassive black holes

interferometric imaging of active galactic nuclei (AGN) at the highest possible angular resolution. **EHT 2019 image of M87** is the canonical example: direct observation of the photon ring around a supermassive black hole. modern interferometry probes:
- the black hole's photon sphere and shadow
- the broad-line region (gas at $\sim 100$ light-days from the BH)
- the central jet base
- accretion disk dynamics

## the typical AGN structure

an AGN consists of (at small to large scale):
- the central SMBH ($10^6$-$10^{10}$ $M_\odot$)
- the **accretion disk** (~10s to 1000s of gravitational radii)
- the **broad-line region (BLR)**: clumpy gas at $\sim 100$-1000 light-days, illuminated by the central UV source
- the **dusty torus** at $\sim 0.1$-100 pc
- the **jet** (relativistic outflow at $\sim$ pc to kpc scales)

interferometric resolution corresponds to:
- VLA at 21 cm: ~1" (kpc scales)
- VLBI at 21 cm: ~1 mas (~10 pc at $z=0.05$)
- EHT at 1.3 mm: ~25 μas (~1000 gravitational radii at $z=0.05$)
- GRAVITY (NIR): ~3 mas (~10 light-days at $z=0.05$)

each technique probes a different physical region.

## the M87 image

EHT 2019 published the first image of M87's central black hole shadow:
- **bright ring** of size ~42 μas
- **dark center** = the photon sphere
- **asymmetry** (south side brighter) = relativistic Doppler boosting by accretion-flow motion

masses inferred from:
- ring size + GR formulas: $M_{\rm BH} = 6.5 \times 10^9 M_\odot$
- consistent with prior gas-dynamical mass measurements
- jet base ~5 R_g away from the BH

## the Sgr A* image

EHT 2022 published the first image of Sgr A* itself:
- **bright ring** of size ~50 μas (smaller than M87 because Sgr A* is closer)
- **dark center** also from the photon sphere
- mass $M_{\rm BH} = 4.3 \times 10^6 M_\odot$, consistent with [GRAVITY S-star](../../../02_Zettel/Theory/interf/Galactic Center Sgr A and S-stars.html) measurements

both M87 and Sgr A* images confirm GR predictions for the photon ring at $\sim 1$ percent precision in linear scale.

## the BLR with GRAVITY

GRAVITY+ has resolved the **broad-line region** (BLR) of nearby type-I AGN:
- detection of velocity-resolved Brackett-gamma emission, mapping BLR clouds at $\sim 100$ light-days from the BH
- direct measurement of BLR sizes vs. continuum luminosity (the BLR R-L relation)
- independent calibration of the M-σ relation between BH mass and host-galaxy velocity dispersion

flagship: **3C 273** observed at sub-mas precision, confirming the standard reverberation-mapping results.

## the AGN jet base

VLBI imaging of AGN jets at mm/sub-mm:
- **M87 jet** imaged from base outward at $\sim 1$ mas resolution
- jet collimation transitions from parabolic (close to BH) to conical (at parsec scales)
- relativistic motion ($v/c$ as high as 0.95) inferred from observed jet patterns

these probe the jet-launching mechanism (electromagnetic vs hydrodynamic origin).

## the science

AGN interferometry probes:

### 1. black hole physics

- direct event-horizon-scale imaging
- tests of GR in the strong-field regime
- spin-direction estimates via jet orientation

### 2. accretion disk physics

- velocity field of the inner disk (~Keplerian)
- viscosity and angular-momentum transport
- dust sublimation radius (with NIR interferometry)

### 3. cosmological evolution

- BH-host coevolution via M-σ relation
- AGN feedback effects on host galaxies
- cosmological evolution of merger-driven feeding

### 4. quasar standard candles

precise BLR sizes calibrate the R-L relation, enabling AGN as standard candles for cosmology (an alternative to SN Ia for $H_0$ measurements).

## the future

planned:
- **ngEHT** (next-generation EHT, 2030s): more stations, higher frequency, time-resolved imaging of accretion flows
- **SKA + ngVLA**: deep AGN surveys at high angular resolution
- **VLTI/GRAVITY+ extension**: BLR studies of fainter AGN
- **GRAVITY+ + ELT extreme AO**: combined observations to fully resolve AGN central regions
