name: Pop III nucleosynthesis signatures
description: characteristic abundance patterns left by Pop III progenitors (CEMP-no, r-process II, even-Z patterns from pair-instability SNe), used to fingerprint single zero-metallicity progenitor events in the metal-poor halo + UFDGs
---

since [Pop III stars](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Population III stars.md) cannot be observed directly today, their nucleosynthesis is studied via the **abundance patterns** they imprinted on the next-generation gas. these patterns persist in the most metal-poor stars in the Galactic halo + ultra-faint dwarf galaxies, providing chemical fossils of single Pop III progenitors.

## the canonical Pop III progenitor types

| progenitor type | $M_{\rm Pop III}$ | nucleosynthesis | observable signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| faint SN (mixing+fallback) | $20$-$50\,M_\odot$ | partial fallback of inner ejecta | high [C/Fe], [N/Fe], [O/Fe]; CEMP-no |
| pair-instability SN (PISN) | $140$-$260\,M_\odot$ | complete disruption | strong even-Z preference, large mass |
| neutron-star merger / MR-SN | from binary | r-process | extreme [Eu/Fe], r-II pattern |
| direct-collapse BH | $> 260\,M_\odot$ | no ejecta | none |

each leaves a chemical fingerprint distinguishable by high-resolution spectroscopy.

## CEMP-no stars

the **carbon-enhanced metal-poor (CEMP) class without s-process enhancement** is the largest category of Pop III chemical fossils. defining cuts:

- $[{\rm C/Fe}] > +0.7$,
- $[{\rm Ba/Fe}] < 0$ (excludes AGB s-process pollution),
- usually $[{\rm Fe/H}] < -2$.

CEMP-no stars constitute $\sim 20\%$ of stars at $[{\rm Fe/H}] \sim -3$ and rise to $\sim 80\%$ at $[{\rm Fe/H}] < -4$. examples:

- **HE 0107-5240** (Christlieb et al. 2002, Nature 419, 904): $[{\rm Fe/H}] = -5.3$, $[{\rm C/Fe}] = +4.0$ — first ultra-iron-poor giant.
- **HE 1327-2326** (Frebel et al. 2005, Nature 434, 871): $[{\rm Fe/H}] = -5.4$, $[{\rm C/Fe}] = +4.2$.
- **SDSS J1029+1729**, **SDSS J0023+0307** (newer discoveries).

interpretation: progenitor was a $20$-$50\,M_\odot$ Pop III star where mixing during the SN folded surface He + C + N + O into the inner ejecta, then partial fallback retained the iron, leaving high C/Fe in the ejecta that polluted the next-generation cloud.

## r-process II stars

a small fraction of metal-poor halo stars show **extreme r-process enrichment**: $[{\rm Eu/Fe}] > +1$, with the full r-process pattern from Ba to Th. examples:

- **CS 22892-052** (Sneden et al. 1996): $[{\rm Eu/Fe}] = +1.6$ at $[{\rm Fe/H}] = -3$.
- **CS 31082-001** (Hill et al. 2002): used for cosmochronology via Th/U.

these are likely polluted by **rare events**: neutron-star mergers, magneto-rotational SNe, or collapsars producing r-process material in $\geq 10^{-2}\,M_\odot$ quantities per event.

the breakthrough discovery: **Reticulum II UFDG** (Ji et al. 2016, Nature 531, 610) — 7 of 9 stars in a single UFDG show extreme r-process enrichment, consistent with one ancient neutron-star merger having polluted the entire small UFDG halo.

## even-Z elemental pattern (PISN signature?)

pair-instability SNe ($M_{\rm Pop III} \sim 140$-$260\,M_\odot$) destroy the entire star, leaving no remnant. their nucleosynthesis is dominated by even-Z elements (Si, S, Ca, Ar) with deficits of odd-Z elements (Na, Al, K).

predicted PISN signature: $[{\rm Si/Fe}] \sim +0.5$, $[{\rm Ca/Fe}] \sim +0.5$, $[{\rm Na/Fe}] \sim -1$, with a sharp odd-even contrast ratio.

**no clear PISN candidate has yet been found** in halo + UFDG stars. the search continues. interpretations:
- Pop III IMF was bottom-heavier than expected; few or no PISNe occurred;
- PISN ejecta were diluted by other SN ejecta before being incorporated into surviving stars;
- alternatively, the more recent claim of a probable PISN descendant: J1010+2358 (Xing et al. 2023, Nature 618, 712) at $[{\rm Fe/H}] = -2.4$ shows a strong odd-even pattern + light-element deficit consistent with a $> 250\,M_\odot$ progenitor.

## stochastic enrichment

at very low metallicities, the IGM is poorly mixed. each metal-poor star reflects the chemistry of one or a few progenitors, not the average IGM. this is why CEMP-no stars cluster around specific abundance patterns: each pattern is one Pop III progenitor.

statistical studies of the abundance distributions of $[{\rm Fe/H}] < -3$ stars constrain the **Pop III IMF**: the relative frequency of CEMP-no, r-II, and PISN signatures sets the relative proportions of $20$-$50\,M_\odot$, $> 140\,M_\odot$, and other progenitors.

## why this matters

these signatures provide the **only observational handle on the Pop III IMF** until JWST or future telescopes detect Pop III stars directly. they:

- constrain the high-mass cutoff of the Pop III IMF;
- test Pop III SN explosion models;
- measure the rate of rare events (NSMs, MR-SNe) at high redshift;
- inform models of the chemical evolution of the Milky Way + dwarf galaxies.
