# color gradients in ellipticals

up: [Astrophysics_of_Galaxies_MOC](../../00_Atlas/Astrophysics_of_Galaxies_MOC.md) · [Alpha-Fe enhancement](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Alpha-Fe enhancement.md) · [Color bimodality of galaxies](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Color bimodality of galaxies.md)

## the observational fact

Early-type galaxies (ellipticals and S0s) are not monochromatic; their colors vary smoothly with radius:

$$\nabla(B-R) = \frac{\Delta(B-R)}{\Delta\log R} < 0$$

The centers of elliptical galaxies are consistently **redder**, while the outskirts are **bluer**. Typical gradients are $\Delta(U-R)/\Delta\log R \approx -0.2$ mag/dex.

## resolving the age-metallicity degeneracy

A redder color can be caused by either older age or higher metallicity. To resolve this:
- **spectral line indices (lick system)**:
  - Balmer lines ($\text{H}\beta, \text{H}\gamma, \text{H}\delta$) depend primarily on main-sequence turnoff temperature, acting as sensitive **age indicators**.
  - Metal lines ($\text{Mg}_b, \langle\text{Fe}\rangle = (\text{Fe}5270 + \text{Fe}5335)/2$) measure **metallicity**.
- **result**: spectroscopic studies (Peletier et al., Mehlert et al., Kuntschner et al.) show that line-index age is virtually flat across the galaxy ($t \gtrsim 10$ Gyr everywhere), whereas metallicity drops outward:
  $$\frac{\Delta\log Z}{\Delta\log R} \approx -0.25 \pm 0.10$$
  Color gradients in ellipticals are **metallicity gradients**, not age gradients.

## formation implications

- **monolithic collapse** (Larson 1974, Carlberg 1984): gas collapses toward the center while forming stars. Supernova enrichment purifies the inward-flowing gas, creating steep metallicity gradients ($\Delta\log Z / \Delta\log R \sim -0.5$).
- **dissipationless (dry) mergers** (White 1980): collisionless merging of stellar systems mixes stars in phase space via violent relaxation, substantially flattening pre-existing gradients.
- Observed moderate gradients ($-0.2$ to $-0.3$) indicate early dissipative collapse followed by a small number of dry minor/major mergers.
