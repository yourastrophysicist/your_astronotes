# mass-radius and mass-velocity relations

up: [Astrophysics_of_Galaxies_MOC](../../00_Atlas/Astrophysics_of_Galaxies_MOC.html) · Scaling relations in ellipticals · [Tully-Fisher relation](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Tully-Fisher relation.html)

## early-type galaxy scaling relations

Early-type galaxies (ETGs) follow tight scaling relations that link their structural and kinematic properties:

1. **faber-jackson relation** (Faber & Jackson 1976):
   $$L \propto \sigma_0^4$$
   connecting optical luminosity $L$ to central stellar velocity dispersion $\sigma_0$.
2. **kormendy relation** (Kormendy 1977):
   $$\langle \mu_e \rangle = a \log R_e + b$$
   a projection of the fundamental plane linking effective surface brightness to effective radius.
3. **the fundamental plane** (Djorgovski & Davis 1987, Dressler et al. 1987):
   $$\log R_e = \alpha \log \sigma_0 + \beta \langle \mu_e \rangle + \gamma$$
   In physical units: $R_e \propto \sigma_0^{1.24} I_e^{-0.82}$. The deviation from the virial expectation ($R \propto \sigma^2 I^{-1}$) is known as the "tilt" of the Fundamental Plane, driven by variations in stellar $(M/L)$ with mass and non-homology.
4. **mass-size relation for etgs** (Shen et al. 2003):
   $$R_e \propto M_*^{0.56} \quad (M_* > 3 \times 10^{10} M_\odot)$$

## late-type galaxy scaling relations

Late-type galaxies (spirals) are governed by angular momentum and rotational equilibrium:

1. **tully-fisher relation** (Tully & Fisher 1977):
   $$L \propto V_{\rm max}^4$$
   Best measured in NIR ($K$-band) or as the **Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation (BTFR)**:
   $$M_{\rm bar} = M_* + M_{\rm gas} \propto V_{\rm flat}^4$$
   Holding with extremely low intrinsic scatter ($< 0.1$ dex) over 5 orders of magnitude in mass.
2. **mass-size relation for disks**:
   $$R_d \propto M_*^{0.25}$$
   Disks exhibit a much shallower mass-size relation than early-type spheroids.
