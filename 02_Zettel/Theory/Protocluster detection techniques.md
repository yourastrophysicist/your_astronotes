**protoclusters** are the high-redshift progenitors of today's galaxy clusters. caught in the act of assembling at $z = 2$ to $7$, they're the **birth sites** of the most massive structures in the universe. detecting them requires specialised techniques because they're rare + their members are typical galaxies (not dramatically brighter).

## what is a protocluster

a region in the early universe ($z \gtrsim 2$) that **will collapse** into a galaxy cluster ($M \gtrsim 10^{14}\,M_\odot$) by $z = 0$. at high $z$:
- the structure is still **forming**.
- mass is distributed across $\sim 10$ to $30$ Mpc comoving (Chiang et al. 2013).
- contains **overdensities** of typical galaxies, not yet a virialised cluster.

so protoclusters are **diffuse, large, hard to find**, but central to understanding cluster formation.

## detection techniques

### 1. Lyman-alpha emitters (LAEs) overdensities

**narrow-band imaging** at the redshifted Ly-α wavelength reveals galaxies with strong Ly-α emission. count them in narrow $z$-slices. overdensities of LAEs trace protoclusters.

example: Ouchi et al. SSA22 protocluster at $z = 3.1$. imaged in narrow band at $\lambda = 4977$ Å. shows clear LAE overdensity (factor $\sim 3$ above average).

### 2. Lyman-break galaxies (LBGs) overdensities

LBGs are galaxies identified by their Lyman-break dropout in broadband colors. select LBGs in a narrow $z$ slice + look for overdensities.

protoclusters at $z = 3$ to $6$ identified this way. Pérez-González et al. + Overzier reviews.

### 3. IR / sub-mm-selected protoclusters

dusty star-forming galaxies cluster at high $z$. **sub-mm number-density overdensities** in deep ALMA / Herschel maps reveal protoclusters.

example: SPT2349 protocluster at $z = 4.3$, $\sim 14$ massive dusty galaxies in $\sim 100$ kpc, found by SPT.

### 4. radio galaxy / AGN tracers

**high-$z$ radio galaxies (HzRGs)** + **QSOs** sit at the centres of massive overdensities. selecting around them reveals protoclusters.

example: Spider Web Galaxy MRC 1138-262 at $z = 2.16$, surrounded by an overdensity of star-forming + dusty galaxies.

### 5. spectroscopic confirmation

regardless of selection technique, **spectroscopy** confirms membership. measure $z$ for tens of candidate galaxies in the patch + check that they sit in a narrow $\Delta z$ slice.

modern: VIMOS, MOSDEF, KMOS surveys + JWST NIRSpec.

### 6. IFU mapping (JWST era)

JWST + KCWI + MUSE provide IFU spectra of large fields. simultaneously identify $z$ + properties of $\sim 100$s of galaxies per field. accelerated protocluster identification.

## the protocluster reviews

key papers:
- **Overzier 2016** (A&A Rev): comprehensive review of protocluster detection + properties.
- **Chiang et al. 2013, 2017**: simulations + theoretical mass estimates for protoclusters.

## the link to today's clusters

each protocluster will form a present-day rich cluster:
- $z = 2$ protoclusters $\to$ Virgo-Coma-class clusters.
- $z = 3$ protoclusters $\to$ same.
- $z = 6+$ protoclusters $\to$ richest clusters.

abundance + properties of $z \sim 2$ protoclusters constrain $\sigma_8$ + dark-energy equation of state.

## the science

protoclusters reveal:
- **galaxy formation in dense environments** at the peak of cosmic SF.
- **early quenching mechanisms**: ram-pressure stripping, harassment, virial heating.
- **AGN feedback** on protogalaxies.
- **BCG progenitors**: how today's brightest cluster galaxies formed.
- **mass assembly history**: progenitor-descendant matching with simulations.

JWST is currently revolutionising this field with **spectroscopically confirmed protoclusters at $z = 7$ to $10$**.
