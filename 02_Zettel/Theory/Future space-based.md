# Future space-based interferometers

space mission concepts for interferometry without atmospheric limitations. baselines limited only by formation-flying capabilities. designed for direct exoplanet imaging, ultra-high-resolution AGN observations, and other extreme-resolution science.

## the proposed missions

### Darwin (ESA, 2000s, never realized)

a 4-element formation-flying nuller in space. mid-IR observations. designed to detect Earth-mass planets around nearby stars. canceled in 2007.

key science goals:
- detect $\sim$ 30 Earth-mass exoplanets within 10 pc
- characterize their atmospheres for life signatures (water, oxygen, methane)
- baseline: ~50-100 m

### Terrestrial Planet Finder (TPF, NASA, never realized)

similar concept. canceled in 2010.

key science goals: same as Darwin.

### LIFE (Large Interferometer For Exoplanets) — current

a European-led 4-aperture formation-flying nuller. proposed for 2040+ launch.

key science goals:
- $10^{10}$ contrast at mid-IR (8-15 μm)
- 30+ Earth-twin candidates
- atmospheric characterization

### SIM (Space Interferometry Mission, NASA, canceled)

planned for sub-microarcsecond astrometry. canceled.

### EEI (Exo-Earth Imager) — concept

ambitious concept for 5-10 m baseline, $10^{12}$ contrast for direct *imaging* of Earth-twin planets (not just spectroscopy).

## the technical challenges

![Space Interferometry Mission (SIM) concept for micro-arcsecond optical astrometry from space.](Future_space-based.jpg)


three:

### 1. formation flying

multiple satellites maintaining *micrometer*-level precision over kilometers. requires:
- continuous metrology between satellites
- continuous propulsion (small ion thrusters)
- redundant communication

ESA's PROBA-3 mission (2024) tests these technologies.

### 2. cryogenic operation

mid-IR observations need cold telescopes. JWST's $\sim 50$ K is achievable but limits observing time (must hibernate to recool).

### 3. data downlink

high-resolution interferometric data are voluminous. limited by deep-space network bandwidth.

## the scientific expectations

if LIFE flies (~2040):
- detect $\sim 30$ Earth-mass exoplanets
- characterize their atmospheres
- spectroscopic detection of water, methane, oxygen → potential life signatures
- *first definitive answer* to "are we alone in the universe"

these are the highest-stakes scientific goals of any planned space astronomy mission.

## the EHT-style space VLBI

extending EHT to space:
- **RadioAstron** (Russia, decommissioned 2019): 10m antenna in elliptical orbit, baselines up to 3 × Earth diameters
- **Millimetron** (Russia, planned): 10m millimeter-wave antenna in space, baselines >> Earth-diameter
- **next-gen EHT (ngEHT) space extension**: proposed 1-3 satellites for time-resolved black-hole imaging at $\sim 1$ μas resolution

these would resolve event-horizon-scale dynamics around the *closest* SMBHs (M87 jet base, Sgr A* hot-spot orbits).

## the lunar interferometers

far-side of the Moon: free-fall conditions, no atmospheric phase noise, no terrestrial RFI. ideal for very long baseline interferometry at low frequencies (where Earth's ionosphere is opaque).

planned:
- **Lunar Crater Radio Telescope** (NASA concept): a single-dish radio telescope in a crater
- **Lunar low-frequency array**: distributed network of small antennas for $\sim$ MHz observations
- **Lunar VLBI extension**: combining lunar stations with Earth ones for exotic baselines

these are conceptual but increasingly serious as Artemis and lunar-base programs proceed.

## the timeline

approximate projections:
- **2025-2030**: LIFE Phase A studies, formation-flying technology demonstrations
- **2030-2040**: LIFE Phase B/C/D. potentially launched 2040
- **2030-2050**: lunar far-side telescope concepts and possible deployment
- **2040-2050**: Earth-Moon VLBI baseline observations
- **2050+**: dedicated formation-flying space arrays (LIFE-2, EEI, etc.)

these are extremely long timelines. the decisions for the late-2040s missions are happening now.

## the funding reality

space interferometry has historically been *expensive* and *high-risk*:
- Darwin: $\sim$ €5 billion estimated
- TPF: $\sim$ \$5 billion
- LIFE: not yet costed (likely ~€2-5 billion)

these costs require sustained political support over decades. science cases must be airtight.
