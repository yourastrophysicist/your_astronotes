most stars have spectra dominated by **absorption lines**: photons climb out of a hot photosphere through cooler upper layers, absorbing in the cores. but some stars show **emission lines**, indicating circumstellar gas or non-photospheric emission. these are the **emission-line stars**.

## the families

### Be stars (B stars with emission)

B-type main-sequence or pre-MS stars with circumstellar **decretion disks** (gas spun off the rapidly rotating star). the disk contributes Balmer emission, He I emission, and double-peaked profiles indicating disk rotation.

key features:
- H$\alpha$ in emission, often with a double-peaked profile.
- variable: emission can come and go on timescales of years.
- rapid rotation (often $v\sin i > 200$ km/s).

example: $\gamma$ Cas, the Be prototype.

### Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars

very hot ($T \sim 30\,000$ to $100\,000$ K), massive, evolved stars that have lost their hydrogen envelope, exposing helium-burning cores. extreme winds drive broad emission lines.

subclasses:
- **WN**: nitrogen-dominated emission (He II, N III, N IV, N V).
- **WC**: carbon-dominated (He II, C III, C IV).
- **WO**: oxygen-rich.

emission lines are **very broad** ($v_{\rm FWHM} \sim 1000$ to $3000$ km/s) reflecting wind speeds. WR stars are progenitors of Type Ib/c supernovae.

### Of stars

an intermediate class: O-type with weak He II emission. transitional toward WR.

### Be (Pre-MS): T Tauri and Herbig Ae/Be

young stars with accretion + outflows. emission lines from accretion shocks and disk gas.
- **T Tauri**: $M \lesssim 2\,M_\odot$, K-M types, strong H$\alpha$ emission, [OI], [SII].
- **Herbig Ae/Be**: $M \sim 2$ to $8\,M_\odot$, A-B types, similar emission diagnostics.

## the diagnostics

emission line stars are characterised by:
- **emission line width and shape**: indicates wind/disk velocities.
- **P Cygni profiles**: blueshifted absorption + redshifted emission, sign of expanding wind.
- **inverse P Cygni**: redshifted absorption + blueshifted emission, sign of accretion.
- **multiplet ratios**: diagnose density and ionisation in the line-emitting region.

for Be stars, the **double-peaked H$\alpha$** profile peaks separated by twice the disk rotation velocity at the line-emitting region.

## peculiar examples

### LBVs (luminous blue variables)
massive, unstable, episodic mass loss with H, He, Fe emission. e.g. $\eta$ Carinae.

### sgB[e] stars
B-type supergiants with forbidden line emission ($[OI], [FeII]$), suggesting low-density circumstellar disks.

### symbiotic stars
binary systems with a cool giant + a hot accreting WD. spectra mix M-giant absorption with WD emission. see [Symbiotic star spectroscopy](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Symbiotic star spectroscopy.html).

## the role in stellar evolution

emission-line stars trace specific evolutionary phases:
- T Tauri / Herbig: pre-MS accretion.
- Be: rapid rotators on the MS.
- WR: post-MS hydrogen-stripped.
- LBV: pre-WR or post-WR instability.

each connects to the broader narrative of massive-star evolution.
