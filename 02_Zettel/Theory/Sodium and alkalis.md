title: "Sodium and alkalis"

**alkali atoms** (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs) have a single valence electron outside a closed-shell noble-gas core. their spectra look essentially **hydrogenic** with the active electron in $s, p, d$ states, but with energies modified by the screening core.

## sodium structure

Na ($Z = 11$): configuration $[Ne] 3s^1 = 1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$. only the $3s^1$ electron is "active." the spectrum is just the H-like spectrum of a single electron with $Z_{\rm eff} \approx 1.84$ (effective charge after core screening, slightly above 1 because of incomplete screening).

ground state: $3\,^2S_{1/2}^e$.

low-lying excited states:
| level | $n^{2S+1}L_J$ | energy (eV) |
|---|---|---|
| $3s^1$ ground | $3\,^2S_{1/2}^e$ | 0 |
| $3p^1$ | $3\,^2P^o_{1/2}$, $3\,^2P^o_{3/2}$ | $2.10$ to $2.10$ (D-doublet) |
| $4s^1$ | $4\,^2S_{1/2}^e$ | 3.19 |
| $3d^1$ | $3\,^2D^e_{3/2}$, $3\,^2D^e_{5/2}$ | 3.62 |

## the famous D-doublet

the $3p \to 3s$ transition is split by spin-orbit into:
- **D$_2$**: $3\,^2P_{3/2}^o \to 3\,^2S_{1/2}^e$, $\lambda = 5889.95$ Å.
- **D$_1$**: $3\,^2P_{1/2}^o \to 3\,^2S_{1/2}^e$, $\lambda = 5895.94$ Å.

separation: $\sim 6$ Å, easily resolved at $R \gtrsim 1000$. seen as **absorption** in solar-type and cooler stars (very strong in K and M because Na not yet ionised), as **emission** in solar prominences, sodium streetlights, and lunar exospheres.

## the alkali doublet pattern

every alkali shows the same structure: one strong absorption doublet from the lowest $p$ state to the ground:
- Li: $\lambda 6708$ (Li I).
- Na: D-doublet $\lambda 5890, 5896$.
- K: $\lambda 7665, 7699$.
- Rb: $\lambda 7800, 7948$.
- Cs: $\lambda 8521, 8943$.

each is a useful diagnostic in stellar atmospheres for $[Na/H]$, $[K/H]$, etc., or as ISM absorber along quasar sight lines.

## why alkalis matter

- **chemical evolution tracer**: Na is an $\alpha$-element-light, traces SN II vs SN Ia. modest sensitivity but well-measured.
- **stellar atmosphere diagnostic**: D-line shape is sensitive to surface gravity (pressure broadening, stronger in dwarfs than giants of same $T$). a luminosity-class indicator.
- **interstellar absorption**: Na D and K $\lambda 7665$ in absorption profile of bright background stars or QSOs trace cold neutral diffuse ISM.
- **lunar / exospheric studies**: Na D in emission is the brightest atomic line in tenuous-atmosphere bodies (Mercury, Moon, Io).

## the role on the OBAFGKM sequence

Na D appears as absorption in stars cooler than $\sim$ G0, becomes very strong in K and M dwarfs (where Na is mostly neutral), then weakens in cooler M giants where molecular bands (TiO, VO) overwhelm it. always in the optical, it is one of the strongest features in cool-star atmospheres.

in hotter stars (B, A, F), Na is mostly ionised; the Na I D lines are weak or absent. so D-line strength **decreases with increasing $T$**.
