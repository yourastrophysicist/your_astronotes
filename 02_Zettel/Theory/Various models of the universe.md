title: "Various models of the universe"
---

historical + theoretical models of the universe, ranging from the static Einstein universe through the Big Bang flavours. each helps illustrate different solutions to Friedmann's equations + the role of $\Omega_m, \Omega_\Lambda, k$.

## Einstein static universe (1917)

Einstein's first cosmological model. assumed:
- $\Lambda$ tuned to balance gravitational attraction.
- result: $\dot a = \ddot a = 0$, **eternally static**.

problem: **unstable**. small density perturbations grow without bound. plus, observations from Hubble (1929) showed expansion. Einstein retracted: "biggest blunder."

## de Sitter universe (1917)

vacuum-only universe with $\Lambda$:
- $\rho = 0$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 1$.
- $a(t) \propto e^{Ht}$, exponential expansion.
- relevant as the **asymptotic future** of $\Lambda$CDM + as the **inflation epoch** in early universe.

## Friedmann's matter universes (1922)

Friedmann's original three models, before $\Lambda$:
- **closed** ($k = +1$, $\Omega_m > 1$): expands, recollapses ("Big Crunch").
- **flat** ($k = 0$, $\Omega_m = 1$): expands forever, decelerating. Einstein-de Sitter (EdS).
- **open** ($k = -1$, $\Omega_m < 1$): expands forever, asymptotes to $a \propto t$.

## Lemaître's universe (1927)

Lemaître proposed a **slowly-decelerating** universe with $\Lambda$, predicting:
- a hot dense beginning (the **"primeval atom"**).
- expansion + redshift.
- a finite age.

includes both Friedmann + de Sitter limits as special cases. now considered the founding paper of modern cosmology.

## Einstein-de Sitter (EdS, 1932)

specifically the **flat matter-only** model:
- $\Omega_m = 1$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 0$, $k = 0$.
- $a(t) \propto t^{2/3}$.
- age $t_0 = (2/3)/H_0 \approx 9.3$ Gyr.

historical: standard model from $1932$ to $1998$. now ruled out:
- predicts age too young.
- predicts $\Omega_m = 1$ vs observed $0.31$.
- doesn't match SN Ia Hubble diagram.

## Milne universe (1935)

empty universe with $k = -1$:
- $\rho = 0$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 0$.
- $a(t) = ct$, linear expansion.
- equivalent to Minkowski in expanding coordinates.

unphysical (universe is not empty). but useful as a reference + asymptotic limit.

## Steady-State universe (1948, Bondi-Gold + Hoyle)

alternative to Big Bang:
- universe expands but **continuously creates** new matter to maintain constant density.
- no Big Bang; eternal universe.
- predicts no CMB.

**ruled out** by:
- discovery of CMB (1965) — predicted by Big Bang only.
- BBN abundances ($^4$He primordial fraction).
- evolution of QSO + galaxy populations.

## $\Lambda$CDM (modern, 1998-)

the standard model:
- $\Omega_m \approx 0.31$, $\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.69$, $|\Omega_k| < 0.005$.
- flat with $\Lambda$ + cold dark matter + standard inflation.
- accelerated expansion since $z \sim 0.7$.
- age $13.8$ Gyr.

fits **all** current cosmological data (CMB, SN Ia, BAO, weak lensing, BBN). still has open issues (Hubble tension, $\sigma_8$ tension, possible $w \ne -1$).

## comparison: matter-only vs $\Lambda$CDM at high $z$

at small $z$, EdS + $\Lambda$CDM agree.
at high $z$ ($z \gtrsim 0.5$): $\Lambda$CDM predicts SN Ia **fainter** than EdS would, due to:
- larger comoving distance (for same $z$).
- corresponding larger $d_L$.

this is the SN Ia signature that revolutionised cosmology in 1998.
