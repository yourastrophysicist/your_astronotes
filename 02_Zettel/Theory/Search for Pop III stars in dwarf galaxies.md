description: surveys hunting for chemically primitive stars with Pop III nucleosynthetic fingerprints, with UFDGs and the Galactic halo as the best hunting grounds

since [Pop III stars](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Population III stars.md) are not directly observable today (the metal-free first generation has died out), the **search for Pop III chemical signatures** in surviving stars is the main observational frontier. ultra-faint dwarf galaxies + the Galactic outer halo are the best hunting grounds because their stars are old, chemically primitive, and were polluted by very few progenitors per cloud.

## why dwarf galaxies are the best targets

unlike the Milky Way disk + bulge, [UFDGs](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Ultra-faint dwarf galaxies definition.md) have:

- **brief star-formation episode** (single old burst, truncated at reionisation);
- **shallow potential well** (only one or few SNe pollute the entire UFDG);
- **no later enrichment** from Pop II/I;
- **stochastic chemistry** preserved per-star, not averaged over many progenitors;
- **resolved photometry possible** (UFDGs are nearby, $d \sim 30$-$300$ kpc).

so each star in a UFDG is approximately a chemical fossil of one Pop III progenitor.

## the major surveys

### SkyMapper (Australia, 2014-present)

photometric survey using the **CaHK filter** sensitive to the strong Ca II H+K absorption line in metal-poor stars. wide-area photometric metallicity catalogue covering the southern hemisphere.

key result: discovery of a $[{\rm Fe/H}] = -7.1$ star (Keller et al. 2014, Nature 506, 463) in the Galactic halo — the most iron-poor known.

### Pristine survey (CFHT, 2015-present)

similar idea: narrow-band Ca H+K photometry to pre-select metal-poor candidates from CFHT imaging, then high-resolution spectroscopic follow-up. Starkenburg et al. 2017 review.

key result: hundreds of new $[{\rm Fe/H}] < -3$ stars; mapping of metal-poor stellar streams.

### Hamburg/ESO Survey (Christlieb et al. 1990s-2000s)

historical, wide-area, southern. yielded HE 0107-5240 and HE 1327-2326 — the original ultra-iron-poor giants.

### R-Process Alliance (Hansen et al. 2018+)

systematic spectroscopic survey targeting CEMP + r-process II candidates from photometric pre-selection. has uncovered $\sim 100$ r-II stars, several confirmed CEMP-no, several Pop III candidates.

### S-PLUS, J-PAS (similar narrow-band surveys)

next-gen photometric surveys with even more bands; will provide thousands of new metal-poor candidates.

## ground-based + space-based spectroscopic follow-up

high-resolution spectroscopy is essential to confirm Pop III chemical signatures. used spectrographs:

- **HIRES** (Keck) — north hemisphere, $R \sim 50{,}000$.
- **MIKE** (Magellan) — south hemisphere.
- **UVES** (VLT) — south hemisphere, $R \sim 100{,}000$.
- **HDS** (Subaru) — north.
- **ESPRESSO** (VLT, since 2018) — extreme precision.

multi-object spectrographs for population-scale surveys:

- **APOGEE** (SDSS-IV/V) — IR, $R \sim 22{,}000$, $> 10^5$ stars.
- **GALAH** (HERMES at AAT) — optical, $R \sim 28{,}000$, $> 10^6$ stars.
- **WEAVE, 4MOST, DESI** — next-gen.

## the chemical fingerprint search

stars in dwarf galaxies + halo are screened for:

1. **CEMP-no** signature: $[{\rm C/Fe}] > +0.7$, $[{\rm Ba/Fe}] < 0$, $[{\rm Fe/H}] < -2.5$.
2. **r-process II** pattern: $[{\rm Eu/Fe}] > +1$ + complete heavy-element pattern.
3. **even-Z dominance** (PISN signature): $[{\rm Si/Fe}] + [{\rm Ca/Fe}] > +0.5$, $[{\rm Na/Fe}] < -0.5$.
4. **extreme low Fe**: $[{\rm Fe/H}] < -5$.

each of these constrains a different Pop III progenitor mass range.

## key results from UFDGs

- **Reticulum II** (Ji et al. 2016, Nature 531, 610): 7 of 9 stars are r-process II; entire UFDG enriched by one ancient neutron-star merger.
- **Tucana III** (Hansen et al. 2017): single r-II star in a sparse UFDG.
- **Boötes I** (Frebel et al. 2010): predominantly CEMP-no stars; primitive chemistry.
- **Segue I** (Frebel et al. 2014): one of the most metal-poor stellar systems known, $\langle [{\rm Fe/H}] \rangle = -2.7$, with several stars at $[{\rm Fe/H}] < -3.5$.

## why this matters

these surveys are slowly mapping the Pop III IMF + SN explosion physics by counting fossils:

- the relative numbers of CEMP-no, r-II, and PISN-descendant stars constrain the relative event rates of $20$-$50\,M_\odot$, NSM, and PISN progenitors;
- the absence of clear PISN signatures argues that the Pop III IMF top end is steeper than predicted (or PISNe are rarer than expected);
- the discovery of $[{\rm Fe/H}] < -7$ stars sets a lower limit on the chemical pollution of the most primitive proto-galactic clouds.
