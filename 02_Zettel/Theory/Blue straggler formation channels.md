name: Blue straggler formation channels
description: collisions, mass transfer, and binary mergers as competing pathways to blue straggler formation
---

[blue stragglers](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Blue stragglers in star clusters.md) cannot be made by single-star evolution; they need to acquire mass after the cluster formed. three main channels are discussed, and the relative contribution depends strongly on the cluster's local stellar density.

**(i) stellar collisions in dense cores**

in the dense cores of [Globular Clusters](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Globular Clusters.md) the encounter rate is high enough that direct stellar collisions occur. two MS stars in a close passage can merge hydrodynamically, forming a single, more massive object. the dominant channel here is actually the dynamical interaction of binaries with single stars (binary-single, $bs$) or other binaries (binary-binary, $bb$), which often produces a collision during the chaotic resonance.

the rate of collisions scales with the encounter rate parameter

$$\Gamma \propto \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma},$$

where $\rho$ is the local stellar density and $\sigma$ is the velocity dispersion. cluster cores like M15, NGC 6397, and 47 Tuc, with $\rho_c \gtrsim 10^5\,M_\odot\,\text{pc}^{-3}$, are prime sites. collisional BSS show signatures: faster rotation (because of conserved angular momentum from the collision) and depleted surface C and O (because the merger product is partially mixed and shows the deeper layers of the progenitors).

**(ii) binary mass transfer (case A and B)**

a primordial binary in which the more massive primary fills its Roche lobe transfers material to the secondary. depending on the evolutionary stage at the onset of mass transfer:

- case A: primary still on MS,
- case B: primary on Hertzsprung gap or [RGB](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Red giant branch RGB.md),
- case C: primary on AGB.

the secondary, gaining mass, becomes more massive than the MSTO and is "rejuvenated" into a BSS. the primary becomes a white dwarf. the resulting BSS is therefore expected to have a WD companion (sometimes detectable in UV) and to rotate slowly, because mass transfer can spin down the accretor through magnetic braking and tidal coupling.

this channel dominates in [Open clusters](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Open clusters.md) and in the low-density haloes of [Globular Clusters](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Globular Clusters.md) where collisions are negligible. mccrea (1964) was first to propose the channel.

**(iii) binary mergers**

a tight primordial binary, hardened by magnetic braking or by tidal dissipation in a triple system (kozai-lidov oscillations), can spiral in and merge into a single more massive star. this produces a single BSS with no companion. the perez-blanco et al. and naoz et al. work has emphasised the role of triples.

**ratios and observations**

in low-density open clusters and the haloes of GCs, channels (ii) and (iii) dominate. in dense GC cores, channel (i) becomes important. the radial distribution of BSS in many GCs (the "double-peak" found by ferraro et al. 2012) reflects this: collisional BSS sink fast to the centre, while binary BSS in the outskirts diffuse in slowly through two-body relaxation.

distinguishing the channels uses:
- rotation rate (collisions $\to$ fast; mass transfer $\to$ slow),
- C and O surface abundances (collisional products underabundant),
- presence of a WD companion (mass transfer products show UV excess),
- binary fraction of the BSS sample.

cosmologically, binary-channel BSS link to [CV progenitors](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Cataclysmic variables in clusters.md) and to Ia supernova progenitors in old populations (whether BSS pathways feed the Ia rate is open).
