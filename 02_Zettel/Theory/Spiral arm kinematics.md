title: "Spiral arm kinematics"
---

spiral arms in disk galaxies are not material structures — they are **density waves** that move through the disk while individual stars and gas pass in and out. this is the **density wave theory** (Lin & Shu 1964).

without this, spirals would have wound up tight after just a few rotations: at $V = 220$ km/s and $R = 8$ kpc, the orbital period is $\sim 230$ Myr, so over the age of the disk ($\sim 10$ Gyr) we'd have $\sim 40$ rotations and the arms would be hopelessly tightly wound. observations show they aren't.

---

## the rotation curve

the rotational velocity $V(R)$ at radius $R$ in a disk galaxy is related to the enclosed mass:
$$V^2(R) = \frac{G M(<R)}{R}$$

(circular orbit balance.) so $V(R)$ is a **direct measurement of the mass distribution** $M(<R)$.

for the Milky Way, the rotation curve:
- rises rapidly out to $\sim 1$ kpc (rigid-body rotation in the bulge)
- nearly flat out to large radii: $V \approx 220$ km/s for $R = 5$–$15$ kpc

the flat outer rotation curve is the **strongest galactic-scale evidence for dark matter** — see [Cosmic_inventory_dark_matter](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Cosmic_inventory_dark_matter.html). without dark matter, $V$ would Keplerian-decline outside the visible disk.

---

## differential rotation

at the Sun's radius ($R = 8.2$ kpc), $V = 220$ km/s, period $T \sim 230$ Myr.
at $R = 4$ kpc: $V \approx 220$ km/s, period $T \sim 110$ Myr (twice as fast).

so different radii rotate at *different angular velocities*. this is **differential rotation**.

if the spiral arms were material structures (always made of the same stars), they would wind up. but they aren't.

---

## density wave theory

Lin-Shu (1964) proposed that the spiral pattern is a **density wave** rotating with **a single pattern speed** $\Omega_p$:
- at radii where $\Omega(R) = \Omega_p$ — the **corotation radius** — material rotates with the wave
- inside corotation: material rotates faster than the pattern → gas overtakes the wave → enters the spiral arm from the trailing side
- outside corotation: material rotates slower → spiral arm catches up to it from the trailing side

so material **passes through** the arms. when a gas cloud enters an arm:
- compression triggers star formation
- young (hot, blue) O/B stars are born in the arm and live their short lives there
- by the time they evolve and die ($\sim 10^7$ yr), they have moved a significant fraction across the arm

this is why **spiral arms appear blue and clumpy** — they are populated by short-lived O/B stars, which trace recent star formation.

---

## tracers of spiral arms

different tracers light up different parts of the wave:

| tracer | what it shows |
|---|---|
| **HI 21 cm** | gas distribution (broad arms) |
| **CO** | molecular clouds, the densest gas (where stars form) |
| **HII regions / H$\alpha$** | active star formation (inside the arms) |
| **O/B stars** | recent star formation, sharp arm structure |
| **A/F stars** | older population, more dispersed |
| **K/M stars** | dynamically equilibrated, smooth |

so the *visible* spiral structure depends on which tracer you use. the underlying density wave is the same, but the visible imprint is dominated by short-lived populations.

---

## resonances and pattern speed

the density wave has **resonances** at specific radii where:
- **inner Lindblad resonance (ILR)**: $\Omega - \kappa/m = \Omega_p$
- **outer Lindblad resonance (OLR)**: $\Omega + \kappa/m = \Omega_p$
- **corotation**: $\Omega = \Omega_p$

(with $\kappa$ the epicyclic frequency, $m$ the symmetry — $m = 2$ for a two-armed spiral.) at these radii, gas piles up due to resonant interactions, often forming rings or features visible in the morphology.

the pattern speed $\Omega_p$ for the Milky Way bar is $\sim 40$ km/s/kpc → corotation at $\sim 5-6$ kpc.

---

## a still-active question: what drives the spiral?

mechanisms producing spiral patterns:
- **density waves** (Lin-Shu): self-sustaining, long-lived
- **swing amplification**: shear amplifies leading perturbations into trailing spirals
- **bar-driven**: the central bar drives a wave through the disk
- **tidal**: an interaction with a nearby galaxy produces transient spirals

the relative role of these is still under debate, especially for **flocculent** spirals (irregular, broken arms — Sb-Sc galaxies) vs **grand design** spirals (smooth two-armed structures — M51).

---

## why this matters

beyond the Milky Way, spiral arm kinematics:
- enable **rotation-curve measurements** in external galaxies → rotation-curve based dark matter
- give the **Tully-Fisher relation**: $L \propto V_{\rm flat}^4$ for spiral galaxies, a key distance indicator (see [Tully-Fisher relation](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Tully-Fisher relation.html))
- explain the **morphology** of spiral types Sa-Sb-Sc-SBc (see [Hubble morphological sequence](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Hubble morphological sequence.html))
- track recent **star formation history** (the arms are stellar nurseries)
