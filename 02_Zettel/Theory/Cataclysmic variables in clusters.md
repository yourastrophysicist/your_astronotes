name: Cataclysmic variables in clusters
description: white dwarfs accreting from Roche-lobe-filling companions in globular clusters, found via UV excess and Halpha variability
---

cataclysmic variables (CVs) are close binary systems in which a white dwarf accretes from a low-mass companion that fills its Roche lobe. in the field, CVs are common (orbital periods 1.4 hr to a day) and span dwarf novae, classical novae, polars, and intermediate polars. in [Globular Clusters](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Globular Clusters.md) they are rarer per unit stellar mass, but they trace the dense binary populations of cluster cores.

**why CVs in clusters matter**

the CV is the simplest example of an interacting compact-object binary. its rate per unit cluster mass depends on:

- the [primordial binary fraction](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Cluster binary fraction methods.md),
- the rate of dynamical exchange encounters in the core,
- the fraction of binaries that survive common-envelope evolution to become hardened post-CE.

in dense [GC](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Globular Clusters.md) cores, CVs can also be assembled dynamically: a single WD captures a low-mass MS star through tidal interactions or exchange with another binary. the predicted CV abundance per unit mass therefore correlates with the encounter rate parameter $\Gamma \propto \rho^2 / \sigma$.

**how we find them**

CVs in clusters reveal themselves via a combination of:

1. **UV excess**: in quiescence the WD primary plus a hot inner accretion disc emits in the UV. CV candidates appear blueward of the WD cooling sequence and below the MS in UV-optical CMDs ($U\text{--}V$ or $\text{F275W}\text{--}\text{F606W}$).
2. **H$\alpha$ excess**: the accretion disc and accretion stream emit a strong H$\alpha$ line. narrow-band imaging on H$\alpha$ vs nearby continuum reveals emission-line objects. surveys of NGC 6397, NGC 6752, and 47 Tuc have used HST ACS/WFC narrow-band data.
3. **photometric variability**: CVs flicker on short timescales (minutes) due to disc instabilities, and dwarf novae show outbursts of $\Delta m \sim 2\text{--}5$ mag every weeks to months.
4. **X-ray emission**: the boundary layer where the disc meets the WD emits soft X-rays ($L_X \sim 10^{30}\text{--}10^{33}\,\text{erg/s}$). [chandra surveys](../../02_Zettel/Theory/X-ray sources in globular clusters.md) of cluster cores find dozens of CV candidates per cluster.
5. **classical novae**: occasional thermonuclear flashes on the WD surface produce $\Delta m \gtrsim 10$ mag outbursts. only a handful of cluster novae are known, e.g. T Sco in M80 (1860!).

**numbers**

a typical massive [GC](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Globular Clusters.md) hosts $\sim 10\text{--}100$ CV candidates, depending on density. 47 Tuc hosts roughly 50 confirmed and candidate CVs; NGC 6397 has several well-studied systems. the CV fraction per unit luminosity is enhanced by factors of a few in core-collapsed clusters.

**connection to other exotica**

CVs share the same parameter space as [MSPs](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Millisecond pulsars in GCs.md) and [qLMXBs](../../02_Zettel/Theory/X-ray sources in globular clusters.md). the difference is the compact-object mass: WD for CVs, NS for MSP/LMXB. dynamical processing in dense cluster cores produces all three, and the CV/MSP/LMXB number ratios constrain the binary processing efficiency. some CVs are believed to be the descendants of [blue stragglers](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Blue stragglers in star clusters.md) that have evolved through the CE phase.
