# Lunar interferometry concepts

the Moon's far side is a unique observing platform: shielded from Earth's RFI, no ionosphere, free-fall conditions, very stable temperature. proposed since the Apollo era for high-resolution astronomy. now becoming feasible thanks to renewed lunar programs (Artemis, lunar bases).

## the unique advantages

three:

### 1. RFI silence

Earth's far-side hides terrestrial radio transmissions. mobile phones, satellites, FM radio — all blocked. for low-frequency observations (<30 MHz, blocked by Earth's ionosphere), the Moon is *the only place* without RFI.

### 2. no ionosphere

Earth's ionosphere is opaque below ~10 MHz. on the Moon, this band is fully accessible — the **lowest cosmic-frequency** unexplored regime.

### 3. mechanical stability

no atmosphere, no weather, very stable temperature. sub-mm precision over kilometers becomes feasible.

## the typical concept

![The Parkes radio telescope; lunar far-side concepts expand on dish arrays into the solar system quiet zone.](Lunar_interferometry_concepts.jpg)


a lunar far-side radio telescope:
- one or more antennas (single dish or distributed)
- communication relay: line-of-sight to lunar polar relay satellite
- power: solar panels (Sun is in the lunar sky for ~14 day-night cycle)

## the proposed instruments

### Lunar Crater Radio Telescope (LCRT)

NASA concept (2020+): a 1km-diameter wire-mesh dish suspended in a lunar far-side crater. uses the crater walls as natural support. operates at 1-10 MHz — the lowest astronomical frequencies ever observed.

scientific goal: cosmic Dark Ages — the period after recombination but before the first stars formed (z = 30-200, $\sim$ 100-200 Myr after Big Bang). detection of the redshifted 21cm signal from this era.

### Lunar low-frequency array (LLFA)

distributed network of $\sim 1000$ small antennas spread over the lunar far side. baselines $\sim$ km, operating at $\sim$ 0.5-50 MHz. could be assembled by a series of robotic missions.

### Lunar high-frequency interferometer

a small array of $\sim 10$ optical telescopes spread across $\sim 100$ km on the Moon's surface. ground-fixed, sharing the lunar coordinate frame. potentially achievable contrast and precision orders of magnitude better than Earth.

mostly conceptual; not seriously funded.

## the link to Earth

lunar interferometry can link to Earth-based instruments via VLBI-style data recording. signals from a lunar station + Earth-based EHT or VLBA could give Earth-Moon-baseline interferometry: 384000 km baselines!

at 230 GHz: $\theta = \lambda/B = 1.3$ mm $/$ 384000 km $\sim 0.7$ μas. resolution comparable to a single SMBH gravitational radius for nearby AGN.

## the timeline

NASA's Artemis program (2025-onwards) brings infrastructure for:
- regular lunar landings (~2026 onwards)
- lunar surface bases (~2030+)
- science instrument deployment as part of lunar base infrastructure

lunar interferometry could become a reality by ~2035-2040 if these programs proceed.

European Space Agency and other agencies have similar plans.

## the challenges

three:

### 1. cost

the most expensive astronomical observatory ever built — perhaps $5-10 billion.

### 2. lunar dust

regolith dust is sticky, abrasive, electrostatic. damages mechanisms. solutions: encased designs, stationary deployment.

### 3. communication

far-side has no direct line-of-sight to Earth. requires relay satellites at lunar L2 (Earth-Moon Lagrange point). multiple satellites for redundancy.

## the science cases

unique science only possible from the Moon:

### cosmic Dark Ages 21cm signal

the most ambitious goal. the signal is at $\sim 10$ MHz, blocked by Earth's ionosphere. detecting it would:
- probe the universe between recombination and first stars (~100 Myr post-Big Bang)
- distinguish dark-matter models
- test structure-formation theory in unprecedented ways

### exoplanet auroras

low-frequency emission from exoplanet magnetospheres detectable from a quiet lunar environment. diagnostics of planetary magnetic fields and habitability.

### early universe SMBH formation

low-frequency signatures of the first SMBHs at z > 20.

### lunar regolith composition

ground-truth measurements aided by radio sounding of subsurface structures.
