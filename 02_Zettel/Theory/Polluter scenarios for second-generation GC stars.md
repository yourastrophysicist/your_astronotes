description: candidate sources of the proton-capture-processed gas that formed the 2G in GCs, and why every scenario currently fails
---

the central unsolved problem of multiple populations is: what stars produced the chemically processed gas from which the 2G formed? the polluter must (i) reach high temperature for proton-capture nucleosynthesis, (ii) eject this gas slowly enough to retain it in the cluster, (iii) produce the observed abundance pattern, + (iv) supply enough mass to form a 2G that is often more numerous than the surviving 1G. no candidate satisfies all four. this is the **mass budget problem** + the **abundance pattern problem**.

## the four major candidates

### 1. intermediate-mass AGB + super-AGB stars (4-8 M☉)

the original + most-developed scenario (cottrell + da costa 1981, ventura + d'antona, d'ercole, decressin in 2000s).

- **mechanism**: in AGB stars of $4$-$8\, M_\odot$ the convective envelope reaches the top of the H-burning shell, a process called **hot bottom burning** (HBB). temperatures at the base of the convective envelope reach $\sim 60$-$110$ MK, igniting the CNO + NeNa + (marginally) MgAl cycles. processed gas is dredged to the surface + ejected slowly via stellar winds.
- **strengths**: low-velocity ejecta retained by the cluster's potential well; produces N enhancement, Na enhancement, He enhancement; correct timescale ($\sim 30$-$100$ Myr after 1G formation, after SNe have cleared); composition does not show iron-peak or s-process pollution (consistent with most Type I GCs)
- **problems**: 
  - mass budget: even with 100% efficient capture, AGB stars of an initial 1G of $\sim 10^5\, M_\odot$ can only seed a 2G of a few $\times 10^4\, M_\odot$, while observed 2G fractions average ~65% of present mass → the 1G would have to be $\sim 10\times$ more massive originally + selectively lost
  - chemistry: detailed AGB yields tend to produce a Na-O **correlation** at extreme ends (not anti-correlation), unless dilution with primordial gas is invoked
  - MgAl cycle marginally activates only in the most massive super-AGB models

### 2. fast-rotating massive stars (FRMS, 20-100 M☉)

proposed by decressin, meynet, charbonnel, prantzos (2007).

- **mechanism**: massive MS stars rotating near critical velocity launch slow equatorial decretion disks. CNO + NeNa products from the convective core diffuse into the envelope via rotational mixing + are released into the disk. the disk material is slow + retained
- **strengths**: produces hot-H-burning chemistry; releases mass on $\sim 10$ Myr timescale, before SNe go off; large mass available
- **problems**:
  - timescale also overlaps SNe + radiation feedback, which would clear gas
  - exact yields of the disk depend on rotation + mass loss prescriptions, with significant uncertainties
  - mass budget still tight; the FRMS scenario also needs preferential 1G mass loss
  - observed populations are typically discrete, not continuous, while FRMS would give a smoother distribution

### 3. massive interacting binaries

de mink, izzard, pols, bastian (2009 onward).

- **mechanism**: in binary mass transfer, the donor's CNO-processed core layers are exposed + transferred. the secondary spins up + later loses material as well. the total ejected processed mass per binary is large
- **strengths**: addresses some FRMS problems; binaries are known to be common in massive stars
- **problems**: chemistry of ejecta hard to predict cleanly; does not naturally reproduce extreme He enhancement; spatial + temporal distribution of ejecta uncertain

### 4. supermassive stars (SMS, $10^3$-$10^5\, M_\odot$)

denissenkov + hartwick (2014), gieles, charbonnel et al. (2018), most recently championed for solving the mass budget.

- **mechanism**: in dense proto-cluster cores, runaway stellar collisions build a single supermassive star. its core reaches $\sim 75$ MK, fully activating the MgAl chain, + it loses mass continuously to the cluster gas, polluting it as the 2G forms. the SMS itself collapses to an intermediate-mass BH
- **strengths**: solves the mass budget trivially (one $10^4\, M_\odot$ SMS can pollute the entire 2G); reaches MgAl temperatures naturally; predicts a correlation between cluster mass + SMS mass that matches observed scaling of $\Delta Y$ + Mg-Al with cluster mass
- **problems**: existence of SMSs not directly demonstrated; runaway collisions require very dense initial conditions; simulations of formation still developing

## the mass budget problem in detail

if the 2G is currently $\sim 65\%$ of cluster mass + the polluter is some fraction of the 1G, then the **initial 1G mass must have been many times larger than the present total**. for AGB pollution this factor is ~$10$. the standard solution is **preferential 1G loss**: 1G stars formed in an extended halo + were preferentially stripped early by tides + the cluster relaxed to a 2G-rich core. d'ercole et al. (2008) modelled this with hydrodynamic + N-body simulations + found it works qualitatively. but it requires specific initial conditions + the original cluster would have contributed a large population of stripped halo stars to the galactic field with GC-like 1G chemistry, which is partly seen + partly not.

## the dilution requirement

every polluter scenario requires diluting the polluter ejecta with **pristine gas** to reproduce the observed continuous Na-O distribution. pure polluter ejecta would give only a discrete extreme 2G, but observations show a continuous spread including intermediate compositions. the dilution gas must be available + must mix with ejecta in varying proportions. where does it come from? possibly residual primordial cluster gas, or accreted material from the host galaxy at later epochs.

## why this is unsolved

each scenario explains 2 or 3 of the constraints (chemistry, He, mass, timing) but none explains all of them simultaneously. the supermassive star idea is the most ambitious because it solves mass budget cleanly, but it is also the most speculative.

bastian + lardo (2018) wrote the canonical review summarizing the failure of every scenario. the problem remains open + is one of milone's active research areas.
