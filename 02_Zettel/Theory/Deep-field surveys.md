**deep-field surveys** are pointed observations of small areas of the sky to **very faint magnitudes**. by integrating long, they reveal galaxies far more numerous + distant than wide-field surveys can see. each major deep field has shaped a generation of high-$z$ galaxy science.

## the canonical deep fields

### Hubble Deep Field (HDF, 1995)

- **area**: $5.3$ arcmin$^2$.
- **bands**: HST/WFPC2 in $U, B, V, I$.
- **depth**: $V \sim 28$ (10x deeper than ground-based).
- **target**: empty patch in Ursa Major.
- **science**: revealed thousands of faint blue galaxies at $z = 1$ to $5$. deep counts established the galaxy buildup picture. ~1995 Williams + HDF team.

### Hubble Deep Field South (HDF-S, 1998)

southern counterpart of HDF. similar parameters but in the southern sky. added STIS + NICMOS data.

### Hubble Ultra-Deep Field (HUDF, 2003-2004)

- **area**: $11$ arcmin$^2$.
- **bands**: HST/ACS $B, V, i, z$ + NICMOS NIR.
- **depth**: $V \sim 29.5$, $B \sim 30.5$. the deepest optical image at the time.
- **science**: $\sim 10\,000$ galaxies, including the most distant known objects at $z \sim 6$ to $8$. the Lyman-break galaxy (LBG) sample.

### FORS Deep Field (FDF, 2000)

- **area**: $\sim 7$ arcmin$^2$.
- **instrument**: VLT/FORS2 + ISAAC.
- **depth**: $R \sim 27$, $K \sim 23$.
- **science**: spectroscopic follow-up of HUDF-equivalent depth in the south.

### GOODS (Great Observatories Origins Deep Survey)

multi-wavelength campaign 2003-onward across two fields (GOODS-N, GOODS-S) of $\sim 160$ arcmin$^2$ each. combines HST + Spitzer + Chandra + Herschel + ALMA + JWST. the deepest panchromatic dataset in galaxy science.

### COSMOS (Cosmic Evolution Survey, 2003-onward)

- **area**: $1.7$ deg$^2$ (much wider than HUDF/HDF).
- **bands**: HST F814W + multi-band photometry from many telescopes.
- **depth**: $i \sim 27.2$ HST imaging.
- **science**: $\sim 10^6$ galaxies, photometric redshifts to $z \sim 6$. balanced area + depth.

### Frontier Fields (2013-2017)

- HST imaging of 6 strong-lensing clusters + parallel fields.
- the **gravitational lensing magnification** by clusters reveals galaxies at $z = 9$ to $11$, otherwise invisible.
- discovered some of the most distant known galaxies before JWST.

### JWST deep fields (2022-onward)

- **JADES** (NIRSpec + NIRCam): hundreds of $z > 6$ galaxies with spectra.
- **CEERS, COSMOS-Web, PRIMER**: each adding deep NIR photometry + spectroscopy.
- early surprise: **bright $z > 10$ galaxies more abundant** than expected, challenging standard galaxy-formation models.

## the science driver

deep-field surveys answer:
- **galaxy number counts** as a function of magnitude + wavelength.
- **redshift distribution** $N(z)$.
- **luminosity functions** at high $z$, including the UV LF (a SFR proxy).
- **morphological evolution** of galaxies across cosmic time.
- **first galaxies** at $z > 10$, the epoch of reionisation.

## the trade-off: area vs depth

each survey balances:
- **area** ↔ **depth**: integration time per square arcmin.
- **shallow + wide** (SDSS): $\sim 14\,000$ deg$^2$ at $r \sim 22$.
- **deep + narrow** (HUDF): $0.003$ deg$^2$ at $V \sim 29.5$.

modern strategy: **deep-wide hybrids** (Euclid + LSST) combine moderate depth over thousands of deg$^2$.
