a **classical nova** is a thermonuclear runaway on the surface of a white dwarf accreting from a companion. the resulting spectrum evolves dramatically over weeks to months, with line widths and ionisation state changing as the ejecta expand and thin.

## the event

a WD in a close binary accretes hydrogen-rich material from its companion (Roche lobe overflow). when enough fuel accumulates ($\sim 10^{-4}\,M_\odot$), pressure ignites a thermonuclear runaway in the accreted shell. result:
- ejecta velocities $\sim 500$ to $5000$ km/s.
- mass ejected $\sim 10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}\,M_\odot$.
- visual brightening by $\sim 10$ mag (typical) over a few days.

## spectral evolution

over weeks to months, the spectrum passes through several phases:

### maximum light (day 0 to day 5)
absorption line spectrum dominated by Balmer lines, He I, ionised metal lines. resembles an A or F supergiant with a wind. **P Cygni profiles** are characteristic, with blueshifted absorption from the expanding photosphere.

### early decline (day 5 to day 30)
photosphere recedes as ejecta thin. emission components grow. spectrum shifts to a hot continuum with broad emission lines.

### nebular phase (day 30 to year 1)
ejecta become optically thin. spectrum dominated by **forbidden lines** ($[OIII], [NII]$) and Balmer recombination. resembles a planetary nebula.

### final phase (year 1+)
ejecta fade, only the WD remnant visible. system returns to quiescence.

## key features

at all phases, the **broad lines** (FWHM $\sim 1000$ to $3000$ km/s) are characteristic. line widths give the **ejecta velocity** directly.

P Cygni profiles confirm **expanding outflow**.

forbidden lines in the nebular phase confirm **low density** and **photoionisation** by the post-nova WD.

example: V2491 Cyg (April 2008). multiple-epoch spectra (in `intro_spec.pdf`) show this evolution clearly. Balmer lines and Fe II prominent at maximum, transitioning to $[OIII]$ in nebular phase by August.

## ONe vs CO novae

two compositional classes:
- **CO novae**: low-mass C/O white dwarfs. ejecta enriched in C and O.
- **ONe novae**: more massive O/Ne/Mg white dwarfs (near Chandrasekhar mass). ejecta enriched in Ne, Mg, Al.

distinguished by emission-line strengths in the nebular phase. ONe novae show bright [Ne III] $\lambda 3869$.

## recurrence

some novae recur on $\sim 1$ to $100$ year timescales (**recurrent novae**, e.g. RS Oph, T CrB). high-mass WDs accreting at high rate.

## relation to SN Ia progenitors

novae are **single-degenerate analogs** of SN Ia progenitors, but at much lower mass per event. studying the WD mass distribution from novae helps constrain whether some fraction reach Chandrasekhar mass and explode.
