# Keck interferometer

a (now decommissioned) interferometer that combined the two 10m Keck telescopes on Mauna Kea, Hawaii. operated 2003-2012. baselines: 85 m. K and L band imaging and nulling.

## the architecture

- **2 × 10m Keck telescopes** on a single mount (no, wait — separated dishes, separate mounts)
- baseline: 85 m (fixed, the inter-telescope distance)
- wavelength: K (2.2 μm), L (3.5 μm)
- AO at each telescope (LGS-AO + adaptive secondaries)
- beam combiner: K-band pairwise

## the unique features

![Keck Interferometer: combining the twin 10-meter Keck telescopes across an 85-meter baseline for high-resolution infrared observations.](Keck_interferometer.png)


three:

### 1. largest individual apertures of any optical interferometer at the time

the two 10m Kecks were the *largest* interferometric collectors ever (until ELTs come online). collecting area equivalent to combined 14m telescope.

### 2. nulling capability

KI included a **nulling interferometer** mode for high-contrast imaging — destructive interference on the central source, looking for faint companions or dust shells.

### 3. fixed single baseline

unlike VLTI (multiple ATs and variable baselines), Keck had only one fixed baseline. (u, v) coverage came purely from Earth rotation. limited imaging fidelity but excellent for diameter measurements.

## the science

- **Sgr A* monitoring**: early NIR observations of the Galactic Center black hole, before GRAVITY took over
- **stellar diameters**: high-precision measurements of giants and supergiants
- **YSO inner disk imaging**: at AU-scale resolution
- **nulling for exo-zodi**: searches for warm dust around nearby stars

## why it was decommissioned

KI shut down in 2012 due to:
1. NASA funding pressures
2. the rise of VLTI/GRAVITY (Sgr A* science)
3. operational complexity of the dual-Keck mount

modern alternatives: the Keck Observatory has retained its individual telescopes for AO + interferometric techniques (aperture masking, kernel-phase) but no longer operates as a coherent two-element interferometer.

## the legacy

KI demonstrated:
- 10m-class interferometry with adaptive optics
- nulling at significant contrast
- science-grade thermal-IR imaging

these capabilities now exist at VLTI (but with multiple smaller dishes). the dual-10m approach didn't survive, but the techniques developed at KI are still part of modern interferometric practice.
