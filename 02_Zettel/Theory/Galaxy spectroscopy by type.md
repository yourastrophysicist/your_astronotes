galaxy spectra are the **integrated light** of all stars + nebular gas + dust + AGN. their broad characters trace galaxy morphology and stellar age. four main types: elliptical, Sa-Sc spiral, starburst, AGN.

## elliptical galaxy spectra

**old, metal-rich, gas-poor stellar population**:
- **strong absorption** dominated by K-giant features.
- **$4000$ Å break** very pronounced ($D_n4000 > 1.7$).
- **Ca II H+K**: deep, very wide.
- **G-band $\lambda 4300$**: CH absorption.
- **Mg b $\lambda 5170$**, **Na D**, **CaII IR triplet**: strong.
- **no emission lines** (no SF, no AGN in most).
- **no UV continuum** (no young stars).

example in `intro_spec.pdf`: smooth spectrum with deep absorptions, increasing redward, very faint UV. prototype: M87, NGC 3115.

velocity dispersions $\sigma \sim 100$ to $300$ km/s $\to$ **broad lines** (kinematic broadening, not natural). measuring $\sigma$ from line widths gives stellar dynamical mass.

## Sa / Sb / Sc spiral spectra

**mixed old + young stellar populations + ionised gas + dust**:
- absorption features from old + intermediate stars (similar to ellipticals but weaker).
- weaker $4000$ Å break.
- emission lines: **H$\alpha$ + $[OIII]$ + $[NII]$ + $[OII]$ + $[SII]$** from HII regions in disk.
- modest UV continuum from younger stellar populations.

example: Sa galaxy in `intro_spec.pdf` shows mostly absorption with mild emission. moving from Sa to Sc, emission strengthens as the star formation rate increases.

## starburst galaxy spectra

**very young stellar population dominating + strong emission**:
- **bright Balmer emission** (Hα strongest).
- bright **$[OIII]\,\lambda 5007$**, sometimes brighter than H$\alpha$ in low-metallicity starbursts.
- **forbidden lines** ($[OII], [NII], [SII], [NeIII]$) all visible.
- **He I, He II** in some.
- **weak metal absorption** (dominated by young stars without metal lines yet).
- **strong UV continuum** from O and B stars.

example: starburst in `intro_spec.pdf` shows a featureless blue continuum + strong Hα, [OIII] forest. M82, NGC 1569, II Zw 40 are prototypes.

## AGN spectra

**diversity** based on viewing angle and intrinsic luminosity. four canonical types:

### Seyfert 1 (NLR + BLR visible)
- **broad emission lines** (FWHM $\sim 5000$ km/s) from BLR.
- **narrow emission lines** (FWHM $\sim 100$ to $1000$ km/s) from NLR.
- featureless blue UV-optical continuum.
- example: NGC 4151. broad H$\alpha, H\beta$, He II, narrow $[OIII]$, $[NeV]$.

### Seyfert 2 (only NLR visible)
- only **narrow emission lines** (BLR hidden by torus).
- featureless continuum suppressed, host galaxy continuum dominant.
- example: NGC 4941.

### LINER (low-ionisation nuclear emission)
- modest narrow emission lines.
- low-ionisation lines ($[OII], [SII], [NII], [OI]$) prominent.
- $[OIII]/[OII]$ low.
- could be weak AGN, post-AGB photoionisation, or shocks.
- example: NGC 4579.

### BL Lac (featureless beamed continuum)
- almost no emission features.
- power-law UV-optical continuum from beamed jet.
- variable, polarised.
- example: 0814 + 425.

### quasar / radio galaxy / NLRG / BLRG
- the most luminous AGN.
- **broad-line radio galaxy (BLRG)**: 3C390.3.
- **narrow-line radio galaxy (NLRG)**: Cyg A.

## the BPT classification

a 2D diagnostic plot ($[OIII]/H\beta$ vs $[NII]/H\alpha$) separates:
- **star-forming galaxies** (curving locus, "main sequence").
- **AGN / Seyfert** (above the locus).
- **LINERs** (intermediate).
