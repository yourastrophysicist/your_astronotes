besides [blue stragglers](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Blue stragglers in star clusters.html), [cluster CMDs](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Color-magnitude diagrams of clusters.html) reveal two further peculiar populations whose origin is also tied to [binary](../../02_Zettel/Theory/interf/Binary star orbits.html) evolution: **yellow stragglers** (YSS) and **sub-subgiants** (SSG, also called **red stragglers**).

**yellow stragglers**

YSS sit redward of the MSTO but bluer than the [RGB](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Red giant branch RGB.html) and brighter than the [SGB](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Subgiant branch SGB.html). they occupy the "Hertzsprung gap" of the cluster CMD, a region rapidly traversed by single-star evolution and therefore normally underpopulated.

interpretation: YSS are evolved descendants of [blue stragglers](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Blue stragglers in star clusters.html). once a BSS exhausts central H, it crosses to the giant branch like any other star but starts from a higher mass and luminosity, ending up offset from the standard cluster track. simulations of [binary mass transfer](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Blue straggler formation channels.html) products predict a YSS phase lasting $\sim 10^7\text{--}10^8$ yr, consistent with the small numbers observed (a few per cluster).

YSS act as the "smoking gun" linking BSS to the rest of stellar evolution: if BSS are real higher-mass MS stars, their descendants must populate the YSS region.

**sub-subgiants (red stragglers)**

SSG sit **below** the [Subgiant branch SGB](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Subgiant branch SGB.html) and **redward** of the MSTO. they are anomalously faint and red relative to where a normal subgiant should be. unlike YSS, their position is hard to reach by any reasonable single-star evolution, since stars do not spend significant time below the SGB after leaving the MS.

four formation channels are debated:

1. **rapid mass loss from a Roche lobe-filling MS star**: the partial removal of envelope lowers the luminosity below the SGB and reddens the colour.
2. **mass transfer onto an RGB star**: the gainer is a subgiant whose envelope is reddened by accretion.
3. **stripped giants**: a star whose envelope is being stripped by a companion, in transit to becoming a He WD.
4. **chromospherically active spotted binaries**: heavy starspot coverage on a tidally locked binary lowers the integrated photometric flux and shifts colour redward, mimicking SSG positions.

surveys of [Open clusters](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Open clusters.html) (e.g. M67, NGC 6791) and [Globular Clusters](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Globular Clusters.html) (e.g. M5, 47 Tuc, $\omega$ Cen) by geller, mathieu, leiner, and collaborators have identified $\sim 50\text{--}100$ candidate SSG systems. essentially all SSGs studied spectroscopically are confirmed binaries or show H$\alpha$ activity, supporting the active-binary or mass-transfer scenarios.

**rarity and significance**

both YSS and SSG are rare: typically a handful per cluster, $\lesssim 1\%$ of the BSS population. they survive only briefly because they are short-lived evolutionary phases. their numbers nevertheless calibrate binary stellar evolution codes (BSE, MESA, COMPAS) and constrain the long-term fate of [cluster binaries](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Cluster binary fraction methods.html).

YSS, SSG, [BSS](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Blue stragglers in star clusters.html), [CVs](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Cataclysmic variables in clusters.html), and [MSPs](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Millisecond pulsars in GCs.html) together form an inter-related family: products of the same dynamically processed binary populations that make GC cores so much weirder than the field.
