name: Multiple populations in extragalactic GCs
description: confirmation that multiple stellar populations are universal in massive old globular clusters across the Local Group, with extensions to extragalactic systems via JWST
---

a key test of [multiple populations (MPs)](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Multiple populations in GCs discovery.md) as a universal property of massive old globular clusters is whether they appear in clusters **outside the Milky Way**. they do. MPs have now been confirmed in GCs of:

- LMC + SMC (Magellanic Clouds);
- Andromeda (M31);
- Fornax + other dwarf galaxies in the Local Group;
- M87 + other ellipticals (via integrated light, less direct);
- and tantalisingly, JWST is starting to resolve the issue at $z > 1$.

## the LMC + SMC clusters

ancient ($> 10$ Gyr) GCs in the Magellanic Clouds (NGC 2210, NGC 1841, NGC 1466, etc.) show clear MP signatures:

- **HST UV photometry** reveals split MS + RGB consistent with He variations + N-rich populations;
- **chromosome maps** of LMC GCs (Milone et al. 2020, MNRAS 491, 515) show the same 1G + 2G separation as Galactic GCs;
- **Type II GC examples** also exist (e.g., NGC 1851-like with iron variations).

the absence of MPs in young ($< 2$ Gyr) LMC + SMC clusters is interpreted as evidence that MPs require old age + high mass. [eMSTO](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Extended main sequence turn-off eMSTO.md) in young Magellanic clusters is a different phenomenon (likely [rotation-driven](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Stellar rotation effects on CMD.md)).

## the Andromeda (M31) GC system

M31 hosts $\sim 500$ GCs (vs. $\sim 150$ in MW). HST photometry of M31 GCs by Nardiello et al. (2018+) reveals:
- chromosome-map-like splits in $\sim 60\%$ of M31 massive old GCs (consistent with MW Type I/II rates);
- a similar 2G fraction-cluster mass dependence as MW GCs;
- some Type II candidates with iron variations.

so M31 GCs are essentially **identical to MW GCs** in their MP properties.

## the Fornax dwarf galaxy GC system

Fornax has 5 GCs (Fornax 1, 2, 3, 4, 5) within its DM halo. Larsen, Strader, Brodie 2012 reported integrated-light evidence of N-rich (2G) populations in Fornax 3 + 5. recent HST resolved photometry (Larsen et al. 2018) confirmed multiple populations in Fornax GCs.

interesting twist: Fornax is a **dwarf galaxy**, so its GCs formed in a low-mass galactic environment. yet they still show MPs. this argues that the MP-forming mechanism does NOT require a massive host galaxy.

## extragalactic GCs (M87 + giant ellipticals)

M87 hosts $\sim 13{,}000$ GCs, mostly unresolved. integrated-light spectroscopy + photometry by Strader, Brodie, et al. show evidence consistent with N-rich + Na-rich populations in some M87 GCs. resolving them individually requires JWST or future ELTs.

these clusters are **not coeval with M31 + MW GCs**; they may be older + more diverse, providing a wider laboratory for MP testing.

## connection to dense star cluster formation

the universality of MPs in massive old GCs (across MW + M31 + LMC + Fornax + giant ellipticals) implies that the **cluster formation channel** itself produces MPs. proposed mechanisms (see [Polluter scenarios for second-generation GC stars](../../02_Zettel/Theory/Polluter scenarios for second-generation GC stars.md)):

- cluster mass above $\sim 5 \times 10^4\,M_\odot$ retains polluter ejecta against winds;
- formation in dense gas-rich proto-galaxies enables 2G formation from 1G ejecta + retained primordial gas;
- the universality argues for an **internal self-enrichment scenario**, not an external-pollution one.

## what JWST is adding

JWST is now imaging:

- proto-globular cluster candidates at $z \sim 1$-$3$ (Sparkler galaxy, Mowla et al. 2022);
- younger cluster systems in nearby starburst galaxies;
- direct $z > 8$ objects that may evolve into present-day GCs.

these observations test whether MPs form during cluster assembly or develop later. preliminary results: very young clusters do NOT show MPs (consistent with rotation + chemical enrichment requiring time to manifest), but old clusters at $z \sim 1$-$2$ may already show signs.

## why this matters

extragalactic confirmation of MPs:
- cements the result that ALL massive old GCs (universal phenomenon);
- decouples MP formation from MW-specific physics;
- provides a sample size large enough for statistical tests of polluter models;
- closes the loop with high-z galaxy formation.
