title: "Dark matter in dwarf galaxies"

**dwarf galaxies** are the **most dark-matter-dominated** systems known. their high mass-to-light ratios (up to $\sim 1000$) make them clean tests of dark matter on small scales + key probes of $\Lambda$CDM predictions.

## the dwarf-galaxy classes

### dwarf irregulars (dIrr)
gas-rich, star-forming. examples: LMC, SMC. typical $M_*/L \sim 1$ to $5$, total $M/L \sim 10$ to $50$.

### dwarf spheroidals (dSph)
gas-poor, dispersion-supported, in the Local Group. examples: Sculptor, Fornax, Carina, Draco. typical $M_*/L \sim 5$, total $M/L \sim 20$ to $1000$.

### ultra-faint dwarfs (UFD)
very faint ($M_V > -7$), discovered in modern surveys (SDSS, DES, LSST). examples: Bootes I, Segue 1, Hercules. total $M/L \sim 100$ to $10000$.

so dwarfs span a wide range of $M/L$, but all are **dark-matter-dominated** at all radii where stars are detected.

## the kinematic measurement

for dwarf spheroidals:
1. **observe individual stars** spectroscopically (only feasible in Local Group dwarfs).
2. measure each star's **radial velocity**.
3. compute the **velocity dispersion** $\sigma_v$ from the sample.
4. apply the **virial theorem**:
$$M_{\rm dyn} \sim \frac{R\sigma_v^2}{G}$$

with $R$ = half-light radius. gives the dynamical mass within the half-light radius.

typical Local Group dSph: $\sigma_v \sim 5$ to $15$ km/s, $R \sim 100$ pc to $1$ kpc, $M_{\rm dyn} \sim 10^7\,M_\odot$. compare with stellar mass $M_* \sim 10^5\,M_\odot$. **$M_{\rm dyn}/M_* \sim 100$**. enormous dark matter fraction.

## the importance: $\Lambda$CDM tests

dwarfs probe dark matter on **small scales** ($\sim 1$ kpc) where $\Lambda$CDM makes specific predictions:

### the missing-satellites problem

$\Lambda$CDM N-body sims predict $\sim 1000$ DM sub-halos around the MW. only $\sim 60$ luminous dwarfs are observed. resolution: most sub-halos failed to form luminous galaxies (suppressed by reionisation + SN feedback).

### the cusp-core problem

$\Lambda$CDM predicts NFW DM density profiles with $\rho \propto 1/r$ inner cusps. observations of dwarf rotation curves often prefer **cores** ($\rho = $ const inner). resolution: baryonic feedback (SN-driven outflows) erodes cusps to cores; or alternative DM (warm DM, self-interacting DM).

### the too-big-to-fail problem

$\Lambda$CDM predicts $\sim 10$ MW dwarfs more massive than observed. resolution: scatter in baryonic effects, or alternative DM.

so dwarfs are the **front line** of small-scale $\Lambda$CDM tests.

## the ultra-faint dwarfs

UFDs are particularly interesting:
- **fainter than a single bright star**: $M_V > -3$ in some cases.
- **most dark-matter-dominated**: $M/L \sim 1000$ to $10000$.
- **chemically primitive**: $[Fe/H] \sim -2.5$ to $-3$, hint of Pop II/III star formation.
- **plausibly true reionisation fossils**: formed before reionisation + cut off by it.

## modern UFD discoveries

facilitated by:
- **SDSS** (2005-2010): first UFD discoveries (Bootes I, Ursa Major II).
- **DES** (Dark Energy Survey, 2015-onward): southern sky UFDs.
- **LSST / Vera Rubin**: will discover thousands of new UFDs.

## the cosmological connection

dwarf abundance + properties test the **dark-matter particle nature** + the impact of **baryonic feedback**:
- standard $\Lambda$CDM: cold + collisionless DM. predicts NFW profiles + missing satellites.
- WDM (warm dark matter, e.g. sterile neutrinos): suppresses small-scale structure.
- SIDM (self-interacting): gives cores naturally.
- baryonic feedback: alternative explanation for cusps becoming cores.

resolving these is an active research area + a major science goal of the next decade.
