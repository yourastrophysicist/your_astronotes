## the question this course is trying to answer

> **what is the interstellar medium physically, how do its multi-phase components interact dynamically and chemically, and how does this diffuse substrate regulate the lifecycle of stars and galaxies?**

the interstellar medium (ISM) contains everything in a galaxy that is not locked inside stars or compact remnants. although it accounts for only $\sim 10 - 15\%$ of the baryonic mass of a spiral galaxy, the ISM is the thermodynamic engine of galaxy evolution. stars are born from its densest, coldest molecular cores; stars inject kinetic energy, momentum, ionizing radiation, and synthesized metals back into it as they evolve and die; and every astronomical observation of the distant universe must be corrected for its obscuring dust, extinction, and polarization.

prof. carraro's foundational definition:
> "the ISM is a magnetized, turbulent, partially ionized, and dusty fluid."

this course provides the physical, mathematical, and observational toolkit to model this complex non-ideal fluid: from microscopic quantum spin-flips ($21\text{ cm}$) and forbidden line plasma diagnostics ([O III], [S II]) to macroscopic shock waves (Rankine-Hugoniot), supersonic turbulence (Kolmogorov), supernova blast waves (Sedov-Taylor), and cosmic magnetic fields.

## the chain of physical regimes

$$\text{Diffuse HI Gas} \xrightarrow{\text{compression, cooling}} \text{Molecular Clouds } (H_2) \xrightarrow{\text{collapse}} \text{Stars}$$
$$\text{Massive Stars} \xrightarrow{\text{UV photoionization}} \text{HII Regions} \xrightarrow{\text{radiation pressure}} \text{Stellar Winds}$$
$$\text{Supernovae} \xrightarrow{\text{blast waves}} \text{Hot Coronal Gas (HIM)} \xrightarrow{\text{blowout}} \text{Chimneys \& Fountains} \xrightarrow{\text{condensation}} \text{HVCs}$$

## key oral exam questions & derivations

frequently asked questions in Padova oral examinations for this course:

1. **Strömgren radius derivation**:
   - state the assumptions (uniform density, steady state, Case B recombination, sharp ionization front).
   - balance photon arrival rate $Q(H^0) = \int_{\nu_0}^\infty \frac{L_\nu}{h\nu}d\nu$ against recombinations in sphere $\frac{4}{3}\pi R_S^3 n_H^2 \alpha_B$.
   - solve for $R_S = [3 Q(H^0) / 4\pi n_H^2 \alpha_B]^{1/3}$. explain why $R_S$ depends more strongly on density than stellar luminosity.
2. **Plasma diagnostics ($T_e$ and $n_e$)**:
   - sketch the energy level diagrams for $p^2$ ions ([O III], [N II]) and $p^3$ ions ([O II], [S II]).
   - explain why [O III] $\frac{\lambda 4363}{\lambda 4959 + \lambda 5007}$ measures $T_e$ (large energy separation, exponential Boltzmann tail).
   - explain why [S II] $\frac{\lambda 6717}{\lambda 6731}$ measures $n_e$ (identical excitation energies, differing transition probabilities, transition between low-density and high-density limits).
3. **21 cm column density formula**:
   - derive $N_{\text{HI}} = 1.823 \times 10^{18} \int T_B(v) dv$ in the optically thin limit.
   - define spin temperature $T_s$ and explain why it cancels out in the optically thin integral.
4. **Sedov-Taylor blast wave**:
   - perform the dimensional analysis showing $R_s(t) \propto (E_0/\rho_0)^{1/5} t^{2/5}$.
   - show that shock velocity decelerates as $v_s \propto t^{-3/5}$ and post-shock temperature drops as $T_s \propto t^{-6/5}$.
   - explain why the remnant transitions to the snowplow phase ($R_s \propto t^{1/4}$) once radiative cooling peaks at $T \sim 10^5 - 10^6\text{ K}$.
5. **Cardelli, Clayton, & Mathis (CCM 1989) extinction law**:
   - define $A_\lambda, E(B-V)$, and $R_V$.
   - explain the physical significance of $R_V$ (grain size distribution).
   - describe the physical origin of the $2175\text{ \AA}$ bump (plasmon resonance in graphite/PAHs) and the far-UV rise.
6. **Interstellar magnetic fields**:
   - describe the Davis-Greenstein mechanism and why optical starlight polarization is parallel to $\mathbf{B}_\perp$ while sub-mm dust thermal emission is perpendicular to $\mathbf{B}_\perp$.
   - derive $\langle B_\parallel \rangle = 1.232 \frac{\text{RM}}{\text{DM}}$ from the ratio of Faraday Rotation Measure to pulsar Dispersion Measure.
7. **Rankine-Hugoniot jump conditions**:
   - write the mass, momentum, and energy flux conservation equations.
   - derive the maximum compression ratio $\rho_2/\rho_1 = (\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1) = 4$ for an adiabatic monatomic gas ($\gamma=5/3$).
   - show that for an isothermal shock ($T_2 = T_1$), compression reaches $\rho_2/\rho_1 = \mathcal{M}^2$.
8. **MHD wave modes**:
   - distinguish between Alfvén waves (transverse shear, incompressional $\delta\rho = 0$, restoring force magnetic tension, $v_A = B_0/\sqrt{4\pi\rho}$) and fast magnetosonic waves (compressional $\delta\rho \ne 0$, restoring forces gas + magnetic pressure, $v_{\text{ms}} = \sqrt{c_s^2 + v_A^2}$).

## Essential Formulas Quick Sheet (Interstellar Medium)

- **Strömgren Sphere**: $R_S = \left(\frac{3 Q(H^0)}{4\pi n_H^2 \alpha_B}\right)^{1/3}$ with $\alpha_B \approx 2.6 \times 10^{-13}\text{ cm}^3\text{s}^{-1}$ at $10^4\text{ K}$
- **21cm Optical Depth**: $\tau(v) \approx \frac{3 c^2 h A_{10}}{32\pi k_B T_s \nu_{10}} \frac{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}{\Delta v}$
- **21cm Column Density (Thin)**: $N_{\mathrm{HI}} = 1.823 \times 10^{18} \int T_B(v) dv\ [\mathrm{cm^{-2}}]$
- **Total-to-Selective Extinction**: $R_V = \frac{A_V}{E(B-V)} \approx 3.1$ (diffuse ISM)
- **Sedov-Taylor Blast Wave**: $R(t) = 1.15 \left(\frac{E_{\mathrm{SN}}}{\rho_0}\right)^{1/5} t^{2/5}$
- **Alfvén Speed**: $v_A = \frac{B}{\sqrt{4\pi \rho}} \approx 2.18 \left(\frac{B}{\mu\mathrm{G}}\right) \left(\frac{n_{\mathrm{H}}}{\mathrm{cm}^{-3}}\right)^{-1/2}\text{ km/s}$
- **Isothermal Shock Compression**: $\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = M_1^2$
