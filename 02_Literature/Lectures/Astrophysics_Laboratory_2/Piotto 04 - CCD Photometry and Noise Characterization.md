## Quantum Efficiency

Quantum Efficiency ($QE$) is the probability that an incident photon of wavelength $\lambda$ generates an electron collected in the potential well:
$$QE(\lambda) = \frac{N_{e^-}(\lambda)}{N_{\text{photons}}(\lambda)} = [1 - R(\lambda)] [1 - e^{-\alpha(\lambda) d}] \eta_{\text{coll}}$$
- $R(\lambda)$: surface reflection coefficient at the silicon interface (reduced by anti-reflective coatings such as $SiO_2, Si_3N_4$).
- $\alpha(\lambda)$: silicon absorption coefficient.
- $d$: active depletion layer thickness.
- $\eta_{\text{coll}}$: charge collection efficiency.

### Front-Illuminated vs Back-Illuminated CCDs
- **Front-Illuminated**: photons must pass through polysilicon gate electrodes before reaching depletion layer; gates absorb UV and blue photons ($QE < 20\%$ below 400 nm).
- **Back-Illuminated (Back-Thinned)**: chip is etched to $\sim 15?20\, \mu\text{m}$ thickness and illuminated from the rear, exposing raw silicon directly to photons ($QE > 90\%$ across 450?700 nm).
- **Near-IR Fringing**: at $\lambda > 800\text{ nm}$, silicon becomes weakly absorbing; photons reflect between front and back surfaces, creating interference fringes that must be removed via flat fielding.

## Detector Noise Sources

An astronomical CCD image is corrupted by four distinct physical noise components:

### 1. Readout Noise (RON)
Generated by electronic noise in the on-chip output FET transistor and off-chip analog circuitry during charge-to-voltage conversion.
- Independent of exposure time and incident photon flux.
- Modeled as zero-mean Gaussian white noise with standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{RON}}$ (electrons per pixel).
- Mitigated by Correlated Double Sampling (CDS), subtracting baseline voltage before charge deposit:
$$\sigma_{\text{RON}} \sim 2 - 10\, e^- \text{ rms}$$

### 2. Dark Current
Thermal excitation of electrons across the silicon band gap into the potential well in the absence of light:
$$N_{\text{dark}} = D \cdot t_{\text{exp}}$$
Dark generation rate $D$ depends exponentially on operating temperature via the Arrhenius equation:
$$D(T) = C \, T^{3/2} \exp\left( -\frac{E_g}{2 k_B T} \right)$$
- Cooling to cryogenic temperatures ($T \sim 150?170\text{ K}$ via liquid nitrogen) or multi-stage thermoelectric Peltier coolers ($T \sim -40^\circ\text{C}$ to $-80^\circ\text{C}$) reduces dark current to negligible levels ($D < 10^{-3} e^-/\text{pixel}/\text{s}$).
- Dark current follows Poisson statistics:
$$\sigma_{\text{dark}} = \sqrt{N_{\text{dark}}} = \sqrt{D \cdot t_{\text{exp}}}$$

### 3. Photon Noise (Shot Noise)
Fundamental quantum noise originating from the discrete, independent arrival of photons:
- Governed by Poisson statistics where variance equals the mean:
$$\sigma_{\text{phot}} = \sqrt{N_{e^-, \text{source}}}$$

### 4. Pixel Response Non-Uniformity (PRNU)
Fixed-pattern sensitivity variation across the pixel array due to lithographic variations in pixel area and local doping gradients (typically 1?3% rms). Corrected by dividing by a normalized flat field frame.

## The Analog-to-Digital Converter and Gain

The output amplifier voltage is digitized by an Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) into discrete Analog-to-Digital Units (ADU):
$$\text{Signal [ADU]} = \frac{\text{Signal } [e^-]}{g}$$
where $g$ is the **ADC gain** (conversion factor in $e^-/\text{ADU}$).

ADC bit depth determines dynamic range:
$$\text{Max ADU} = 2^N - 1 \implies 65,535 \text{ ADU for a 16-bit converter}$$

## The Complete CCD Signal-to-Noise Ratio Equation

For a star with total photo-electrons $S_\star$ measured over a synthetic aperture of $N_{\text{pix}}$ pixels, with sky background $B$ ($e^-/\text{pixel}$), dark current $D$ ($e^-/\text{pixel}/\text{s}$), readout noise $\sigma_{\text{RON}}$ ($e^-$), and exposure time $t_{\text{exp}}$ (Howell 2000):

$$\text{SNR} = \frac{S_\star}{\sqrt{S_\star + N_{\text{pix}} \left( B + D \, t_{\text{exp}} + \sigma_{\text{RON}}^2 + g^2 \sigma_{\text{digitization}}^2 \right) + \frac{N_{\text{pix}}^2}{N_{\text{sky}}} B}}$$

### Operating Regimes:
- **Source-Dominated (Bright Stars)**: $S_\star \gg N_{\text{pix}} (B + \sigma_{\text{RON}}^2) \implies \text{SNR} \approx \sqrt{S_\star}$ (Poisson limit).
- **Background-Dominated (Faint Stars / Ground)**: $N_{\text{pix}} B \gg S_\star \implies \text{SNR} \propto S_\star / \sqrt{N_{\text{pix}} B}$.
- **Readout-Noise-Dominated (Short Exposures / Space)**: $\sigma_{\text{RON}}^2 \gg B, S_\star \implies \text{SNR} \approx S_\star / (\sqrt{N_{\text{pix}}} \sigma_{\text{RON}})$.

## Aperture Photometry vs PSF Fitting Photometry

- **Aperture Photometry**:
	- Sums all counts within a fixed circular radius $R_{\text{ap}}$ and subtracts local sky background.
	- Simple, computationally fast, model-independent.
	- Optimal in uncrowded stellar fields with high SNR.
- **Point Spread Function (PSF) Fitting**:
	- Fits an analytical 2D profile (Moffat, Gaussian, empirical lookup table) to each star.
	- Essential in crowded fields (e.g., globular cluster cores) where stellar profiles overlap.
	- Optimal weighting gives lower photometric error for faint stars.
