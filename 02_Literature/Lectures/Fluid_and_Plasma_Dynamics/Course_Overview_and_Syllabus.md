## 1. Course Identity and Scope

Astrophysical and laboratory plasmas make up more than 99 percent of the visible matter in the universe. Understanding phenomena across cosmic scales (such as stellar interiors, solar wind, interstellar medium, accretion disks, relativistic jets, and galaxy clusters) and laboratory scales (such as magnetic confinement fusion devices including tokamaks, stellarators, and reversed-field pinches) requires an integrated framework connecting kinetic theory, fluid dynamics, and electromagnetism.

This course builds the physical and mathematical bridge from microscopic particle dynamics to macroscopic fluid and magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) descriptions. It investigates how collective behavior emerges from long-range Coulomb interactions, analyzes wave propagation and instabilities that drive transport, and explores nonlinear turbulent cascades and magnetic field generation.

The curriculum develops along five core pillars:
1. **Kinetic Foundations and Neutral Fluid Dynamics**: From Liouville's theorem and the Boltzmann transport equation to macroscopic conservation laws, Navier-Stokes viscosity, boundary layers, and incompressible flow.
2. **Waves, Hydrodynamic Instabilities, and Turbulence**: Acoustic waves, Jeans gravitational collapse, Rayleigh-Taylor and Kelvin-Helmholtz interfacial instabilities, Rayleigh-Bénard thermal convection, and Kolmogorov's theory of developed turbulence.
3. **Single-Particle Motion, Confinement, and Plasma Kinetics**: Cyclotron gyration, guiding-center drifts ($E \times B$, gradient, curvature, polarization), magnetic mirror reflection, trapped particles in toroidal geometry (tokamak banana orbits), gyrokinetic theory (6D to 5D reduction), and collisionless Landau damping.
4. **Two-Fluid Theory, Collisional Transport, and Microinstabilities**: The Braginskii transport equations, Spitzer resistivity, ambipolar diffusion in magnetized weakly ionized media, drift-reduced two-fluid equations, resistive ballooning modes (RBM), and the slab ion temperature gradient (ITG) instability.
5. **Magnetohydrodynamics, Reconnection, and Dynamo Theory**: Single-fluid ideal and resistive MHD, Alfvén's theorem of flux freezing, magnetostatic equilibria (Z-pinch, Bennett relation, Grad-Shafranov equation for axisymmetric tokamaks), shear Alfvén and magnetosonic waves, Sweet-Parker magnetic reconnection, tearing instabilities, Woltjer's theorems, Taylor plasma relaxation in Reversed Field Pinches (RFP), Cowling's anti-dynamo theorem, and turbulent mean-field dynamos ($\alpha\Omega$ mechanism).

## 3. Reference Literature and Textbooks

The course material combines graduate textbooks, foundational review papers, and Prof. Giacomin's lecture manuscripts:
- **Primary Textbook**: Arnab Rai Choudhuri, *The Physics of Fluids and Plasmas: An Introduction for Astrophysicists and Physicists*, Cambridge University Press, 1998.
- **Statistical Mechanics and Kinetic Theory**: Kerson Huang, *Statistical Mechanics* (Chapter 5: The Boltzmann Transport Equation), John Wiley & Sons.
- **Collisional Transport**: Stanislav I. Braginskii, *Transport Processes in a Plasma*, Reviews of Plasma Physics, Vol. 1, Consultants Bureau, 1965.
- **Magnetic Confinement and Tokamaks**: John Wesson, *Tokamaks*, 3rd/4th Edition, Oxford University Press.
- **Gyrokinetic Theory**: Steve Cowley, *Lectures on Gyro-Kinetics: From ITER Time-scales to Gyro-Kinetics*, Culham Science Centre notes.
- **Magnetic Reconnection and Stability**: Dieter Biskamp, *Magnetic Reconnection in Plasmas*, Cambridge University Press, 2000.
- **Turbulence**: Roberto Benzi and Uriel Frisch, *Lectures on Turbulence*, Physics Reports 1021 (2023) 1-106.
- **Plasma Waves and Kinetic Instabilities**: Nicholas A. Krall and Alvin W. Trivelpiece, *Principles of Plasma Physics*, McGraw-Hill, 1973.
- **Two-Fluid Edge Turbulence**: Andreas Zeiler, *Tokamak Edge Turbulence and Two-Fluid Instabilities*, Max-Planck-Institut für Plasmaphysik.

## 4. Examination Format and Evaluation

The final evaluation consists of a comprehensive **oral examination**:
- The student is asked **three questions** selected from the official course question pool (containing 30 questions).
- **One question is chosen by the student**, allowing in-depth presentation of a favored topic.
- **Two questions are chosen by the examiner** (Prof. Maurizio Giacomin).
- Assessment criteria:
  1. Ability to perform mathematical derivations from first principles at the blackboard without skipping intermediate physical steps.
  2. Clear articulation of underlying physical approximations, validity limits, and characteristic dimensionless numbers (Reynolds number $Re$, magnetic Reynolds number $R_m$, Knudsen number $Kn$, plasma parameter $\Lambda$, Lundquist number $S$).
  3. Understanding of experimental and astrophysical manifestations (solar wind, tokamak core turbulence, RFX-mod reversed-field pinch, coronal heating, accretion discs).

The complete preparation guide for all 30 questions is documented in [[Giacomin_Oral_Exam_Questions_Complete_Guide]].

## 5. Research and Thesis Opportunities at Consorzio RFX

Consorzio RFX (located in Corso Stati Uniti 4, Padova, within the CNR research area) hosts world-leading fusion experiments and research facilities:
- **RFX-mod2**: The world's largest Reversed Field Pinch experiment ($R = 2.0\text{ m}, a = 0.46\text{ m}$), operating with plasma currents up to $2\text{ MA}$, transitioning to quasi-single helicity (QSH) states.
- **Neutral Beam Test Facility (NBTF)**: Hosting the SPIDER and MITICA negative-ion source prototypes for the ITER heating neutral beams.
- **Theoretical and Computational Projects**:
  - Boundary plasma turbulence simulations in RFP and tokamak configurations.
  - Development of linear and quasi-linear instability solvers based on drift-reduced Braginskii equations (supervisor: Prof. Maurizio Giacomin, `maurizio.giacomin@unipd.it`).
  - Analysis of magnetic chaos, stochastic magnetic transport, and helical self-organization.

## 6. Modular Note System Map

The course notes in this vault are organized into eight thematic modules:
- [[Part1_Kinetic_Foundations_and_Fluid_Equations]] - Liouville theorem, Boltzmann collision operator, moments, Chapman-Enskog closure, macroscopic momentum equation.
- [[Part2_Incompressible_and_Viscous_Flows]] - Vorticity dynamics, Kelvin circulation theorem, solar corona equilibrium, Navier-Stokes viscosity, Hagen-Poiseuille pipe flow, Prandtl boundary layer.
- [[Part3_Waves_Instabilities_and_Turbulence]] - Sound waves, Jeans instability, Rayleigh-Taylor, Kelvin-Helmholtz, Rayleigh-Bénard convection, Kolmogorov K41 turbulence, Reynolds stresses.
- [[Part4_Single_Particle_Motion_and_Magnetic_Confinement]] - Guiding-center drifts, magnetic mirror force, first adiabatic invariant, loss cone, tokamak magnetic geometry, trapped particles, banana orbits.
- [[Part5_Plasma_Kinetics_Gyrokinetics_and_Waves]] - Gyrokinetic ordering and 5D reduction, gyro-averaging, Landau damping complex contour integration, wave propagation in cold magnetized plasmas.
- [[Part6_Two_Fluid_Theory_Transport_and_Microinstabilities]] - Braginskii equations, Spitzer resistivity, drift-reduced equations, resistive ballooning mode, slab ITG instability, ambipolar diffusion.
- [[Part7_Magnetohydrodynamics_Equilibrium_and_Waves]] - Ideal and resistive MHD equations, Alfvén flux freezing, Z-pinch Bennett relation, Grad-Shafranov tokamak equilibrium, shear and compressional Alfvén waves.
- [[Part8_Reconnection_Relaxation_and_Dynamo_Theory]] - Sweet-Parker reconnection, resistive tearing modes, Woltjer theorems, Taylor relaxation, Reversed Field Pinch (RFP), Cowling anti-dynamo theorem, mean-field $\alpha\Omega$ dynamo.
- [[Giacomin_Oral_Exam_Questions_Complete_Guide]] - Blackboard derivations and answers for the 30 oral examination questions.
