# About Your AstroNotes

Welcome to the public archive of Year 1 Master of Science notes in Astrophysics and Cosmology from the University of Padua (Università degli Studi di Padova), Italy.

This project transforms a private working Obsidian second brain into a free, open-access digital garden for physics students, researchers, and self-directed learners worldwide.

## Academic Scope

The notes synthesize the complete first-year graduate curriculum across ten primary lecture courses:

1. **Fundamentals of Astrophysics and Cosmology**
   Spherical astronomy, coordinate systems, radiation processes, stellar magnitudes, galactic components, Hubble expansion, and cosmic thermal history.

2. **General Relativity**
   Differential geometry, curved spacetime, tensors, Christoffel symbols, Riemann curvature, Einstein field equations, Schwarzschild metric, and gravitational wave propagation.

3. **Stellar Astrophysics**
   Stellar structure differential equations, equation of state, nuclear fusion cycles (pp chains, CNO, triple-alpha), radiative atmospheres, opacity, and stellar population tracks.

4. **Astrophysics of Galaxies**
   Photometric profiles (Sérsic, de Vaucouleurs), stellar dynamics, collisionless Boltzmann equation, Jeans equations, dark matter haloes, and scaling relations.

5. **Observational Cosmology**
   FLRW metric dynamics, Friedmann acceleration equations, distance measures, cosmic microwave background physics, big bang nucleosynthesis, and dark energy phenomenology.

6. **Observational Astrophysics**
   Atmospheric physics, airmass and extinction, CCD and IR detector architectures, read noise, Poisson statistics, exposure time calculations, and error budgets.

7. **Astronomical Interferometry**
   Spatial coherence theory, van Cittert-Zernike theorem, Fourier synthesis imaging, dirty beams, deconvolution algorithms (CLEAN), ALMA, and the VLTI.

8. **Astronomical Spectroscopy**
   Quantum atomic transitions, selection rules, thermal and pressure broadening, optical grating physics, spectrograph designs, slit geometry, and radial velocity measurements.

9. **Laboratory of High-Energy Astrophysics**
   Non-thermal radiation mechanisms (synchrotron, inverse Compton, relativistic bremsstrahlung), grazing-incidence X-ray optics, scintillation detectors, and space missions.

10. **Mathematical and Numerical Methods for Physics**
    Matrix decomposition, root-finding, numerical quadrature, ordinary differential equation solvers (Runge-Kutta, symplectic integrators), and Monte Carlo techniques.

## Architecture and Methodology

The archive is organized according to an atomic Zettelkasten model:

* **00_Atlas**: Ten comprehensive Maps of Content providing curriculum navigation and structural syllabi.
* **01_Literature**: Detailed reading summaries and mathematical breakdowns of graduate reference texts.
* **02_Zettel**: Over one thousand modular concept notes. Each note covers an explicit physical principle or derivation with bilateral links.
* **04_Images**: Astronomical diagrams, instrument schematics, ray diagrams, and observational plots.

All mathematical equations are formatted using standard LaTeX syntax and rendered natively via KaTeX.
